# Qualifications

_Figures in this file: 2025–2026. The year files in `figures/` win if they differ._

## Commerce

**No qualification** is required for commercial activities that are not otherwise regulated. [C · S1] Special sectors with their own requirements: special-sectors.md.

## Crafts (lists A, B, C)

Crafts are split into **list A** (main trades) and **lists B and C** (secondary trades), set out in annexes 1 to 3 of the law since 2023. [C · S1]

| List | Examples | Qualification | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | Baker, hairdresser, electrician, roofer, car mechanic | Master craftsman certificate (*brevet de maîtrise*) covering the trade | [C · S1] |
| B | Secondary trades | Vocational diploma (DAP) covering the trade | [C · S1] |
| C | Tattooist, pet groomer, taxi and car-rental operator, domestic help, photographer-camera operator, designer | **None** (created in 2023) | [C · S1; S3] |

Equivalences (grand-ducal regulation of 1 December 2011) [C · S16]:
- List A: a bachelor covering the trade counts fully; one covering it partly needs 1 year of practice; one not covering it, 2 years; or a DAP plus **6 years** in a management function in the trade after the DAP.
- List B: **3 years** of full-time experience in the trade plus business-management knowledge.
- A craftsman working only at fairs and markets applies for a **commercial** permit, not a craft permit. [C · S11]

## Liberal professions under the permit law

- Still under the 2011 law: architect, interior architect, landscape architect, accountant (*comptable*), industrial-property adviser, chartered accountant (*expert-comptable*), surveyor, construction engineer (*ingénieur-conseil*) and town planner. [I · S7]
- Architect: a degree after at least five years of full-time study (or four years plus two years of supervised internship), or a title recognised under the law of 28 October 2016. [C · S1]
- Expert-comptable: a bachelor in economics, finance, management or business law, three years of practice (one with an expert-comptable) and an aptitude test; also registration with the Ordre des Experts-Comptables (regulated-professions.md). [C · S1]
- The « ingénieur indépendant » profession is no longer in the law: the law of 26 July 2023 deleted its definition (art. 2, point 26°) and repealed art. 20; the same law removed the « ingénieur-paysagiste » (art. 2 2° and art. 12: struck from the definition in art. 2 point 5° and from art. 18) – the « architecte-paysagiste » remains – the Legilux consolidation still prints the text, hidden only on screen. Guichet's 2021 page and the MESR's 2026 page still name it – do not rely on them. [C · S42 arts 2, 12, 13]

## Foreign qualifications

- The Minister may require foreign diplomas to be entered in the register of training titles first. [C · S1]
- Experience gained in another EU country is proved by an **EC certificate** (attestation CE) under Directive 2005/36/EC from the country of origin. [C · S6]
- Liberal professions: if the Ministry of the Economy finds a foreign training does not meet the criteria, an ad hoc commission reviews it and the **MESR** (higher-education ministry) decides on professional recognition. [C · S29] Fee: figures.

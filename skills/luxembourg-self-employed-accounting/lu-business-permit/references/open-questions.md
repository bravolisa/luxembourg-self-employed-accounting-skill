# Open points – lu-business-permit

Answer an open point by saying it is not settled, giving the working answer with its label, and naming who can settle it.

## BP-O-01 · Translators, interpreters and experts
- Question: Do translators, interpreters, experts and similar unlisted intellectual providers working in their own name need a commercial permit?
- Why it matters: Guichet's 2025 page says unlisted intellectual providers need a commercial permit; its 2021 page says translators and experts need none. BravoLisa's profession taxonomy follows the 2021 page.
- Working answer: The law requires a permit only for commerce, crafts and the liberal professions it names (art. 1), and defines a liberal profession as mainly intellectual services outside commerce and crafts (art. 2, 28°). In that reading a translator working on their own needs no permit; Guichet's 2025 page and the 2018 bill commentary (« conseil » covered by the commercial permit) point the other way for consulting. Flag translators as uncertain. [I · S1, S2, S34]
- Who can settle: Ministry of the Economy (DG Classes moyennes, info.pme@eco.etat.lu).
- Status: open

## BP-O-02 · Legal basis of the €50 fee
- Question: Which grand-ducal regulation fixes the €50 permit fee (the law sets a range of €24 to €2,500)?
- Why it matters: Citation; a 2013 draft proposed €24 → €50.
- Working answer: €50, per Guichet and the official form (a « Droit de Chancellerie » stamp). [C · S2, S4] The only fee regulations found are of 1962 and 1985 (1,000 francs, about €24), made under a law since repealed; a 2013 draft for €50 was not found adopted. [O · S44]
- Who can settle: Ministry of the Economy; a full-text search of Legilux.
- Status: open

## BP-O-03 · €12 registration duty on a sole trader's RCS registration
- Question: Does the €12 registration duty the LBR collects for the State apply to a sole trader's registration request?
- Why it matters: First-day cost (€17.09 or €29.09 incl. VAT).
- Working answer: Probably no €12: the fixed registration duty is charged per private deed (« acte sous signature privée ») filed with the RCS, and a sole trader's registration request is not a deed. The cost is the LBR fee unless a deed is attached. [I · S20; RCS law art. 22-3]
- Who can settle: LBR helpdesk.
- Status: open

## BP-O-04 · Current Chambre des Métiers amounts
- Question: What is the current indexed euro amount of the Chambre des Métiers part B contribution (and part A's €75 minimum, if indexed)?
- Why it matters: Yearly cost of a craft business.
- Working answer: Base amounts at the 2020 index (834.76), adjusted with the civil-service index; the regulation ties the indexation to the part-B scale, so the €75 part-A minimum may not be indexed at all. Current amounts unknown. [C · S25 for the base; I for the indexation]
- Who can settle: Chambre des Métiers.
- Status: open

## BP-O-06 · RCS number on a sole trader's invoices
- Question: Does any rule require the RCS number on a commercial sole trader's invoices (the permit number is required)?
- Why it matters: Invoice content (lu-vat invoicing.md).
- Working answer: Show the permit number – required for every permit holder (art. 34 of the 2011 law). No rule requiring a sole trader to show the RCS number was found in the RCS law or its regulation of 23 January 2003; company law requires it form by form (SARL art. 710-10, SA art. 462-1). Showing it is good practice. [I · S18, S41, S40]
- Who can settle: LBR.
- Status: open

## BP-O-07 · Filing clients' VAT returns and the accountant's permit
- Question: Does an intermediary who prepares and transmits clients' VAT returns need the « comptable » permit, and does that permit cover it? Which size limits apply to a comptable's clients?
- Why it matters: BravoLisa and partners filing for users.
- Working answer: Preparing tax returns is not reserved to experts-comptables: the 1999 law lists « établir les déclarations fiscales » among activities compatible with the profession and reserves only organising and checking accounts and drawing up balance sheets (art. 1). A business that only prepares and transmits VAT returns therefore seems to need the general commercial permit, not the « comptable » permit. [I · S43] The 1999 text was read as originally published; later amendments were not checked. [O]
- Who can settle: Ministry of the Economy; AED.
- Status: open

## BP-O-08 · Graphic and UX designers
- Question: Does a graphic, web or UX designer working in their own name need a craft permit (list C) or the general commercial permit?
- Why it matters: Many freelancers; the wrong permit risks refusal.
- Working answer: List C « designer » is product design and « atelier graphique » covers making graphics and applying films; pure screen design is neither by its wording, which points to the general commercial permit. [I · S1] Caution: list B « Imprimeur » (a qualified trade) includes in its pre-press part « Conception graphique … pour tout support ou matériel » – probably aimed at print, but not limited by its words. [C · S1 annex 2; I]
- Who can settle: Ministry of the Economy.
- Competing reading (2026-10-31): the maintainer of our articles reads list C « Exploitant d'un atelier graphique » – « Projection et confection de graphiques de tout genre » – as covering graphic design in general, screens included; « de tout genre » is not limited to print. Plausible, not settled. [I · S1 annex 3]
- Status: open

## BP-O-09 · Body piercers
- Question: Which business permit does a self-employed body piercer (no tattooing) need: list C « tatoueur », another craft, or the general commercial permit?
- Why it matters: The wrong permit risks refusal; piercing studios are common side businesses.
- Working answer: List C « tatoueur » covers tattoos and permanent make-up only, and no craft list names piercing [C · S1 annexes 1–3]; by the wording that points to the general commercial permit [I]. Either way, no diploma is needed for the permit; the 21-hour hygiene training and the health declaration apply in any case (special-sectors.md) [C · S48 art. 3].
- Who can settle: Ministry of the Economy.
- Status: open

## BP-O-10 · EU Blue Card holder as the manager who holds the business permit
- Question: The law lets an EU Blue Card holder carry on a « subsidiary » self-employed activity alongside the main job (immigration law, art. 45-2(3)). Does being the manager on whom his or her own SARL's business permit rests fit that word?
- Why it matters: Founders with a Blue Card ask whether they may manage the company they set up; a wrong answer can cost the residence permit.
- Working answer: Owning shares is not an activity and is not restricted. [I · S51 art. 3] A corporate officer who is a beneficial owner and holds the permit is treated as self-employed (art. 51(2)), so the role falls under art. 45-2(3) and must stay subsidiary. [C · S51] The law does not define the word; the permit law asks the dirigeant for actual, permanent day-to-day management through physical presence, which is hard to call subsidiary. [I · S1] Advise that another founder holds the permit, or that the user asks the immigration directorate first. [I]
- Who can settle: Direction générale de l'immigration (Ministry of Home Affairs).
- Status: open

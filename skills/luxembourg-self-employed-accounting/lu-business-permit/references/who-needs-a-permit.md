# Who needs a business permit

_Figures in this file: 2025–2026. The year files in `figures/` win if they differ._

- Nobody may, **for profit and on a habitual basis**, run an independent commercial, craft, industrial or listed liberal-profession activity – as a **main or a side activity** – without a business permit (*autorisation d'établissement* / *Niederlassungsgenehmigung*) (art. 1 of the law of 2 September 2011). [C · S1]
- There is **no minimum turnover** below which a habitual side activity is exempt; the only euro threshold in the law (€35,000) is for school entrepreneurship projects. The CCSS exemption below a third of the minimum wage is a social-security rule, not a permit rule. [I · S1]
- The permit is issued to the **business**: to the sole trader personally, or to the company the dirigeant manages. [C · S8]

## Who needs one – typical cases

| Situation | Permit? | Status |
|---|---|---|
| IT, marketing or management consultant, other intellectual services not listed as a liberal profession (a product designer is craft list C – qualifications.md; graphic and UX designers: BP-O-08) | **Yes – general commercial permit** (« activités et services commerciaux »), no diploma needed | [C · S2] |
| Old « conseil » or « conseil économique » permits | No longer issued since 2018; consultants get the commercial permit; old permits stay valid as commercial permits | [C · S34] |
| Translators, interpreters, experts working in their own name | **Unclear**: Guichet's 2025 page says unlisted intellectual providers need a commercial permit; an older page (2021) says these need none | [O · BP-O-01] |
| Listed liberal professions (architects, accountants, engineers…) | Yes – qualifications.md | [C · S1] |
| Lawyers, doctors, dentists, vets, réviseurs | No business permit – their own laws apply (regulated-professions.md) | [C · S2] |
| Journalism, writing books (other than self-publishing) | No (art. 8(3) 1°) | [C · S1] |
| Selling one's own handmade products, recovered items or things not bought for resale | No – not treated as a professional | [C · S2] |
| The same hobby sellers at fairs and markets or on their own dedicated website | **Yes – commercial permit** | [C · S2] |
| Short-term rentals of furnished units (90 nights a year or more) | Yes – accommodation (special-sectors.md) | [C · S1] |
| A business from another EU/EEA country or Switzerland providing occasional, temporary services | No permit; craft and industrial businesses notify beforehand | [C · S2] |

- **Do not mix the permit category with the tax category.** The permit law decides **who may** run an activity; the income-tax law (LIR arts 14, 91) decides **how its profit is taxed**, with its own definitions. « Commercial » in the permit (« activités et services commerciaux ») is an access category, not a tax category, and the activity carried on stays within it. No sentence of the law states the independence; it follows from the two laws' separate definitions. [I · S1, S34] The two do diverge in both directions:
  - **journalists** need no permit (art. 8(3) 1°) but are named as a liberal profession for income tax (LIR art. 91); [C · S1]
  - a **pharmacist** works under a health-law authorisation, yet the Cour administrative held the profit commercial (22 July 2026, n° 54190C – lu-income-tax-business);
  - an **IT developer or consultant** holds the commercial permit, while the income may still count as liberal-profession profit (lu-income-tax-business IT-O-21). [I · S34]
- Working without a permit is a criminal offence (procedure-and-fees.md).

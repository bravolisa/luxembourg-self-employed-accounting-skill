# Luxembourg business permit

_Figures in this file: 2025–2026. The year files in `references/figures/` win if they differ._

Follow lu-tax-core for triage, labels and answer shape. **[C]** Confirmed, **[I]** Interpretation, **[P]** Practice, **[O]** Open, **[B]** BravoLisa convention; source ids in [references/sources.md](references/sources.md).

## Before answering

1. **What activity**, exactly – and is it habitual and for profit? Main or side activity?
2. Sole trader or company (then the dirigeant holds the conditions)?
3. **Diplomas and experience**, and from which country.
4. **Premises**: shop, office, workshop, coworking, domiciliation or home.
5. Year: [2025](references/figures/2025.md), [2026](references/figures/2026.md); the reform in [2027 and later](references/figures/2027.md) is a bill.

## Rules people get wrong most often

- "Freelancers don't need a permit" is wrong for most: IT, marketing and management consultants need the **general commercial permit** – easy, no diploma. [C · S2]
- There is **no minimum turnover**: a habitual side activity needs a permit too. [C · S1; I · S1]
- A **domiciliation address** is not a fixed place of business. [C · S1]
- The « supplementary » declaration of honour is only for owners or managers **other than** the manager applying; the applicant's own declaration is inside the application form. [C · S45, S4]
- Tattoo and piercing need a **21-hour hygiene training** and a declaration to the Ministry of Health, for every practitioner, employees included; health or nursing studies do not replace the training by the text. [C · S48 art. 3; I · S49]
- The permit comes **before** the activity; working without one is a criminal offence. [C · S1]
- An **EU Blue Card** holder may own shares, and the law allows a **subsidiary** self-employed activity beside the job. Whether being the manager who holds the permit still counts as subsidiary is not settled – do not promise it. [C · S51 art. 45-2(3); O · BP-O-10]
- The « conseil » permit and « ingénieur indépendant » no longer exist, even if old articles still cite them. [C · S34; O · S1]
- The fee is **€50**; the decision is due within 3 months, but in 2025 it took 6.62 days on average; silence counts as approval. [C · S2, S1, S31]
- Liberal professionals working in their own name do not register with the RCS; sole traders never file in the RBE. [I · S18; C · S21]
- Short-term rentals of 90 nights a year or more need an accommodation permit. [C · S1]
- The permit number must appear on invoices, quotes, websites and e-mails. [C · S1]

# Company managers (gérants) and partners

What the law and the CCSS's instructions say.

- **Who has the self-employed status in a company**: the CCSS's instructions to the self-employed name « associés détenant plus de 25% des parts sociales » and « membres du conseil d'administration figurant comme gérants sur l'autorisation d'établissement » – partners holding **more than 25% of the shares**, and board members named as **managers on the business permit**. [C · S24]
- **Which form**: for simplicity, a company declares all its people – including those who have the self-employed status – with the **employee** entry form (*déclaration d'entrée pour salarié du secteur privé*); the self-employed entry form is only for a business run in one's own name. [C · S24]
- After the business permit is granted, the dirigeant of a SARL, SARL-S or SA declares his or her entry with social security as employee or as self-employed. [C · lu-entity-profiles S16]
- A **helping spouse or partner** of someone who works through a company is affiliated as an **employee**, not as a helping spouse. [C · S24]
- **The law** (social-security code, art. 1, point 4): partners of a SARL (or SNC, SCS) who hold **more than 25% of the shares**, and directors of an SA who are delegated to day-to-day management, count as self-employed – « à condition qu'il s'agisse de personnes sur lesquelles repose l'autorisation d'établissement ». Both conditions must be met; a manager who fails either is not assimilated and, if paid, is insured as an employee. [C · S28]
- Not stated in the law: how the 25% is counted (indirect holdings, a spouse's shares). Refer such cases to the CCSS. [O]
- This applies to a SARL-S too (Guichet names it next to the SARL). [C · S22]

## What a self-employed manager pays

- **Base**: all professional income. The pay the company gives the manager counts as self-employed income for social security even with a payslip – « Le revenu est assimilé à un revenu provenant d'une profession libérale ». Salary and profit share paid by the same company are added together, and so is pay from several companies. [C · S29; S28 art. 37] Dividends from a SARL are probably not part of it (the CCSS cites the business-profit articles of the LIR, not the dividend article). [I · S29]
- **Who bears it**: the manager alone – there is **no employer share**; health and accident contributions are « entièrement à charge » of the person (social-security code arts 32, 150). [C · S28; S22] Rates, minimum and maximum base and the yearly recalculation: as for every self-employed person (contributions.md). [C · S29, S6]
- **Dependency insurance**: 1.4% on the whole income, **without** the allowance of ¼ of the minimum wage that employees get – art. 377(4) lists the points of art. 1 it applies to, and point 4 is not among them. The CCSS page on contribution bases describes the allowance without saying who gets it; do not apply it to a self-employed manager. [C · S28 art. 377(4); figures/2026.md]
- **How it is declared**: the company affiliates the manager with the **employee** entry form (or DECAFF on SECUline), ticking that the person holds the permit, is gérant and with the share percentage. [C · S30; S31] The contributions are then billed to the manager personally: the whole charge is on the insured person (arts 32, 240), a company withholds only for people working for another's account (art. 428(2)), and the CCSS runs a personal account for each self-employed person – identification number plus « 60 » – with a monthly statement. [C · S28, S21; I for managers] If the company pays that statement, it pays the manager's own debt (lu-corporate-tax, payroll and shareholder benefits).

## Pay that is owed but not paid

The answer depends on the manager's status (first section).

- **Insured as an employee**: the contribution base is the pay « payable mensuellement » (art. 34), and the company declares the contribution base every month (art. 426). [C · S28 arts 34, 426] So the pay owed for the month is declared and charged – employee and employer shares – whether or not the company pays it. [I · S28: no text treats unpaid pay differently] The monthly base is never below the minimum wage, reduced pro rata for part-time work (art. 39). [C · S28 art. 39]
- **Insured as self-employed**: the base is the net income the ACD fixes for tax (arts 35, 427). Until then the CCSS bills provisionally on the last known income – the minimum for a newcomer – and recalculates after the tax assessment. [C · S28 arts 35, 427; S22] For tax the pay counts in the year it is paid or made available (art. 108 LIR – lu-income-tax-personal income-categories.md). [C · lu-income-tax-personal S2] Pay that is only booked is therefore not yet income; credited to a current account the manager can draw on, it is. [I] Whether the ACD treats a controlling manager's decided but unpaid pay as received when due: lu-income-tax-personal PIT-O-38. [O]
- Either way the self-employed minimum base, the low-income exemption on request and the art. 244 pension reduction still apply (contributions.md, exemptions-and-side-activity.md). Pay held back and paid later counts in the year it is paid, which can take that year over the exemption limit (figures). [I]

## Can a manager keep health insurance without pension insurance?

Not as a free choice: the status comes from the law, and the branches go together. The narrow exceptions (details: exemptions-and-side-activity.md):
- **Low income** (not above ⅓ of the minimum wage a year; for part of a year, the months of activity count – art. 180(2)): the exemption on request removes **all** branches; a resident can then insure health voluntarily (social-security code art. 2(2)) – whether the CCSS allows this to someone who could stay insured is not stated (SS-O-07). [C · S28 arts 2(2), 5(3); S22, S32]
- **Income below the minimum wage**: the **pension base** can be reduced down to ⅓ of the minimum wage on request (art. 244); health and accident stay on the minimum wage. A reduction, not an opt-out. [C · S28 art. 244; I that it covers managers]
- **After 65 with an old-age pension**: no pension insurance for own-account work; health and dependency remain (art. 178(1)). [C · S28; I that it covers managers]
- **Insured in another EU country** (e.g. an employee there): one country insures everything – then nothing is paid in Luxembourg, with an A1 certificate; branches are never split. [C · S33]
- **Illness**: pension contributions are waived on request for whole months in which illness stops the business and the cash benefit is suspended (art. 244(2)). [C · S28]
- **Mutualité des employeurs**: the company's compulsory membership is for its employees under labour law (art. 53(1)); a manager insured as self-employed may join **personally** (art. 53(2)) – within 3 months of the start of affiliation for cover from the start (affiliation-and-mde.md). It does not change the pension insurance. [C · S28 art. 53; S14]

## A manager without the self-employed status

If paid, insured as an **employee** (art. 1, paragraph 3): the company withholds the employee's share and adds its own. 2026 rates – employee: health 2.80% + cash benefits 0.25% + pension 8.50% + dependency 1.40%; employer: health 2.80% + 0.25% + pension 8.50% + accident (0.65% × the company's factor) + Mutualité by class, plus 0.14% occupational health only for companies using the multi-sector health service (STM). The low-income exemption and the art. 244 reduction are for the self-employed only. [C · S28; S34]

Taxes of the manager's pay (salary, directors' fees, dividends): lu-corporate-tax, owner-manager-pay.md. The salary still bears wage withholding tax; the manager can have the own CCSS contributions written on the tax card (form 164 R, box 540): lu-corporate-tax, payroll-withholding.md. [I]

## Cross-border manager — 2025

Classify the activity before applying the ordinary mixed-activity or telework rules. A payslip does not override the social-security status; a real foreign employed activity may change the applicable country for all this person's work. See [cross-border-work.md](cross-border-work.md).
Disputed classification, marginal work and historical corrections remain SS-O-06; this does not settle CIS eligibility under personal-tax PIT-O-12. [C · S38, S39, S43] [O]

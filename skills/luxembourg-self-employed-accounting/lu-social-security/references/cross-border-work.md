# Cross-border work and social insurance — 2025

Figures in this file: 2025 — the year files win if they differ. Read on 2026-11-04.
Ordinary employed and self-employed work between Luxembourg, France, Germany and Belgium, for a person within the EU coordination regulations' personal scope. Special statuses and disputed classifications remain in SS-O-06. Income-tax allocation is a separate question.

## Determine the applicable country for each person

Residence here means habitual residence under Regulation 883/2004 article 1(j). Do not automatically reuse the income-tax residence conclusion for this separate determination. [C · S37]

One person's activities are subject to one Member State's legislation. A spouse's employment does not decide the other spouse's applicable legislation. Actual activity, residence and classification matter; Luxembourg business registration or clients alone do not select CCSS.
[C · S37; I · S37]

| Ordinary work pattern | Applicable legislation |
|---|---|
| Employed or self-employed work exercised in one state | That work state, subject to the special rules such as posting or an agreed exception. [C · S37] |
| Employment normally in several states, substantial activity in residence | Residence state. [C · S38] |
| Employment in several states, no substantial residence activity, one employer or several all established in one state | Employer establishment state. [C · S38] |
| No substantial residence activity; employers in exactly two states, one of them residence | The other employer state. [C · S38] |
| No substantial residence activity; at least two employers established in different states outside residence | Residence state. [C · S38] |
| Only self-employed work in several states, substantial activity in residence | Residence state. [C · S37] |
| Only self-employed work in several states, no substantial activity in residence | State containing the activities' centre of interests. [C · S38] |
| Employment in one state plus self-employment in another | Employment state's legislation for both activities. If employment itself spans states, apply the employee allocation first. [C · S38] |

For substantial activity, employment uses time and/or remuneration; self-employment also uses turnover and number of services. The regulation calls for an overall assessment: less than **25%** is an indicator that activity is not substantial, while CCSS expresses the practical threshold as at least 25%. Assess expected activity over the next **twelve calendar months**.
The centre of interests includes premises, duration, habitual activity, services and evidenced intentions. It is not a simple highest-turnover or registration test. [C · S39]

Marginal activities are disregarded for the allocation determination. Do not let a token job automatically displace substantial self-employment, and do not turn a practical-guide example into a universal statutory numerical cutoff. Disclose all activities to the institution.
[C · S39]

Classify company managers before selecting the branch. The 2025 Social Security Code includes qualifying SARL partners with **more than 25%** of shares and the business-permit condition, and separately specified delegated managers of other company types. A payslip does not by itself make the activity employed. [I · S43]

## Obtain the decision and keep it current

For normal multistate work, notify the competent institution in the residence state. Its determination is initially provisional and normally becomes final after **two months** unless contested or settled definitively earlier. If Luxembourg legislation applies, CCSS issues the A1; otherwise obtain the competent institution's certificate. An application is not an approval.
[C · S40, S37]

An ordinary statutory determination can in principle be retrospective. This differs from the restricted retroactivity of the telework framework below. Do not assume that every past period can be changed without corrections, transfers or refunds; use the actual institutional decision.
Report changed residence, employers, activities or work shares. Incorrect/incomplete information can lead to withdrawal of an A1 and retrospective changes. [C · S41, S42, S48]

## Employee telework framework

France, Germany, Belgium and Luxembourg participate from **1 July 2023**, covering 2025.
Eligible employees with residence-state telework of **at least 25% but less than 50%** may, on request with employer and employee agreement, remain covered in the employer state.
Telework must use the employer's IT environment and take place in the residence state;
it need not be inside a home owned by the worker. [C · S42, S44]

The framework excludes self-employed people, habitual residence-state work other than qualifying telework, habitual third-state work, and employers established in more than one state. Several employers all established in the same employer state are not inherently excluded. A manager classified as self-employed cannot use this employee exception merely because they receive a payslip. [I · S42; C · S42]

For requests from **1 July 2024**, including 2025, routine retroactivity is limited to **three months**, conditional on prior employer-state coverage/contributions; the CCSS notice specifies Luxembourg affiliation for its applications. The earlier twelve-month transition has ended.
Each agreement/A1 covers at most **three years** and requires a new request for an extension.
[C · S42, S45]

A first request in November 2026 cannot routinely use this framework to cover all of 2025.
Keep any existing 2025 decision and distinguish an ordinary historical determination or an individual article 16 exception. Below 25%, at 50% or above, or outside the framework's scope, apply ordinary coordination; a separately agreed exception must not be presumed.
[C · S42, S45] [I for the dated example]

Social-security percentages do not change the treaty working-day limits for income tax.
An eligible LU A1 can coexist with a foreign-taxable salary portion. [C · S42]

## Foreign contributions in Form 100

Article 110 recognises qualifying compulsory employee and non-employee contributions to foreign regimes covered by a bilateral or multilateral social-security instrument. Identify the legal regime and compulsory nature, person, payer and income link. Do not include every foreign payroll withholding, employer share, tax or voluntary premium. [C · S46]

Contributions linked to exempt income are excluded from the deduction against taxable income;
article 110(1) has the stated exception for article 115(11) salary supplements. Report the qualifying exempt-income connection separately for the foreign-income/rate calculation. Do not move an exempt amount into a taxable column or silently discard it. Luxembourg dependency contributions are not income-tax operating expenses, acquisition expenses or special expenses.
[C · S46, S18, S17]

Ordinary non-residents are not universally excluded: article 157(2) retains article 110(1)–(3) deductions for specified Luxembourg-source incomes. Prevent double counting across business profit, salary acquisition expenses and special expenses. [C · S46]

Foreign CSG/CRDS, private/voluntary German insurance and supplementary Belgian items need component-level legal classification; a familiar label or apparent similarity to CCSS is insufficient. Exact mixed-income apportionment and disputed retrospective corrections remain manual questions. [O]

## Spouse and children

Independent sickness rights generally take priority over derived family rights, with the regulation's exception for rights arising only from residence. Family-member eligibility for healthcare in kind uses the family member's residence legislation. A foreign-working spouse is not automatically co-insured through the Luxembourg worker. Follow CNS and residence-institution registration; A1 allocation is distinct from S1/residence healthcare documentation.
[C · S37, S25]

This does not determine every child's family-allowance priority or foreign tax benefit. [O]

## Worked examples — invented households

- One adult works independently only in Luxembourg, while the spouse works only in France:
  determine each person separately; LU for the first and FR for the second under the ordinary single-state rule. A tax-assimilation choice does not change those countries. [I]
- A Belgian resident employed by one LU employer, with 40% qualifying Belgian telework and a valid framework A1 for 2025, may remain in LU social insurance; tax-day allocation is separate. [I]
- A German resident doing substantial independent activity in Germany as well as LU ordinarily falls under German legislation. The employee telework framework does not cover that activity. [I]
- A French-resident person with real non-marginal French employment and a LU manager role properly classified as independent follows the employment state's legislation for both.
  Disputed classification or marginality needs an institutional decision. [I]

## Evidence checklist

Collect periods of residence and activities, contracts, company role/shareholding/permit facts, employer countries, actual and expected work calendars, telework versus other work, turnover and services for independence, existing A1/exception decisions, and all changes. For the return, collect annual contribution certificates, payments/refunds and taxable/exempt-income allocation.
For family healthcare, collect own-cover status and the residence institution's decision. [B]

The statutory anchor is the IGSS December-2025 collection; the framework notice was updated in March 2024. Current CCSS/CNS/DVKA procedural pages help with today's action but do not prove every website step or administrative duration was identical in 2025. Special personal-scope cases, posting, public service, pensions, unemployment and non-EU work are outside this ordinary tree. [O]

Foreign contribution-component classification is tracked in lu-income-tax-personal PIT-O-29; institutional allocation exceptions remain SS-O-06.

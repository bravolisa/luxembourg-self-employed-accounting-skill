## Rules people get wrong most often

- A business registration, tax-assimilation election or spouse's insurance does not decide this person's social-insurance country. The employee telework framework does not cover an independent activity. [C · S38, S37, S42]

- Contributions are about a quarter of professional income: 25.15% in 2026 without the optional Mutualité (24.20% in 2025), on a base between the social minimum wage and five times it; dependency insurance (1.40%) has no minimum or maximum. [C · S11 rates; I arithmetic]
- The first year is billed on a provisional base (the minimum wage, ⅓ of it for a side activity); later years use the last known income, and the CCSS regularises once the tax assessment arrives, often one to two years later. Adjusting the provisional base early, up or down, avoids a large catch-up bill. [C · S6, S22; timing I · S1]
- The low-income exemption is linked to one third of the social minimum wage (indexed), not to a fixed round number such as €10,000, and it is granted on request, with loss of cover. For part of a year, the months of activity count (art. 180(2)) – never spread a few months' income over twelve. A side activity pays on its real income, with one third of the minimum wage as a floor. [C · S13, S3; S28]
- The dependency allowance (¼ of the minimum wage) is for employees and some other groups, not for the self-employed or managers insured as self-employed (art. 377(4)). [C · S28]
- In the income tax return, compulsory contributions are special expenses deductible without limit (art. 110 n° 2 LIR); dependency insurance is excluded from that deduction. [C · S16, S17]
- Mutualité reimbursements are taxable and must be included in profit. [C · S23; S4, MyGuichet wording]
- For the municipal business tax (commercial and craft only), all compulsory contributions, dependency included, reduce the profit (Form 110 line 95). [C · S18; lu-income-tax-business S20, lu-income-tax-business S35]

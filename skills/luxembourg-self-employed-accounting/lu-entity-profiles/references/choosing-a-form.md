# Choosing a legal form

_Figures in this file: 2025–2026. The year files in `figures/` win if they differ._

| | Sole trader (entreprise individuelle) | SARL-S | SARL | SA |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Separate legal person | No – the owner is the business | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| Liability | Unlimited, with private assets | Up to the contribution | Up to the contribution | Up to the contribution |
| Minimum capital | – | €1 to €12,000 | €12,000 (since 2 June 2026 payable within a year) | €30,000 |
| Who can be a partner | – | Natural persons only; one SARL-S per person | Anyone; 1 to 100 partners | At least one shareholder |
| Formation | No formalities | Private deed or notary | Notary | Notary |
| Changes to the articles | – | Private deed possible | Notary every time | Notary |
| Manager | The owner | Natural persons only | Natural or legal person | Not covered here |
| Taxes on profit | Owner's income tax (+ ICC if commercial) | Corporate taxes; owner taxed on pay and dividends | Same | Same |

Sources: [C · S3 sole trader; S6 capital, partners, deeds; S2, S1 managers; S6 art. 420-1 for the SA]

## What changes for tax and money

- **Sole trader**: unlimited liability; private and business assets are not separated. [C · S3]
- **Company**: the company is taxed on its profit; the shareholders are taxed only on distributions. [C · S23 – principle only, the page dates from 2008; rates in lu-corporate-tax]
- A company can deduct the pay of its partner-manager (and of the manager's spouse); a sole trader cannot deduct his or her own pay. [C · S23]
- A sole trader's start-up losses flow straight to the owner and reduce his or her other taxable income. [C · S23] A company keeps its losses for its own future profits.
- A company always pays municipal business tax, also for a liberal-profession activity, and pays at least the minimum net wealth tax (lu-corporate-tax). [C · lu-corporate-tax S12, lu-corporate-tax S10]

## Questions to ask before recommending

1. Does the profession allow a company at all? Some professions' rules limit or forbid it. [C · S3]
2. How large is the personal risk (debts, liability claims)?
3. Expected profit: below roughly the level where personal tax rates exceed company rates, a sole trader is often simpler (compute it with lu-income-tax-personal and lu-corporate-tax; no single official threshold exists). [I]
4. Fixed yearly costs of a company: minimum net wealth tax, double-entry accounts, RCS filing. [C · lu-corporate-tax S10; S7]
5. Social security of the manager: lu-social-security, company-managers.md (self-employed only with more than 25% of the shares and the business permit).

This is general information, not legal or tax advice: the choice usually deserves a meeting with an accountant or the House of Entrepreneurship.

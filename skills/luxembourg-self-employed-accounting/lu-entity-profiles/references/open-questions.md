# Open points – lu-entity-profiles

Answer an open point by saying it is not settled, giving the working answer with its label, and naming who can settle it.

## ENT-O-01 · Converting a SARL-S into a SARL
- Question: Does converting a SARL-S into a SARL (capital above €12,000) need a notarial deed?
- Why it matters: Cost and timing of growing out of a SARL-S.
- Working answer: Probably yes – SARL deeds must be notarial (art. 100-4) and changes follow the form of the target deed (art. 100-12). [I · S6]
- Who can settle: Notary / LBR.
- Status: open

## ENT-O-02 · How much commercial activity may an ASBL carry on?
- Question: Within the art. 1 definition, may a commercial activity be an ASBL's main activity?
- Why it matters: ASBLs selling at events, small shops, fees.
- Working answer: Some commercial activity is allowed if members get no material gain; the main-activity limit is unknown. Tax and VAT are separate questions. [I · S5]
- Who can settle: Ministry of Justice; ACD and AED for tax and VAT.
- Status: open

## ENT-O-06 · Contracts other than employment
- Question: Which contracts (lease, bank loan, leasing, client contracts, insurance) pass to the company on incorporation?
- Why it matters: Users expect an automatic transfer.
- Working answer: Only employment contracts transfer by law; plan assignments or new contracts for the rest. [I · S26]
- Who can settle: Lawyer / notary.
- Status: open

## ENT-O-12 · Share premium in a SARL-S
- Question: Can a SARL-S be formed with a capital of at most €12,000 plus a share premium, when the equipment contributed is worth more than €12,000?
- Why it matters: Otherwise the surplus must be sold or lent to the company, or a SARL chosen.
- Working answer: Not excluded – the SARL rules on share premiums apply to a SARL-S (art. 720-1, art. 710-6(1)) and the SARL-S chapter is silent. But no official guide, model or RCS form field shows one. Check with the LBR or a notary before relying on it. [I · S6, S37]
- Who can settle: LBR / notary.
- Status: open

## ENT-O-13 · Registration duty on a SARL-S formed by private deed: €12 or €75
- Question: Does the private formation deed of a SARL-S filed with the RCS bear the €12 fixed registration duty or the €75 fixed specific duty?
- Why it matters: Cost of forming a SARL-S and of each later change of its articles.
- Working answer: €12 per deed: the €75 duty arises when the operation is recorded in a notarial deed (law of 19 December 2008, art. 2), and private deeds filed with the RCS bear the fixed registration duty the LBR collects (RCS law art. 22-3). Against: art. 1 of the 2008 law taxes « the formation of a company » without naming the form of the deed, and the law pre-dates the SARL-S. [I · S47, S36, S51, S50]
- Who can settle: LBR / AED (enregistrement).
- Status: open

## ENT-O-14 · Proportional registration duty when part of a contribution becomes a debt to the founder
- Question: When equipment is contributed to a SARL and part of its value (for example the VAT, or the value above the capital) is booked as a debt of the company to the contributor, is that part subject to the proportional registration duty – and at which rate for movable goods?
- Why it matters: A mixed contribution could cost more than the €75 or €12 expected.
- Working answer: The law taxes movable goods contributed « autrement que par l'attribution de droits sociaux » at the tariff for transfers for value (art. 5); a plain contribution for shares bears none. The rate for movable goods under the 1920 tariff was not verified. Keep the contribution paid in shares only where possible. [C · S47 art. 5, S48; O for the rate and for a debt covering only the VAT]
- Who can settle: AED (enregistrement).
- Status: open

## ENT-O-15 · How long founders answer for the value of a contribution in kind
- Question: Within what time can the company, a creditor or a later partner sue the founders of a SARL or SARL-S for an overvalued contribution in kind – and does art. 710-7(3) cover overvaluation at all?
- Why it matters: The founder values the equipment herself, without an expert; she should know for how long the figure can be challenged.
- Working answer: Founders are jointly liable for the effective paying-up of the shares and for false statements in the deed (art. 710-7(3)); in our reading that covers an overvaluation. The company law names no limitation period for claims against founders (art. 1400-6 covers managers and others), so the general limitation rules apply; they were not researched, and no court decision was looked up. Use a value she can prove. [I · S6]
- Who can settle: Lawyer / notary.
- Status: open

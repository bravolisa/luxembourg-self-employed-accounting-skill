# Equipment as share capital (contribution in kind)

_Figures in this file: 2025–2026. The year files in `figures/` win if they differ._

A sole trader who sets up a SARL or SARL-S can pay its share capital with business equipment instead of money (« apport en nature »): computers, machines, tools, furniture, a vehicle. Company law makes this simple. The tax and VAT effects are where it goes wrong – read section 8 before anyone signs. Land and buildings are not covered here. Moving the **whole business** into the company follows other rules: from-sole-trader-to-company.md.

## 1. Before answering

1. SARL or SARL-S? One founder or several?
2. Which items, bought when and for how much; what are they worth second-hand today; how far are they depreciated in her books?
3. Does the sole trade stop, or does it go on beside the company?
4. Does anything else pass to the company – clients, contracts, the name, stock, an account on a platform that brings the income? Is the equipment a line of activity of its own that she stops? Then it may be a transfer of the business or of an autonomous part, not of single items (section 8).
5. Her VAT profile (normal regime or franchise) and the company's.
6. Is any item still financed (loan, leasing)? Leased equipment is not hers to contribute. [I]

## 2. The rules in one table

| Rule | SARL | SARL-S | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Capital paid in kind | Allowed, for all or part of the capital | Allowed: contributions are in cash or in kind | [C · S6 arts 710-6(1), 710-7(2), 720-4] |
| What can be contributed | Assets that can be valued economically. A promise to do work or supply services cannot be paid for with capital shares | Same | [C · S6 art. 710-7(2)] |
| Capital limits | Minimum €12,000. No text requires any part in cash, so equipment worth €12,000 or more is enough | €1 to €12,000; above that the company must take another form | [C · S6 arts 710-5, 720-4]; equipment alone [I] |
| When it is handed over | In full at formation – the 12 months allowed since 2 June 2026 are for money only | Same | [C · S6 arts 710-6(1), 720-4] |
| Value above the nominal capital (share premium) | Allowed; paid in full at formation | The SARL-S chapter is silent; the SARL rule applies and nothing excludes it, but no official guide shows a SARL-S with a share premium | [C · S6 art. 710-6(1)]; SARL-S [I · ENT-O-12] |
| Who values the equipment | The founders. The deed specifies each contribution in kind, its conditions and the contributor; Guichet adds that it must be valued in the articles | Same, and every partner signs a declaration of honour describing the items and their value | [C · S6 art. 710-7(1); S1; S12 art. 5(1)] |
| Report by an auditor or other expert | **None, whatever the value.** The report of a réviseur d'entreprises is required for an SA only (art. 420-10); the SARL chapter has no such rule and no amount above which one is needed | None | [C · S41; I · S6] |
| Who checks | The notary: full subscription, paying-up, and the legal statements in the deed | The LBR checks that the deed's statements meet the legal requirements | [C · S6 art. 710-6(2); S12 art. 5(2)] |
| Deed | Notarial | Private deed possible, also with a contribution in kind | [C · S6 art. 100-4; S12 art. 5(1)] |

- A SARL may also provide special, non-transferable shares for work (« apports en industrie »), outside the capital; a SARL-S takes cash or kind only. [C · S6 arts 710-6(3), 720-4]
- Equipment worth more than €12,000 and a SARL-S wanted: contribute items up to €12,000 and sell or lend the rest to the company (section 7), or choose a SARL. A share premium on top of €12,000 is not confirmed for a SARL-S (ENT-O-12). [I]

## 3. Valuing the equipment – and who answers for the value

- The law gives no valuation method. Use the price the items would fetch second-hand today, and keep the proof: purchase invoices, age and condition, a dealer's quote or listings of comparable items. [I] The same figure then serves for VAT and income tax (section 8), where a value that is too high or too low has its own consequences.
- **No independent appraisal, for small and large amounts alike.** Nothing in the SARL chapter asks for one. [C · S41; I · S6] A written valuation by a dealer or an expert is voluntary. It is worth its cost when the amount is large or the items have no clear second-hand price (specialised machines, fitted installations): it is her proof towards the notary, creditors, the AED and the ACD. [I]
- The founders – in a one-person company, she alone – are jointly liable towards anyone concerned, whatever the articles say, for the shares being effectively paid up and for damage caused by false or missing statements in the deed. [C · S6 art. 710-7(3)] In our reading an overvalued contribution falls under both heads; no text names overvaluation. [I]
- How long founders can be sued is not stated; the company law fixes five years only for claims against managers and some others (art. 1400-6). [C · S6; O · ENT-O-15]
- An overvaluation does not make the company void: nullity exists only in the listed cases. [I · S6 art. 100-18]
- Guichet's page on financing a start-up says contributions in kind are valued by an expert or réviseur « depending on the legal form », and its SARL page lists a réviseur's fee « if required ». For a SARL or SARL-S the law requires none. [C · S43, S1 loosely worded; S6]
- If the SARL later becomes an SA or SCA within two years of a contribution in kind, the auditor's report is caught up at the conversion. [C · S6 art. 1010-1(2)]

## 4. The deed and the register

**SARL – notary**

- The deed lists each item or group of items, its value, the conditions, the contributor, the capital and the shares given in exchange. [C · S6 art. 710-7(1); S1]
- The law does not list the documents a notary must see. In practice notaries ask for documents confirming the value of the assets, that they can be transferred and that they are not pledged – usually a valuation certificate signed by the founders. [P · S53] Bring the list of items with values and proof, registration papers for a vehicle, and any loan contract. [I]
- The deed is published in the RESA, so the description and value of the equipment are public. [I · S7]

**SARL-S – private deed**

- The articles themselves must specify each contribution in kind, its conditions and the contributor. [C · S6 arts 710-7(1), 720-1] Guichet's model articles for a one-person SARL-S only offer the words « apport en [numéraire/nature] » – add the description and value. The model was written before 2 June 2026 and is not linked from Guichet's SARL-S page. [C · S45; I]
- With the RCS file: a **declaration of honour for contributions in kind**, signed by every partner, with a short description of the items and their value. [C · S12 art. 5(1) 3°] Models: the LBR's « Déclaration sur l'honneur – apport en nature »; Guichet's Word file (Annexe B; Annexe A is the cash version). [C · S44, S46] Capital partly in cash and partly in kind: file both. [I · S12]
- No valuation report, inventory or invoice is asked for by the regulation, the LBR brochure or the models. [C · S12, S37] The LBR may still ask questions. [I]
- The declaration is not published; it is filed again when the registered information changes, for example on a capital increase. [C · S12 art. 5(1)]

**What the register shows**

- The amount of the capital – nothing says « in kind ». The contribution appears in the published deed and, for a SARL-S, in the unpublished declaration. [C · S36 art. 6; I]
- The LBR's SARL-S brochure (2024) and Guichet's SARL and SARL-S pages (2019) still say the whole capital is paid at formation: out of date for money since 2 June 2026, still right for a contribution in kind. [C · S37, S1, S2; S6]

## 5. Costs

| Cost | SARL (notarial deed) | SARL-S (private deed) | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Registration duty on the deed | €75 fixed specific duty | €12 per private deed filed with the RCS, in our reading; €75 if formed before a notary | [C · S47 arts 1–3, S49]; SARL-S [I · S51, S50; ENT-O-13] |
| Proportional duty on the equipment | None when the equipment is paid for in shares only | Same | [C · S48, S42] |
| … if part of the value is paid otherwise than in shares (the company takes over a loan, pays cash or books a debt to her) | Proportional duty on that part; if it exceeds €75 it replaces the €75 | Same rule; the tax office may claim it after the RCS filing | [C · S47 art. 5, S42; S36 art. 22-3]; rate for movable goods [O · ENT-O-14] |
| RCS filing (excl. 17% VAT) | Registration €105.91; later change of the articles €54.78 | Registration €14.61; change €10.96 | [C · S12 annex J] |
| Notary | Fee under the official tariff, calculated on the values stated in the deed (« les apports bruts »); ask for an estimate – the euro scale was not verified | None | [C · S52] |

- The company bears these costs. [C · S42]
- The registration-duty law leaves VAT untouched (« sous réserve des dispositions relatives au régime de la taxe sur la valeur ajoutée »): no proportional duty does not mean no VAT (section 8). [C · S47 art. 1]
- A debt of the company to her for part of the value is the usual way VAT on the contribution is settled (lu-vat). Whether that alone triggers the proportional duty is open (ENT-O-14).

## 6. Later: a capital increase in kind

- An existing SARL or SARL-S can raise its capital with equipment. It is a change of the articles: three quarters of the capital unless the articles say otherwise (the sole partner decides alone, in writing), in the form of the formation deed – a notary for a SARL, a private or notarial deed for a SARL-S. [C · S6 arts 710-26, 100-12, 710-29]
- The new shares and any share premium are fully paid when issued. [C · S6 art. 710-6(4)] No auditor's report. [I · S6]
- On a capital increase the **managers**, not the founders, are jointly liable for the capital being validly subscribed and effectively paid up. [C · S6 art. 710-7(3)]
- Filing: RCS €54.78 (SARL) or €10.96 (SARL-S); a SARL-S files the declaration of honour again. Duty: €75 for the notarial deed; €12 for a SARL-S private deed in our reading. [C · S12; S47; I · ENT-O-13]
- A SARL-S cannot pass €12,000 of capital this way – it must first become a SARL (ENT-O-01). [C · S6 art. 720-4]

## 7. Selling or lending the equipment to the company instead

- Company law does not forbid a founder to sell equipment to her SARL or SARL-S – for cash or on credit – or to lend or rent it. The control of purchases from founders in the first two years (« quasi-apport », art. 420-11) exists for the SA only; it matters for a SARL only on conversion into an SA or SCA within two years. [I · S6; C · S6 art. 1010-1(2)]
- One-person company: contracts between the sole partner and the company she represents are recorded in minutes or put in writing, except ordinary transactions on normal terms. A manager with a personal financial interest in a transaction has it minuted as well (art. 441-7 through art. 710-15). [C · S6 art. 710-29] A sale of her own equipment is not an ordinary transaction: write a contract with the items, the price and the payment terms. [I]
- Guichet names lending to the company through a partner's current account as the alternative to investing in its capital. [C · S42]
- The price or rent must be the real value: too high is a hidden profit distribution, too low a hidden contribution (lu-corporate-tax, assets-from-the-founder.md).

## 8. Tax and VAT in short – details in the other skills

**A contribution, not a sale.** Legally the equipment is contributed: no sale contract, no price, no payment – the deed lists the items and she receives shares. [C · S6 art. 710-7(1)] Only the **tax is worked out as on a sale**, because she receives shares in return for the items. [I – the rows below] Say to a user « the tax is calculated as on a sale », never « the equipment is sold » or « has to be sold ».

| Question | Short answer | Where |
|---|---|---|
| VAT, she is in the normal regime, single items | Taxed like a sale: 17% VAT on the value in the deed, on top of it. She issues an invoice by the 15th of the next month; the company deducts the VAT if it makes taxed sales | lu-vat contribution-to-a-company.md |
| VAT, the whole business passes | Not a supply – no VAT, and none may be shown on any document | from-sole-trader-to-company.md; lu-vat |
| VAT, she is under the franchise | No VAT; the VAT she paid when buying stays a cost | lu-vat contribution-to-a-company.md |
| Her income tax, single items | Taxed like a sale at the real value: gain = value − book value. Ordinary profit if the sole trade goes on; part of the cessation gain (allowance and half rate) if it stops | lu-income-tax-business contribution-to-a-company.md |
| Her income tax, the business passes | Roll-over possible: no gain if the company takes over the book values (art. 59) | from-sole-trader-to-company.md; lu-income-tax-business |
| The company | Enters the items at the contribution value (not above their real value), depreciates them over the remaining useful life; investment tax credit doubtful | lu-corporate-tax assets-from-the-founder.md |
| Entries | Equipment against subscribed capital | lu-accounting companies-and-asbl.md |

**Equipment only, or the business?** The same question decides VAT and income tax, with different tests. For VAT: do the assets passing allow an independent activity to be carried on? For income tax: do the essential bases of the business pass? A deed that lists only a laptop and a desk while the clients simply follow her to the company is not settled for either tax (lu-vat VAT-O-26; lu-income-tax-business IT-O-30). Say so, and send the user to an accountant before the deed is signed. One line of activity that runs on its own and passes complete – equipment rented out through a platform, with the platform relationship – can be an autonomous part even though she keeps her other activity: from-sole-trader-to-company.md.

## 9. Worked example (invented figures)

A sole trader in the normal VAT regime owns machines bought for €10,000, depreciated to €4,000 in her books and worth €6,000 second-hand. She forms a one-person SARL-S with a capital of €6,000 paid with the machines, and stops the sole trade. No clients, contracts or stock pass.

| Step | Amount | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Articles: capital €6,000, paid in kind; list and value of the machines; declaration of honour (in kind) | – | [C · S6, S12] |
| RCS registration €14.61 + 17% VAT | €17.09 | [C · S12] |
| Registration duty on the private deed | €12 | [I · ENT-O-13] |
| Her invoice to the company: €6,000 + 17% VAT | €1,020 VAT, which the company pays her or owes her and deducts | [I · lu-vat S24] |
| Her gain: €6,000 − €4,000, part of the cessation gain, below the cessation allowance | Taxable €0 | [I · lu-income-tax-business S52] |
| The company's books: machines €6,000, subscribed capital €6,000 | – | [I] |

Had the sole trade gone on, the €2,000 would be ordinary profit of the year.

## 10. What is not settled

Share premium in a SARL-S (ENT-O-12); €12 or €75 for a SARL-S private deed (ENT-O-13); proportional duty when part of the value becomes a debt to her (ENT-O-14); how long founders answer for the value (ENT-O-15). General information, not legal or tax advice: a contribution in kind is worth an hour with a notary or accountant.

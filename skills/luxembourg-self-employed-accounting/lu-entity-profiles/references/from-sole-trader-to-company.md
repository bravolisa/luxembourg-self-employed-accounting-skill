# From sole trader to company

_Figures in this file: 2025–2026. The year files in `figures/` win if they differ._

Moving a business into a SARL or SARL-S is not a change of name: a **new legal person** takes over. Several points are not settled by official texts – say so and suggest an accountant or notary.

This file is about moving the **whole business**. Paying the company's capital with **equipment only** (a contribution in kind of single items) follows other rules – VAT is due, the income-tax roll-over does not apply: contribution-in-kind.md.

| Step | Rule | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Is it allowed? | A commercial sole trader can in general set up a company to continue the business in a compatible legal form; some professions' rules limit or forbid it | [C · S3] |
| Business permit | The permit is issued to the enterprise; the company's final permit requires its articles to be registered with the RCS. The sole trader's personal permit does not pass to the company | [C · S16, S18; I passing] |
| Old permit | When the sole trader stops trading, tell the Ministry of the Economy within one month and cancel the permit online in MyGuichet; otherwise it lapses after more than two years of voluntary cessation | [C · S31; lu-business-permit S1] |
| RCS | The sole trader asks for removal of his or her RCS entry when the business ceases or is transferred (art. 5 RCS law) | [C · S7] |
| VAT number | The company is a new taxable person: it declares the start of its activity within 15 days and gets its own number; the sole trader declares the end of the activity within 15 days (on the AED form, which asks to whom the business went). The number is not transferred | [C · S30 arts 1, 5; I that the number is not transferred] |
| VAT on the transfer | Transferring all or part of a business (universalité de biens) to another taxable person is not a supply of goods (art. 9(2)) or of services (art. 15(2)); the company continues the transferor for VAT. No implementing regulation was found; the conditions come from EU law: the transfer of a business or an autonomous part « susceptible de poursuivre une activité économique autonome », to a taxable person who intends to run it rather than liquidate it – also as a contribution to a company (CJEU Zita Modes, a Luxembourg reference). VAT that fell due before the transfer stays with the sole trader | [C · S14; S29] |
| Only some assets pass | The no-supply rule does not cover a simple transfer of assets: single items contributed or sold to the company are taxed like any sale (lu-vat contribution-to-a-company.md). It is not a choice – where a business passes, the law itself says there is no supply | [C · S14; S29] |
| One line of activity passes, the rest stays | The same rules as for a whole business, if the line is an autonomous part: for VAT a partial universality (everything that line needs to run on its own passes and the company carries it on), for income tax an autonomous part of the business (its essential bases pass and she keeps at least one other autonomous part). Typical case: equipment rented out through an online platform beside a consultancy she keeps. Tests and limits: lu-vat contribution-to-a-company.md §3, lu-income-tax-business contribution-to-a-company.md §2 | [I · S14, S28] |
| Company-law route for a sole trader | A natural person may transfer all or part of his or her **professional assets**, with assets and liabilities, to a company (« transfert du patrimoine professionnel »): a written contract, a transfer project with an inventory and the total value of assets and liabilities, published at least one month before, and a written report. Only allowed when the inventory shows more assets than liabilities. The listed assets and liabilities then pass by operation of law; she stays jointly liable for three years for debts born before the transfer. It is optional – a plain contribution in kind in the deed remains possible. How it combines with paying the capital at formation is for the notary | [C · S6 arts 1050-1, 1050-3 to 1050-8]; combination [I] |
| « Apport d'une branche d'activités » | The company law defines it only for a **company** contributing to another company. Its definition of a branch – a whole that, technically and by its organisation, carries on an autonomous activity and can work by its own means – is a useful yardstick, but a sole trader's deed uses a contribution in kind or the transfer of professional assets. The tax treatment follows what really passes, not the label | [C · S6 arts 1040-1, 1040-3]; yardstick and tax [I] |
| Contributing one building | A first-instance court held that contributing one let building, which is run on its own, can be such a partial transfer (2012) [I · S32]. On appeal the contributor still lost: the contribution deed showed VAT, and VAT shown is owed (art. 61(8) VAT law; Cour d'appel 2015) [C · S33]. Never show VAT on a transfer you treat as outside VAT | [I · S32]; [C · S33] |
| Employees | Employment contracts transfer automatically, including on "mise en société" | [C · S26] |
| Other contracts | Lease, loans, leasing, client contracts, insurance: no official text says they pass automatically – plan assignments or new contracts | [O · ENT-O-06] |
| Contracts signed before the company exists | They bind the signatories personally and jointly unless the company takes them over within two months of its formation | [C · S6] |
| Income tax | Contributing the whole business (or an autonomous part) to a resident, fully taxable company in exchange for its shares: the company may take over the book values or an intermediate value (never above going-concern value), and the contributor values the contribution at the company's figures – so no taxable gain need arise (art. 59(3)–(4) LIR). Acquisition dates carry over; a liberal practice qualifies too (art. 93). When the company takes the business over below its operating value, shares she holds privately stay taxable like business assets (art. 59(6)) until she sells them or declares that she moves them to her private assets – which is taxed like a withdrawal; after a contribution at full operating value they are ordinary private shares. A contribution gain is taxed at half the global rate; the cessation allowance applies only when all hidden reserves are disclosed (art. 59(4)). It is not subject to municipal business tax (circular 59/1, point 6.1.2). Details: lu-income-tax-business contribution-to-a-company.md | [C · S27; S28] |

The tax side of the transfer (gain, ICC, the last sole-trader return) belongs with lu-income-tax-business and an adviser; this is general information, not tax advice.

# Luxembourg bookkeeping and accounts for self-employed people

_Figures in this file: 2025–2026. The year files of the domain skills (lu-vat, lu-income-tax-business, lu-social-security) win if they differ._

Follow lu-tax-core for triage, labels and answer shape. **[C]** Confirmed, **[I]** Interpretation, **[P]** Practice, **[O]** Open, **[B]** BravoLisa convention; source ids in [references/sources.md](references/sources.md).

## Before answering

1. Commercial or craft activity (business permit, Code de commerce) or liberal profession?
2. Turnover level – above the bookkeeping threshold (lu-income-tax-business, figures)? – and whether the ACD has notified a bookkeeping duty.
3. Profit method used for income tax (regular accounts, accrual without accounts, simplified).

## Rules people get wrong most often

- Liberal professions are not traders, but they still fall under the tax bookkeeping duty once turnover exceeds the bookkeeping threshold (lu-income-tax-business, figures), from the year after the tax bulletin for that year is notified. [C · S11]
- Commercial and craft traders do **not** wait for a tax notice: they need double-entry books from the year after a year above that threshold (Code de commerce), and a yearly inventory at any turnover. [C · S11 §2.1.2]
- There is no official ACD form for the balance sheet and P&L attached to the tax return: the taxpayer attaches his own presentation. [C · S2]
- Commercial sole traders above the threshold file annual accounts with the RCS at the latest 7 months after year end [C · S12]; natural persons' accounts are not published. [C · S3]
- Keep accounting documents and invoices for 10 years. [C · S3]
- A separate business bank account is not required by any rule we found, but strongly recommended. [I]

# Deductible and mixed expenses

_Figures in this file: 2025–2026. The year files in `figures/` win if they differ._

## The test

- Deductible: expenses that reduce the business's net assets without a counterpart, incurred for the business (art. 45 LIR). « Une charge n'est déductible des produits que si elle a entraîné une diminution du patrimoine de l'entreprise. » [C · S1] The law's own words: « Sont considérées comme dépenses d'exploitation déductibles les dépenses provoquées exclusivement par l'entreprise. » [C · S52 art. 45(1)]
- Not deductible: household and lifestyle expenses, « même lorsqu'elles sont faites en vue de profiter ou sont susceptibles de profiter à sa profession » (art. 12 n° 1 LIR). [C · S3]
- Mixed private/business costs « sont en principe assimilées à des dépenses privées »; a partial deduction is possible when the parts « peuvent être séparées de façon nette et selon des critères objectifs et retraçables ». [C · S1]
- Costs linked to tax-exempt income are not deductible. [C · S1]
- Proof: no special form, but expenses must be correctly recorded and documents available on request; the burden of proof is on the business owner. [C · S1]

## Never deductible

- Gifts, donations and subsidies (« les libéralités, dons, subventions »), art. 12 n° 2 LIR: never a business expense; approved donations are a personal special expense (lu-income-tax-personal) and reduce the ICC base on Form 110 (municipal-business-tax.md). [C · S3]
- Fines and penalties of any kind, « même lorsque ces pénalités sont en rapport économique » with the income (art. 12 n° 4 LIR). [C · S3]
- VAT due on private withdrawals or private use of business assets (art. 12 n° 3 b LIR). [C · S3]
- Rent the business "pays" to its owner for his own building: a private withdrawal. [C · S1]
- Income tax itself. [C · S3]

## Typical deductible costs for a service business (with proof, business share only)

Computer and equipment (€870 rule or depreciation), software and platform fees, internet and phone (business share by use), books and materials, training for the current activity, website and advertising, accountant and bank fees, professional insurance, home office if the room qualifies, business trips (tickets, hotels). [I]

CCSS contributions are not operating expenses: they are special expenses in the personal return (lu-social-security). [I · S27] A business expense lowers the profit, so it lowers CCSS as well as income tax; what that saves: lu-income-tax-personal deduction-review.md. [I]

## A side activity: optional flat rate instead of actual costs

A person who carries on an independent activity **beside a main activity** (commercial, farming or liberal profit – for example an employee who also gives paid courses or earns commissions) may estimate all operating expenses at a flat rate instead of proving them: 30% of the year's receipts up to €2,000; €600 + 25% of the part between €2,000 and €6,000; €1,600 + 20% of the part between €6,000 and €15,000; €3,400 above €15,000. [C · S68]

- It is optional: higher actual costs can be claimed with proof instead. It replaces **all** operating expenses – no extra car or home-office costs on top. [C · S68; I for "nothing on top"]
- Profit is found by comparing receipts and expenses, noted in date order; the flat rate follows the receipts of the calendar year. [C · S68]
- Not for a main activity, and not for side activities that have their own circular (insurance agents: figures file). Pay from the same employer as the main job is salary, not a side activity. [C · S68]

## Splitting mixed costs

| Cost | Split by | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Home (rent, energy, charges) | Office m² ÷ living area (cellar, garage, laundry, attic excluded) | [C · S2 for employees; I for self-employed] |
| Internet, phone | Share of business use, documented | [I] |
| Car | Business km ÷ total km | [C · S1, S4, S5] |
| Costs paid by a partner | Only the share you actually bear; co-owners: see home-office.md | [I] |

Keep the same method every year and write the calculation down.

## Gifts, meals and representation, clothing, training

| Cost | Rule | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Gifts to clients or partners | Not deductible (art. 12 n° 2). The ACD calls deductible gifts rare: only when the gift serves the business so directly that « le caractère de libéralité disparaît complètement devant l'intérêt immédiat et exclusif de l'entreprise » | [C · S3; S60] |
| Restaurant meals, receptions, representation | Deductible only when caused by the business (art. 45(1)); lifestyle spending is not, even when it helps the business (art. 12 n° 1). No flat rate. The owner's own costs go on « Frais de voyage et de représentation du patron » (Form 112 line 12, Form 152 line 11) | [C · S3, S52]; lines [C · S4, S5] |
| Clothing | Everyday clothes are lifestyle costs. The law names « vêtements typiquement professionnels » only for employees (art. 105(2) n° 6); for the self-employed the general test above gives the same line: work-only clothing (protective, uniform) can be deducted | [I · S3] |
| Training | Training in the current activity is deductible. The ACD treats the cost of acquiring the knowledge needed to enter a profession as non-deductible lifestyle spending (art. 12 n° 1); it says so for employees, and art. 12 applies to every kind of income | [C for employees · S2; I for self-employed] |

VAT on representation and entertainment is never deductible: lu-vat normal-regime.md.

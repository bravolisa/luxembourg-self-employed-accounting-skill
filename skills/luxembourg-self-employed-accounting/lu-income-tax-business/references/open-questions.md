# Open points – lu-income-tax-business

Answer an open point by saying it is not settled, giving the working answer with its label, and naming who can settle it.

## IT-O-01 · Home-office criteria for self-employed people
- Question: Does the ACD apply the employee criteria of circular 105/2 (separate room with a door, at least 90% work use, no other office) to self-employed people?
- Why it matters: Decides whether home costs can be deducted at all.
- Working answer: Use the circular's criteria as a cautious guide. [I · S2]
- Who can settle: ACD (written question).
- Status: open

## IT-O-03 · Mortgage interest on a home with an office
- Question: How is mortgage interest split between the private home-loan deduction and the business share?
- Why it matters: Double deduction risk.
- Working answer: If the office part is a business asset under the regulation of 11 August 1970 (more than one fifth of the building's value or more than €100,000 – home-office.md), the loan share that financed it is a business debt and its interest an operating expense – our reading. [I · S56] If the office part stays private, whether its share of interest and depreciation can be deducted is open. The 2026-11-04 review confirmed that the asset test is value-based, with the 2017 amendment/grandfathering; employee circular 105/2 is not a self-employed ruling. Never deduct the same interest twice. [C · S56, S57, S2 for scope; I for the business-debt reading; O for the private-asset branch]
- Who can settle: ACD.
- Status: open

## IT-O-04 · Form line for a home-office rent share
- Question: Which line does the ACD expect for the business share of rent?
- Why it matters: Filing.
- Working answer: Form 152 line 18 a) / Form 112 line 20 (buildings without VAT). [I · S4, S5]
- Who can settle: ACD.
- Status: open

## IT-O-05 · Home as the fixed place of business for the permit
- Question: Is a private home accepted as the fixed place of business for the business permit?
- Why it matters: Permit applications of freelancers working from home.
- Working answer: Usually yes for intellectual services, with a residence certificate or lease; not for workshops, shops or HORECA. [P · S26] The law's test has five elements – an installation suited to the activity, equipment, effective management, regular presence, documents kept there – and says a domiciliation address is not enough; it does not mention a home either way. [C · lu-business-permit S1]
- Who can settle: DG PME (Ministry of the Economy).
- Status: open

## IT-O-06 · €0.30/km for self-employed people
- Question: Can a self-employed person deduct €0.30/km instead of actual car costs?
- Why it matters: Simpler car deduction for many users.
- Working answer: No: actual costs × business share of km. Business trips in a private car are deductible as operating expenses on the actual costs (insurance, fuel, repairs) with receipts – the ACD director's decision reproduced in a 2016 first-instance judgment refused the deduction because no receipts were produced, and the court asked the taxpayer to prove the business share. [I · S67] Guichet: car costs are deductible only within the business use of the car. [C · S1] No law, regulation or circular gives the self-employed a rate per kilometre; the only flat rate found replaces all expenses of a side activity (circular 94/3, mixed-expenses.md). [C · S68; I for "none found"] The €0.30 rate is an employee rule. [I · S18] The Government-in-Council regulation of 13 December 2024 behind it was repealed from 1 February 2026; the text setting the kilometre allowance for 2026 was not found. [C · S81; O]
- Who can settle: ACD.
- Status: open

## IT-O-07 · Per diem for the owner's own trips
- Question: Can the owner deduct employee-style per diem rates for business trips?
- Why it matters: Simpler travel deduction.
- Working answer: No: actual costs with receipts. [I] Art. 50 LIR allows a regulation dispensing the owner from detailed proof of travel and stay costs; none was found (searched 2026-10-26). [C · S3 for art. 50; I]
- Who can settle: ACD.
- Status: open

## IT-O-08 · When a mixed-use car becomes a business asset
- Question: Which business-use share makes a car a business asset; can a private car be partly depreciated?
- Why it matters: Depreciation and a taxable gain on sale.
- Working answer: The law gives no percentage: business assets are those that by their nature are intended to serve the business; only a trader with regular accounts may opt to add others; liberal professions have no option. [C · S3] For a car that stays private but is also used for the business, the costs are deductible only in the proportion of the actual business use, and the person must prove that proportion – a first-instance court said so in 2016 (not binding in other cases). [I · S67] From which share of business use the car itself is a business asset is stated nowhere: mostly business use – probably a business asset; mostly private – running costs × business km only. [I · S3]
- Who can settle: ACD.
- Status: open

## IT-O-15 · MyGuichet branches and Form 110 route B
- Question: How do the MyGuichet branches map to Form 110 route B (accrual without regular accounts)?
- Why it matters: Filing online.
- Working answer: MyGuichet's Form 100 asks « Comment le bénéfice est-il déterminé ? » with two answers: « Bilan et P&P » or « Recettes et dépenses ». [C · S62] Route B (accrual without regular accounts) has no branch of its own; as an art. 18(1) accrual profit it most likely belongs under « Bilan et P&P », with the « Redressement hors bilan » field for adjustments. [I]
- Who can settle: ACD / MyGuichet help.
- Status: open

## IT-O-20 · When depreciation starts in the first year
- Question: Does depreciation start when the asset is put into use (mise en service), and is the first year counted in whole months or days?
- Why it matters: Form 113 schedules; a full year cannot be taken for an asset bought in December.
- Working answer: Depreciation starts when the asset is delivered – when the business can dispose of it; it need not be in use yet: « La période d'amortissement d'une immobilisation prend cours, en cas d'acquisition, à partir de la fourniture de l'immobilisation … L'utilisation immédiate du bien n'est cependant pas nécessaire. » (circular 34/1). [C · S7] Form 113 asks only for the date of acquisition. [C · S53] The first year is pro rata for the time left until the year end, with a tolerance of a full year for assets acquired in the first half of the year and half a year in the second half (circular L.I.R. n° 61). [C · S58] No official text says whether the pro rata counts months or days; counting whole months, with the rest in an extra final year, is BravoLisa's choice. [B] Where the month of delivery and the month of first use differ, use the month of delivery: the ACD's text counts from delivery. [C · S7 for delivery] In the year of a sale, the same logic gives depreciation pro rata up to the month of disposal. [I]
- Who can settle: ACD; an accountant.
- Status: open

## IT-O-21 · IT developers and consultants with a commercial permit
- Question: Is the profit of a software developer or IT, marketing or management consultant who holds the general commercial permit commercial profit (art. 14 LIR: Form 110, municipal business tax) or liberal-profession profit (art. 91 LIR: Form 100 page 6, Form 152, no ICC)?
- Why it matters: The largest group of BravoLisa users; decides the forms and whether ICC is due.
- Working answer: The permit does not decide the tax category. The Cour administrative's test (54190C, 2026): personal, intellectual services that are the very object of the activity point to art. 91; selling goods or reselling others' work points to art. 14 (categories-and-forms.md). [C · S63 for the test; I for IT] Art. 91(1) 1° names « ingénieurs » and « experts-conseils » [C · S3 art. 91], and the ACD's own Form 100 schema lists them as liberal codes **INGE – Ingénieur** and **EXPE – Expert-conseils** [C · S62]. Applied to IT [I · S63]: France/Germany treaty business-profit definitions include independent professions; Belgium retains a fixed-base liberal-profession article. These treaty labels do not decide domestic commercial/liberal classification, Form 110/152 or ICC. See personal-tax cross-border-income.md. [I]
  - a developer or engineer with higher education who designs and builds software for clients personally – also on a contract through an agency or IT services firm – leans **liberal** (the personal service is the product);
  - the same work without higher education is **unclear** (the first of the court's four features is missing; a « similar activity » is weaker);
  - reselling licences, hardware or hosting, or selling one's own software product, is **commercial** for that part (the pharmacist's logic);
  - employing developers and reselling their work is **commercial** (no longer essentially personal);
  - routine support or helpdesk work risks **commercial** (weaker intellectual and qualification features).
  - **custom development** for a client is a service and fits the test; selling the rights to one's own **standard product** to many customers (licences, SaaS) is closer to selling goods – commercial risk for that part;
  - comparing code to a writer's work (« activité littéraire ») is weak: the closer comparison in art. 91 is « ingénieurs ».
  How to declare if liberal: Form 100 page 6 with code INGE (plus Form 152 on the simplified method), no Form 110, no ICC; the commercial permit does not stand in the way (categories-and-forms.md). Below €40,000 of profit the ICC difference is nil anyway – mostly a question of forms.
  Job titles do not decide: the activity does. « Ingénieur indépendant » is no longer a permit profession (law of 26 July 2023 – lu-business-permit qualifications.md); IT work holds the general commercial permit. German case law on IT work (§ 18 EStG) is context only. Ask the person, and keep the choice consistent with earlier returns.
- Who can settle: ACD.
- Status: open

## IT-O-24 · Two commercial or craft activities of one person
- Question: Are two commercial or craft activities of one sole trader one business (one Form 110, one €40,000 ICC allowance) or separate businesses?
- Why it matters: Forms and municipal business tax.
- Working answer: Treat them as one business when they are run together; ask when they are clearly separate operations. [I] The 2025 MyGuichet schema accepts up to ten commercial activities, each with its own tax file number – possible separate files, but nothing on the €40,000 allowance. [C · S62 for the schema; I]
- Who can settle: ACD.
- Status: open

## IT-O-25 · Investment tax credit on an asset also used privately
- Question: A phone or laptop used mainly for the business, recorded as a business asset, but also used privately: is the 12% credit (art. 152bis §7) on the full price or only on the business share?
- Why it matters: Phones, laptops and tablets are the most common small investments of sole traders.
- Working answer: On the full price, when the asset is a business asset. The credit is calculated « sur le prix d'acquisition ou de revient des investissements effectués »; the law names no reduction for private use (it asks for exclusive use only for certain vehicles), and Form 800 reduces the price only by subsidies. [C · S3, S33] A business asset stays whole in the depreciation table, where the private use is taken out as the private share of the year's depreciation (Form 113, « Part privée »). [C · S53] So the credit is on the full price and the private use is corrected through depreciation and costs; an asset that is not a business asset at all (mostly private use) gets no credit. [I · S3, S33, S53 – medium confidence: no ACD text or judgment names the case]
- Who can settle: ACD.
- Status: open

## IT-O-26 · Investment tax credit on an item written off at once (€870 rule)
- Question: Does an item of €870 or less, written off in full in the year of purchase (art. 34), still get the investment tax credit if its normal useful life is 3 years or more?
- Why it matters: Many small purchases (phones, tools, office equipment) are written off at once.
- Working answer: Probably yes, if the item's usual useful life is three years or more. The law excludes « les biens amortissables au cours d'une période inférieure à 3 années »; the ACD describes them as assets « amortissables normalement » in less than three years. [C · S3, S31] The €870 write-off is an option that departs from normal depreciation « sans égard à leur durée usuelle d'utilisation » (circular 34/1); it does not change the item's usual useful life, and the law lists no exclusion for low-value items. [C · S7, S3] So a phone, tool or piece of furniture of €870 or less is not excluded just because it was written off at once; the other conditions still apply (new, tangible, commercial or craft business, not a vehicle). [I · S3, S7, S31 – medium confidence: no ACD text or judgment says it in so many words] For no risk at all, depreciate the item over its useful life in the year the credit is claimed. [I]
- Who can settle: ACD.
- Status: open

## IT-O-27 · Gain on the sale of a business car that was also used privately
- Question: When a car that is a business asset and was also used privately is sold, is the whole gain (price minus book value) business profit, or only the business share?
- Why it matters: The private share of depreciation was never deducted, yet the book value was written down in full.
- Working answer: The whole gain: a movable asset belongs to the business as a whole (the law splits only buildings), and Form 113 writes the book value down by the full depreciation before the private share is taken out. No Luxembourg text says it. [I – medium · S3, S53]
- Who can settle: ACD.
- Status: open

## IT-O-28 · Higher first lease instalment: at once or spread
- Question: Is a higher first instalment of a car lease deductible in full in the year it is paid, or spread over the lease?
- Why it matters: First instalments of several thousand euros are common.
- Working answer: Guichet says it is « généralement fiscalement déductible par le locataire » without saying when. Receipts-and-expenses method: when paid; regular accounts: spread over the lease; always × the business km share. [C · S74; I – medium]
- Who can settle: ACD.
- Status: open

## IT-O-29 · Purchase subsidy for a business-asset electric car paid in a later year
- Question: Does the State's purchase subsidy reduce the cost of a business-asset car also when it is paid in a later year, and what happens when it must be repaid?
- Why it matters: The subsidy is applied for after registration and can arrive in the next tax year.
- Working answer: Form 113 depreciates the price less subsidies received. [C · S53] Paid later: reduce the amount still to depreciate when it is granted; repaid: add it back. [I – medium]
- Who can settle: ACD.
- Status: open

## IT-O-30 · Equipment only in the deed while the company carries on the activity: contribution of the business?
- Question: A sole trader stops, and her new SARL carries on the same activity with the same clients; the deed contributes only equipment for shares. Does the ACD treat this as a contribution of the business under art. 59 (roll-over possible, goodwill included) or as a cessation with a contribution of single items?
- Why it matters: It decides whether a gain arises, on which assets, and how her shares are taxed afterwards.
- Working answer: Not settled. Art. 59 needs the essential bases of the business to pass (circular 59/1); for a service freelancer the equipment is probably not an essential base, so on the deed alone it is a cessation with single items – gain on the equipment, €10,000 allowance, half rate. But the ACD can look at what really passed (clients, name), and Guichet treats the move into a capital company as a transfer of the business. [I · S82, S86]
- Who can settle: ACD.
- Status: open

## IT-O-31 · Split-business circular and movable equipment rented to one's own company
- Question: Does the ACD apply circular L.I.R. n° 66 (« scission d'entreprise ») when the majority partner rents movable business equipment – not a building – to her SARL?
- Why it matters: If it applies there is no cessation, the rent is commercial profit with municipal business tax, and her shares are business assets.
- Working answer: Only for essential assets – typical for the business or specially adapted (circular 66a); ordinary office or IT equipment probably is not, a fitted workshop or specialised machines could be. Otherwise the rent is rental income after a cessation. No court decision was found. [I · S84, S83]
- Who can settle: ACD.
- Status: open

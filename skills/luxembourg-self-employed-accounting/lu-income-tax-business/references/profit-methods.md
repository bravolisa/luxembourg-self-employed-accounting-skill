## 1. The default and the exception

- **Default (accrual)**: profit is the change in net invested assets between two year-ends, adjusted for withdrawals and contributions (art. 18(1) LIR). On Form 110 section A: closing net invested assets (line 12) − opening (line 13) + personal withdrawals in cash and in kind (line 15) − additional contributions in cash and in kind (line 17) = profit or loss (line 18). [C · S20]
- **Exception (simplified)**: profit as receipts minus operating expenses (art. 18(3) LIR; RGD of 3 December 1969). Switching method triggers catch-up adjustments so that lifetime profit is the same: the method shifts timing, never total tax. [C · S61 arts 3–4; S21 points 4–5] Details: "Switching method" below.
- **Who cannot use the simplified method** (ACD circular 18/5, point 2.1): [C · S21]
  - anyone keeping regular accounts – because §161 AO requires it or voluntarily. Traders bound only by the Code de commerce (not by §161 AO) may still use it if in fact they keep no regular accounts in the tax sense;
  - a business whose net invested assets other than fixed assets and cash (stock, receivables, debts…) usually change significantly during the year – about 25%; an exceptional change does not count;
  - a business whose such assets generally exceed 150% of taxable profit at year-end; an occasional excess (a weak year) does not count;
  - a business or liberal practice bought as a whole, unless those assets are insignificant.
- **Must leave it** once those assets exceed 150% of the average yearly profit of the last few years. [C · S21 point 2.1.3]
- **Stock and work in progress** are not adjusted year by year on the simplified method: purchases count when paid. Stock, work in progress, receivables and debts only matter for the two tests above and for the catch-up when the method changes. [C · S61 arts 1–4; S21 points 2.1.2, 3 b)]
- Software that records invoices does not by itself mean "regular accounts". Distinguish facts from labels. [I · S27]
- Notaries must keep double-entry books whatever their turnover. [C · S48 §2]

### Switching method

- **Into the simplified method** (from the balance-sheet method, or after buying a business or practice as a whole): only if the operating conditions justify it – for example the business can hardly bear the cost of accounts – and the net invested assets other than fixed assets and cash are insignificant. In the first simplified year, deduct those assets (as at the start of the year) from profit. After buying as a whole, split the price paid between fixed assets (normal rules) and the other items (deductible at once). [C · S61 art. 3; S21 point 4]
- **Out of the simplified method** (to the balance-sheet method): add the net invested assets other than fixed assets and cash, as at the start of the first balance-sheet year, to that year's profit – or deduct them if negative. On request, a large amount can be spread over that year and at most the four following years. [C · S61 art. 4 al. 1–2; S21 point 5]
- **Sale or cessation:** treated as a switch just before it. The addition goes onto the sale or cessation gain and the art. 130 abatement cannot reduce it; a reduction goes onto the current year's profit. The gain itself is never computed by the simplified method. [C · S61 arts 1, 4 al. 3]

## 2. The €100,000 bookkeeping test

| Point | Rule | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Commercial and craft traders | Bound first by the Code de commerce, which tax law adopts (§160 AO): double-entry books, unless the previous business year's turnover excl. VAT was not above €100,000 (arts 11, 13 C.com.). No tax notification is needed, so the duty follows the year after a year above €100,000. A yearly inventory of everything the business owns and owes is required whatever the bookkeeping system (art. 15 C.com.) | [C · S48 §§2–2.1.2] |
| Duty under §161 AO | Any business owner not already bound by other law – in practice liberal professions – must keep accrual double-entry books, an annual inventory and a balance sheet once turnover exceeds €100,000 in a year | [C · S48 §§3–3.2] |
| Liberal professions | Included since the 2017 reform; the old waiver is gone, enforced from the first year starting after 31 Dec 2017 | [C · S48 §§3, 3.4] |
| Turnover measured | Excluding VAT; includes exempt turnover; residents: worldwide supplies; includes unbilled work at year-end; excludes disposals of fixed assets; advances count only for work already done | [C · S48 §3.3] |
| When it starts (§161) | From the first day of the business year after the tax bulletin for a year above €100,000 is notified – in practice a lag of one to two years | [C · S48 §3.4] |
| One-off overrun | A single exceptional overrun does not trigger the duty | [C · S48 §3.3] |
| Exit | Falling back below €100,000 does not end it; relief only on motivated request with the tax office's prior consent | [C · S48 §3.4] |
| No detailed turnover record | A simplified-method taxpayer without a detailed record of turnover has it determined on cash receipts | [C · S48 §3.3] |

## 3. What each method does

| | Simplified (art. 18(3)) | Accrual (art. 18(1)) |
|---|---|---|
| Income | When received ("available") | When earned (work done, invoice due) |
| Expenses | When paid | When incurred |
| Unpaid client / supplier invoices | Ignored until paid | On the balance sheet |
| Depreciation | Same as accrual – Form 113, €870 floor | Form 113 |
| Provisions | Not available | Available |
| VAT | Carried gross; VAT paid to the AED is an expense (see vat-in-income-tax.md) | Net; VAT never touches profit |
| Filing | Form 152 (liberal) or Form 110 route C (commercial) | Balance sheet + P&L |
[C · S4, S24 for structure; I for VAT treatment details]

## 4. Rules of the simplified method people get wrong

- "Available" applies to income. Receiving an unpaid supplier invoice is **not** a deduction trigger; payment is. [I · S21, S27]
- Preparing full accrual figures and then filtering out unpaid invoices is not automatically allowed. [I · S27]
- Fixed assets are depreciated, not expensed when paid (except items up to €870). [C · S4]
- Form 110 section III ("données diverses", lines 59–68) asks for balances (cash, bank, receivables, stock, debts) even from simplified-method filers; those balances do not adjust the cash profit. [C · S24]
- "Operating expenses paid (Form 152 or equivalent annex)" allows another presentation; it is not permission to use accrual numbers on the cash method. [I · S27]
- **Loans:** money borrowed or lent and repayments are neither receipts nor expenses; interest counts when paid or received; a repayment premium is deducted in the year the loan money is received. [C · S61 art. 2; S21 point 3 c)]
- **Non-depreciable fixed assets** (land, loans granted…): the cost is deducted only on sale or loss; the sale price is a receipt. [C · S61 art. 2; S21 point 3 d)]

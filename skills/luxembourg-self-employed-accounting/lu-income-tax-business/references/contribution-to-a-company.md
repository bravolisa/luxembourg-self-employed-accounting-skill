# Putting business equipment into your own company – income tax of the sole trader

_Figures in this file: 2025–2026. The year files in `figures/` win if they differ._

A sole trader forms a SARL or SARL-S and contributes equipment as share capital (or sells it to the company). This file covers **her** income tax. The company's side: lu-corporate-tax assets-from-the-founder.md. VAT: lu-vat contribution-to-a-company.md. Company law and costs: lu-entity-profiles contribution-in-kind.md. Several answers are our reasoning from the law and ACD circulars, not a published ACD position – labelled [I]; suggest an accountant before the deed is signed.

## 1. Three situations

| Situation | Treatment | Tax on her gain |
|---|---|---|
| A. The **business** (or an autonomous part) is contributed for shares | Art. 59: the company may take over the book values – then no gain (section 4) | None at book value |
| B. **Single items** are contributed; the sole trade **goes on** | An exchange: sale of the item at its real value (section 3) | Ordinary profit of the year |
| C. Single items are contributed; the sole trade **stops** | Cessation of the business (section 4) | Cessation gain: €10,000 allowance, half rate |

## 2. The business, or single items?

- The roll-over of art. 59 exists only for « une entreprise ou une partie autonome d'entreprise ». The law defines neither. [C · S3 art. 59]
- For the ACD, contributing a business means contributing its **essential bases**: assets whose possession is « nécessaire et indispensable » for the objectives of the business. [C · S82 point 2] An asset is in general essential when it is typical for the business or adapted to its specific needs; a business's trucks are business assets but generally not essential. [C · S83]
- So non-essential assets may stay behind. [I] What she keeps privately is counted at its estimated sale value, and the difference to its book value is part of her gain. [C · S3 arts 36, 55]
- An **autonomous part** is a more or less independent part that forms a whole, and she must keep at least one other autonomous part. One machine, or « the equipment », is not an autonomous part. [C · S82 point 2; I]
- **One line of activity passes, the rest stays** – for example equipment rented out through an online marketplace beside a consultancy she keeps. This can be an autonomous part: the ACD's yardstick is a more or less independent part that forms a whole by itself – it need not be viable on its own – and she keeps at least one other autonomous part. [C · S82 point 2] Its essential bases must pass: the equipment the line cannot work without and, in our reading, the platform relationship that brings its income; she stops that line herself. [I] That the company is afterwards paid by the same platform is evidence that the line continues, not the test. [I] Not settled for platform businesses. [O · IT-O-30]
- **Only equipment is contributed.** It is the business only if that equipment is its essential base – a fitted workshop or salon, specialised machines. A consultant's, translator's or developer's laptop and desk can be replaced at once and are probably not: single items (section 3), and a cessation if she stops (section 4). [I]
- **The deed lists only equipment, but the company carries on with her clients and name.** Not settled. [O · IT-O-30] The texts point both ways: under art. 59 the contribution is valued « y compris les valeurs immatérielles du fonds d'exploitation »; Guichet treats the change from sole trade to capital company as a transfer of the business; any operation that realises all hidden reserves at once counts as a transfer of the business. [C · S3 arts 59, 15; S86] Our reading: the ACD can look at what really passed. If the business as such passed, art. 59 applies to all of it, goodwill included – no gain at book value. If only single items passed, the sole trade has ceased, and clients taken over by the company for nothing raise the hidden-contribution question (lu-corporate-tax). [I]

## 3. Single items for shares: an exchange (situations B and C)

- She does not have to sell anything: the deed stays a contribution. The law only counts the exchange as a sale to measure her gain. [C · S3 art. 22(5); I for equipment]
- No article names a business asset contributed to a company for shares outside art. 59. It is an **exchange**: a sale of the item given, followed by a purchase of the shares; the sale price is the item's **estimated sale value** – the price of a normal, freely agreed sale, so the second-hand market price. [C · S3 arts 22(5), 27(2); S85; I for equipment]
- **Gain = estimated sale value − book value.** [I · S3 arts 18, 22(5)]
- While the sole trade goes on (B): ordinary profit of the year – normal rates, no allowance, no half rate, and inside the municipal business tax base of a commercial or craft business. [I]
- Items fully depreciated or written off under the €870 rule have a book value of nil: their whole value is gain. [I · S3 art. 34]
- The figure in the deed does not bind the ACD: the gain is measured on the real value. Use the second-hand price and keep the proof. [I]
- **Receipts-and-expenses method:** the same result – the cost not yet depreciated is deducted when the item leaves the business, and the value received in shares is added. The pattern of car-and-travel.md §11 (sale, trade-in) applies. [C · S61, S21; I for the receipt in kind]
- A **sale** to the company is taxed the same way: gain = price − book value. [I]

## 4. The sole trade stops (situation C) – and the art. 59 contribution (situation A)

- Stopping a business is a **cessation**; the financial year ends at that moment and the profit up to that day is taxed as usual. [C · S3 arts 15, 17] Liberal professions: the same through art. 92. [C · S3]
- **Cessation gain** = what she receives for what she transfers + the estimated sale value of what she takes into private property − costs − the book value of the net business assets. Equipment contributed to the company counts at its estimated sale value. [C · S3 art. 55; I for the contribution]
- **Allowance:** €10,000 for a whole business (a proportional share for an autonomous part); €25,000 when the gain includes a gain on a building, the extra capped at that gain. **Rate:** half the global rate, without a request. Same in 2025 and 2026. [C · S3 arts 130, 131, 132; S12; S88]
- Outside municipal business tax – the ACD says so for the contribution gain « parce qu'il s'agit … d'un bénéfice de cession au sens de l'article 15 ». [C · S82 point 6.1.2; I for a cessation gain]

When the business is contributed under art. 59 (situation A): [C · S3 art. 59(3)–(4); S82 point 6.1]

| The company takes the business over at | Her gain | €10,000 allowance | Half rate | Municipal business tax |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Book value | None | – | – | – |
| A value between book and operating value | That value − book value | **No** | Yes | No |
| Full operating value, goodwill included | All hidden reserves | Yes | Yes | No |

- **Receipts-and-expenses method:** the cessation is treated as a switch to the balance-sheet method just before; the addition goes onto the cessation gain and gets the half rate, but the allowance cannot exceed the cessation gain proper (profit-methods.md). [C · S61; S21]
- **Last return:** Form 100 for the year the business stops. Commercial or craft: Form 110 (line 9 « Cession ou cessation de l'entreprise »; section IV, from line 69) and Form 100 boxes 521–524. Liberal profession: Form 100 boxes 617–620, with Form 152 or 112 for the expenses up to the last day. The page for extraordinary income carries the art. 131 rates. [C · S20, S23; I for the choice of annex]

## 5. The shares she receives

| How the equipment went in | Cost of her shares | How the shares are taxed |
|---|---|---|
| Art. 59, at book value | Book value of what was contributed | Like the net assets of a business (art. 59(6)) |
| Art. 59, between book and operating value | That value | Like the net assets of a business (art. 59(6)) |
| Art. 59, at full operating value | Operating value | Ordinary private shares (art. 100) |
| Single items (exchange) | Estimated sale value of the items | Private once the sole trade has stopped (art. 100) |

[C · S3 arts 59(5)–(6), 25; S82 points 7–8]

- **Private shares, sold later:** with more than 10% held at any time in the five years before (her own, her spouse's or partner's and her minor children's shares together) the gain is taxable – within six months as a speculation gain at normal rates; later under art. 100, with the ten-year allowance of art. 130(4) and half the global rate (figures: lu-income-tax-personal home-and-rental.md). Liquidating the company counts as a sale. [C · S3 arts 99bis, 100, 101, 130(4), 131] Details: lu-income-tax-personal.
- **Art. 59(6) shares** (business contributed below operating value): a sale or the company's liquidation gives a business cession gain – the business allowance of section 4, not the larger art. 100 allowance – at half the global rate; dividends stay capital income. She can end the regime by declaring that she moves the shares to her private assets, which is taxed like a withdrawal at their value. [C · S3 art. 59(6); S82; S87]
- **While the sole trade goes on**, shares received for a business asset first replace it in the business; they stay business assets only if they serve the business, and never for a liberal profession. If they leave, they leave at their value – no second gain. [I · S3 arts 19, 93]

## 6. Selling the equipment to the company, or keeping it and renting it out

- **Sale:** gain as in section 3. With regular accounts, and at a cessation, the gain is taxed at the sale whether or not the price is paid. Under the receipts-and-expenses method a price counts when collected; leaving it as a loan to her own company is, in our reading, a collection followed by a loan. [I · S61, S21]
- **Keeping and renting, the sole trade has stopped:** the equipment leaves the business at the cessation, taxed at its estimated sale value; the rent is then rental income – that category covers movable goods. [C · S3 arts 55, 98; I for the sequence]
- **The ACD's split-business doctrine** (« scission d'entreprise »): where the same persons control the company and let it use **essential** assets, the letting is itself a commercial business – no cessation, the hidden reserves carry on, the rented assets and her shares in the company are business assets, the rent is commercial profit with municipal business tax. The circular names « l'installation commerciale ou des immeubles servant à l'exploitation » and mentions sole traders who contributed their business except the building. [C · S84] Ordinary office or IT equipment is probably not essential; a fitted workshop or specialised machines could be. Whether the ACD applies the doctrine to movable equipment is not confirmed. [I · S83; IT-O-31]
- The price or rent must be the market one: more is a hidden profit distribution, less a hidden contribution (lu-corporate-tax assets-from-the-founder.md).

## 7. Worked example (invented figures)

Equipment bought for €10,000, book value €4,000, estimated sale value €6,000, contributed for shares at €6,000. No other business assets, no costs.

| Case | Her result | Cost of her shares |
|---|---|---|
| B. The sole trade goes on; single items | Gain 6,000 − 4,000 = **€2,000**, ordinary profit; inside the municipal business tax base if commercial | €6,000 |
| C. The sole trade stops; single items | Cessation gain €2,000, below the allowance → **taxable €0**. Had the gain been €15,000: €5,000 at half the global rate | €6,000 |
| A. Art. 59, the company takes over at book value | **No gain** | €4,000; taxed like business assets |
| A. Art. 59, the company takes €6,000 while the operating value with goodwill is €9,000 | Gain €2,000, **no allowance**, half the global rate | €6,000; taxed like business assets |
| A. Art. 59, €6,000 is the full operating value | Gain €2,000 less allowance → **taxable €0** | €6,000; private |

Had the equipment been fully written off (book value nil), the gain would be €6,000 in every case except the roll-over at book value. [I – our arithmetic on S3 and S82, example 1]

## 8. What is not settled

Equipment only in the deed while the company carries on the activity (IT-O-30); the split-business doctrine for rented movable equipment (IT-O-31).

# Depreciation, the €870 rule and the investment tax credit

_Figures in this file: 2025–2026. The year files in `figures/` win if they differ._

## Depreciation

| Rule | Status |
|---|---|
| Depreciation covers technical and economic loss of value; total depreciation never exceeds cost less estimated residual value (art. 29) | [C · S3] |
| Default: straight-line over the remaining useful life (art. 32(1)) | [C · S3] |
| First year: in principle pro rata for the time left until the year end; the ACD also accepts a full year for assets acquired in the first half of the financial year and half a year for the second half (circular L.I.R. n° 61). The period starts at delivery, when the business can dispose of the asset – it need not be in use yet (circular 34/1). Whether pro rata counts in months or days: IT-O-20 | [C · S58, S7] |
| Declining balance: only for tangible assets other than buildings, used by the owner; max 3× the straight-line rate and max 30%. Switching from declining to straight-line is allowed, not the reverse (art. 32(3), (5)) | [C · S3] |
| Useful life is set by the business according to practice in its sector, not by law. Guichet's examples: industrial building 20 years, office furniture 10 years, vehicles 4 years (a car: car-and-travel.md §9) | [C · S1] |
| The yearly depreciation must be booked every year, or it is lost [C · S1] – unless the business asks, with its tax return, for the art. 32(1a) method: depreciation on cost, at most the yearly amount plus what was not deducted in earlier years, and the rest in full in the last year of useful life. It postpones depreciation, never speeds it up; Form 100 page 18, boxes 1801–1806, with a tax balance sheet showing the undeducted amount | [C · lu-income-tax-personal S2 art. 32(1a); lu-income-tax-personal S1 p. 18] |
| Land is not depreciable; the asset must be owned by the business | [C · S1] |
| Schedule: Form 113 (layout: forms/2025/form-113-and-800.md), feeding Form 110 line 42 (route B) or 56 (route C), or Form 152 line 58 | [C · S4, S20, S53] |
| Depreciation also applies on the simplified (cash) method | [C · S4, S24] |

## The €870 immediate write-off

- An asset costing up to €870 (originally 35,000 LUF) may be written off in full in the year of acquisition (art. 34 LIR). [C · S7, S8]
- Only for the owner who uses the asset. [C · S7] Assets with a usual life of one year or less may also be written off in full in the year of purchase, whoever uses them. [C · lu-income-tax-personal S2 art. 34]
- VAT is **included** in the cost when it is not deductible (e.g. under the VAT franchise) and excluded when it is. [C · S7]
- Not for assets acquired as part of a business transfer. [C · S7]
- Line: Form 152 line 42, Form 112 line 46 (« nouvelles acquisitions … dont le prix d'acquisition ne dépasse pas 870 EUR »), with a detail annex. [C · S4, S5]
- Message for users: the €870 rule concerns eligible immediate write-offs; it is **not** a maximum deductible purchase price. Dearer assets are depreciated over several years. [I]

## Investment tax credit (art. 152bis LIR, Form 800)

Reduces the income tax itself; it is separate from expenses and depreciation. Rates below are confirmed on the 2025 Form 800 and the ACD page. [C · S33, S31] Rates for assets bought before 2024: figures/history-2023-2024.md. Proposed 2027 changes: figures/2027.md.

| Point | Rule |
|---|---|
| Who | Commercial, industrial, mining and craft businesses (art. 14), including sole traders. Liberal-profession profit (art. 91) is outside the scheme |
| Part I – digital or ecological/energy transformation project | Certified project. Software, patents, licences, specialist project advice, dedicated staff and training: generally 18%. Tangible project assets: 6%, generally plus the 12% general credit. Needs an eligibility application (only costs after it count) and a yearly Ministry certificate, requested within two months of the financial year end |
| Part II – general credit | Usually 12%; 14% for assets approved for special depreciation |
| Main exclusions | Assets depreciated over less than three years; assets acquired with the transfer en bloc of a business, an autonomous part or a fraction of a business; used assets; motor vehicles (art. 152bis(7)(2)) [C · S3] |
| Vehicles that do qualify for the 12% credit | Only: passenger-transport businesses and car-rental firms; vehicles used only to carry goods (the ACD names contractors' trucks and traders' or craftsmen's delivery vans); staff buses with at least 9 seats; breakdown trucks; self-propelled machines; zero-emission passenger cars (electric or hydrogen only, M1, at most 9 seats) first registered after 31 Dec 2017, on the first €50,000 of the price. No vehicle qualifies for the digital or ecological credit (art. 152bis(4)) [C · S3; S59] |
| Start-ups | Used assets, and assets taken over en bloc with a business, do count when invested in the first three years after the business is first set up (« premier établissement »), on at most €250,000 of their total price (art. 152bis(7a)). Not a first establishment: a business, part or fraction acquired en bloc against a participation given to the transferor, or from a transferor who holds a participation in the new business – so not a sole trader's business moved into her own company (§7a(3); lu-corporate-tax assets-from-the-founder.md) [C · S3] |
| Software | Separate restrictions, including a cap of 10% of the tax due; different carry-forward rules |
| Routine purchases | Replacing a computer or paying a subscription does not by itself make a "transformation project" |

The older rates (8% / 2%, complementary 13%) applied only until tax year 2023.

### Typical case: a phone or laptop bought for the business

The full path for equipment (write-off, depreciation, credit, VAT, private use, software): [equipment.md](equipment.md).

A commercial sole trader buys a new phone for €1,200 without VAT (invented figures), records it as a business asset and depreciates it over 3 years (€400 a year, Form 113). Investment tax credit: 12% × €1,200 = €144, on Form 800 part II (general credit, « biens amortissables corporels »), carried to the return; it reduces the tax of the year the financial year closes, and what cannot be used is carried forward up to 10 years. [C · S3 art. 152bis §7, §8; S22 for the form]

Conditions to check, all from art. 152bis [C · S3]:
- **Commercial or craft business** (art. 14): a liberal profession gets no credit.
- **New**: a used phone counts only in the first three years after the business is first set up (§7a).
- **A usual useful life of at least 3 years**: « les biens amortissables au cours d'une période inférieure à 3 années » are excluded – the ACD reads it as assets « amortissables normalement » in less than three years. [C · S3, S31] The business sets the useful life by the practice of its sector; a phone depreciated over 2 years gets nothing.
- **Used in a Luxembourg establishment** and physically within Luxembourg or the EEA (§1).
- **The price**: without VAT when the VAT is deductible, with VAT under the franchise (the cost rule of the €870 section). [I]
- **An item written off at once under the €870 rule**: probably still eligible when its usual useful life is three years or more – the write-off is an option « sans égard à leur durée usuelle d'utilisation » (circular 34/1) and does not shorten that life. [I · S7, S31] For no risk, depreciate it over its useful life in the year of the credit. Not confirmed by the ACD: IT-O-26.
- **A business asset also used privately** (phone, laptop): the credit is on the full price; the private use is taken out through the private share of depreciation and costs (Form 113). [I · S3, S33, S53] Not confirmed by the ACD: IT-O-25.

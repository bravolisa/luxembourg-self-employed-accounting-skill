# Dividends and withholding tax

_Figures in this file: 2025–2026. The year files in `figures/` win if they differ._

## The company's side

- Dividends paid by a Luxembourg company bear **15% withholding tax** (*retenue à la source sur les revenus de capitaux*; *Kapitalertragsteuer*) (arts 146, 148 LIR). [C · S8]
- The company declares and pays it **within 8 days** of making the dividend available, and is liable for tax it withheld or should have withheld (art. 149). [C · S8]
- No withholding when the shareholder is a qualifying company (e.g. a fully taxable Luxembourg capital company) holding at least 10% or a participation that cost at least €1.2 million, for at least 12 months (art. 147 n° 2). [C · S8]
- **Participation exemption** (basics only): dividends a fully taxable company receives from such a participation, held or committed to be held for at least 12 months, are exempt from IRC (art. 166). [C · S8]
- Advantages a shareholder receives only because he is a shareholder are **hidden profit distributions**: they are treated as dividends and bear the dividend withholding (circular L.I.R. n° 104/1, point 6.2). [C · S27]
- Pitfall: an old ACD page on the parent-subsidiary regime (2017) still says 20%; the law says 15%. [I · S26 outdated; S8]

## The shareholder's side (resident individual)

- **50%** of dividends from a fully taxable resident capital company (or a comparable treaty or EU company) is exempt (art. 115 n° 15a). [C · S8]
- On top, a yearly allowance exempts part of the capital income taxed by assessment, doubled for a couple taxed jointly (art. 115 n° 15; amount: lu-income-tax-personal, income-categories.md). [C · S8]
- The 15% withheld is an advance credited against income tax in the assessment (art. 154(1) n° 2). [C · S25]
- A resident must file an income tax return when more than €1,500 of taxable income is investment income that bore the 15% withholding (art. 153(1) n° 3). [C · S8]
- Where this goes on Form 100: lu-income-tax-personal, income-categories.md.

## Worked example (invented figures)

A SARL pays its single shareholder a €20,000 dividend from taxed profit: the company withholds €3,000 and pays it within 8 days. In the shareholder's return: €20,000 × 50% = €10,000, minus €1,500 = €8,500 taxable at the personal rate; the €3,000 withheld is credited. [I · our arithmetic on S8]

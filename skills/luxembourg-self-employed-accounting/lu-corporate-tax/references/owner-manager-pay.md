# Paying the owner-manager: salary, directors' fees or dividends

_Figures in this file: 2025–2026. The year files in `figures/` win if they differ._

Which social-security status a company manager has is a separate question (lu-social-security, company-managers.md). This file covers tax only.

## Salary for running the company

- Pay for **actual day-to-day management** is not a directors' fee but **employment income** (art. 95 LIR): wage withholding applies through the payroll (how and when: payroll-withholding.md). [C · S24]
- For the company it is a **deductible** expense: the ACD excludes from deduction only directors' pay that is not for day-to-day management. [C · S19; I · S8 art. 45(1)]
- It must be at arm's length: an excessive salary or other advantage beyond what an unrelated manager would get is a **hidden profit distribution** – added back to the company's profit and taxed as a dividend for the shareholder (art. 164(3)). [I · S8] The Cour administrative's test: an advantage with no real consideration that the shareholder would not have got without being one – « La situation concernée est celle où un gestionnaire prudent et avisé n'aurait pas accordé un avantage similaire à un tiers ». The tax office must first show a set of circumstances that make a distribution probable; the company must then prove the payment is economically justified. [C · S34, S35] What counts as a "normal" salary in figures: open point CT-O-07.
- Example (first instance, 2023 – not binding in other cases): a sports car leased by a SARL and used privately by its sole shareholder-manager, with a logbook the court found unreliable, was a hidden distribution; the private share of the car costs bore 15% withholding tax on capital income. [I · S36]
- A benefit in kind to a shareholder-employee counts as salary only if employees who are not shareholders would get similar benefits for the same work. [C · S27 point 6.1] Whether the flat-rate valuations (e.g. for a company car) apply to dominant shareholders who run the company: open point CT-O-01. Since 2017 the company-car flat rate is set by the RGD of 23 December 2016 (art. 104(3) LIR), which names no exclusion for shareholders and is « subsidiaire » to the general valuation of art. 104(2). [C · S33]

## Directors' fees (tantièmes, jetons de présence)

- The pay of directors, auditors and similar functions of an SA, SARL or other company is taxed as **liberal-profession income** – insofar as it is not pay for day-to-day management (art. 91(1) n° 2). [C · S8]
- **Not deductible** for the company. [C · S24]
- **Withholding**: 20% of the gross fee; 25% of the net amount when the company bears the tax (art. 152). [C · S8] The withholding return (form 510bis) is filed electronically on MyGuichet since 1 January 2025. [C · S24]
- For a resident, the 20% is final unless an assessment is triggered (for instance more than €1,500 of fees) or requested. [C · S24] <!-- separate fact -->
- ACD worked example for 2025 (Luxembourg City company, resident in class 2): total burden on a directors' fee **48.14%** – company 23.87% (not deductible) + 20% withholding + 4.27% personal tax. [C · S24]
- On Form 100 directors' fees go on page 6, line E (lu-income-tax-personal, forms/2025/form-100.md).

## Dividends

Paid out of profit already taxed at company level; 15% withholding; 50% exempt for the resident shareholder plus the yearly capital-income allowance – dividends-and-withholding.md.

## How to answer "what should I pay myself?"

1. Salary for the work actually done in the company is the normal route: deductible for the company, taxed as wages. [C · S24; I]
2. Directors' fees cost the most in total because the company cannot deduct them. [C · S24]
3. Dividends are taxed twice (company, then 50% at home), but the 50% exemption and the yearly allowance soften it. [C · S8]
4. Social security changes the comparison and is not settled here (lu-social-security, company-managers.md): the final mix is a question for an adviser. This is general information, not a tax opinion.

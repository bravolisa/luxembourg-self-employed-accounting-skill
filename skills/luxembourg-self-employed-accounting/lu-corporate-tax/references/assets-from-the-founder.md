# Equipment the founder brings into the company

_Figures in this file: 2025–2026. The year files in `figures/` win if they differ._

A new SARL or SARL-S receives equipment from its founder – as share capital (contribution in kind), by purchase, or with her whole business. This file is the **company's** tax side. Her own income tax: lu-income-tax-business contribution-to-a-company.md. VAT: lu-vat contribution-to-a-company.md. Company law: lu-entity-profiles contribution-in-kind.md. Much of this is our reasoning from the law, not a published ACD position.

## 1. At which value the company enters the equipment

| How it came in | Tax value | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Her business (or an autonomous part) contributed under art. 59 | Any value the company chooses from her book value up to the operating value; that value is its acquisition price | [C · S8 arts 59, 35; lu-income-tax-business S82] |
| Single items contributed for shares | The contribution value in the deed, as long as it does not exceed the operating value | [I · S8 arts 35, 43, 40] |
| Bought from her | The price, if it is the market price | [I · S8] |

- Operating value: what a buyer of the whole business would pay for the item, assuming he carries on. [C · S8 art. 27] For ordinary used equipment that is close to the second-hand price. [I]
- No article names single items contributed in kind to a company. The rules that fit: at the creation of a business, assets not bought for it cannot be entered above their operating value; a later contribution enters at operating value. These rules apply to companies. [C · S8 arts 35, 43, 162; I for the application]

## 2. Depreciation and the immediate write-off of small items

- Depreciation runs on that value over the **useful life still to run**, judged by the kind of item and its use; there is no official table. [C · S8 arts 29, 32; lu-income-tax-business depreciation-and-investment-credit.md]
- The acquisition date is the contribution date – except after a take-over at book value under art. 59, where the company keeps her dates and, in our reading, simply continues her depreciation. [C · S8 art. 59(3a); I]
- An item whose contribution value does not exceed the low-value limit of art. 34 (figure: lu-income-tax-business depreciation-and-investment-credit.md) can be written off at once – but not items acquired with the transfer of a business or an autonomous part, so not after an art. 59 contribution. [C · S8 art. 34; I that the contribution value is the acquisition price]

## 3. Investment tax credit (12%)

Excluded in general: assets depreciable in under three years; assets acquired with the transfer en bloc of a business, an autonomous part or a fraction of one; used assets acquired otherwise; most motor vehicles. [C · S8 art. 152bis §7]

The start-up exception (§7a) lifts the second and third exclusions for assets invested in a first establishment, in its first three years, on at most €250,000. But it is **not** a first establishment when a business, part or fraction is acquired en bloc against a participation given to the transferor, or from a transferor who holds a participation in the new business. [C · S8 art. 152bis §7a; lu-income-tax-business S31]

| Case | Credit | Status |
|---|---|---|
| She contributes her whole business (or an autonomous part) for shares | **No** | [C · S8 §7(2), §7a(3)] |
| She contributes single used items, not a business | Probably yes in the company's first three years: 12% of the contribution value, if the remaining useful life is three years or more and the item is not an excluded vehicle. Not confirmed – the ACD could see the founder's whole set of equipment as a « fraction d'entreprise » acquired en bloc | [I · CT-O-09] |
| The company buys new equipment itself | Yes, under the general rules | [C · S8] |

## 4. A value that is too high or too low

- Dealings between a company and its partner must be balanced as between independent parties. [C · S49]
- **Price too high on a sale to the company, or rent too high:** the excess is a hidden profit distribution – not a cost of the company, dividend income for her (corporate-income-tax.md, dividends-and-withholding.md). [C · S8 art. 164(3); S49; I for the application]
- **Contribution value too high:** the company cannot enter the equipment above its operating value, so the excess gives no depreciation; her own gain is measured on the real value. Nothing leaves the company, so with a sole partner this is not by itself a hidden distribution. [I] Company-law liability for the value: lu-entity-profiles.
- **Value too low** (contribution or sale below the real value): the difference is a hidden contribution (« apport caché ») – a contribution made outside any change of the subscribed capital, an advantage granted because of the shareholding, in the words of the ACD's director. [C · S50] By reasoning: the company enters the equipment at operating value and the difference is a contribution, not profit; she is taxed as if she had disposed of it at its real value; her share cost rises by the same amount. No ACD circular spells this out for equipment. [I]
- Practical rule: use the real second-hand value and keep the proof.

## 5. Worked example (invented figures)

Machines worth €6,000 second-hand (her book value €4,000) are contributed as single items to a new SARL-S at €6,000. The company enters them at €6,000 and, with three years of useful life left, deducts €2,000 a year. If the credit is accepted (CT-O-09): 12% × €6,000 = €720. Had her whole business been contributed at book value under art. 59, the company would enter €4,000, continue her depreciation and get no credit. [I – our arithmetic on S8]

# Open points – lu-corporate-tax

Answer an open point by saying it is not settled, giving the working answer with its label, and naming who can settle it.

## CT-O-01 · Flat-rate benefit valuation for shareholder-managers
- Question: Circular L.I.R. n° 104/1, point 6.3, on flat-rate valuations of benefits (e.g. a company car) for dominant shareholders who run the company, reads « ne sont applicables » without « pas ». Do the flat rates apply to them or not?
- Why it matters: Company car or flat of a SARL owner-manager: flat rate or actual cost.
- Working answer: The flat-rate rules of the circular do not apply to them (the « pas » is probably missing – both the 2009 and 2018 versions read « ne sont applicables »); value at actual cost. [I · S27] The car flat rate now sits in the RGD of 23 December 2016, which has no shareholder exclusion. [C · S33]
- Who can settle: ACD.
- Status: open

## CT-O-03 · Minimum net wealth tax for an ASBL
- Question: Does an ASBL (exempt, or taxable only on a commercial activity) pay the minimum net wealth tax?
- Why it matters: Fixed yearly cost for small associations.
- Working answer: A fully exempt ASBL (§ 3(1) n° 6 VStG, no economic activity) pays no net wealth tax and no minimum (asbl-taxation.md). [C · S32] Open: an exempt ASBL with an economic activity, taxed only on the assets of that activity – does the minimum apply? [O]
- Who can settle: ACD.
- Status: open

## CT-O-04 · Form 500 for a fully exempt ASBL
- Question: Must an ASBL with no commercial income file Form 500 every year?
- Why it matters: ASBL compliance calendar.
- Working answer: File when the ACD asks or when the ASBL has commercial income; confirm with the tax office. [I · S6]
- Who can settle: ACD.
- Status: open

## CT-O-05 · First minimum net wealth tax of a new company
- Question: For a company whose first financial year has not closed before its first 1 January key date, which balance-sheet total sets the minimum net wealth tax?
- Why it matters: Start-ups formed mid-year with a long first financial year.
- Working answer: Liability starts at the first 1 January after formation [C · S32 point 3], and the minimum uses the last closing balance sheet of the tax year before the key date [C · S2]. A first financial year with no closing before that key date is not addressed – say it is open. [O]
- Who can settle: ACD.
- Status: open

## CT-O-06 · Consolidated net wealth tax and municipal business tax laws
- Question: Is the GewStG § 2–3 (municipal business tax: who pays, exemptions) still worded as in the original 1936 text?
- Why it matters: The ASBL exemptions rest on those originals.
- Working answer: The original GewStG matches the ACD's summaries. [I · S14] For net wealth tax the question is settled: § 8(2) VStG was replaced by the law of 20 December 2024 [C · S10], and the ACD's 2026 circular restates the current § 3(1) n° 6 exemption [C · S32].
- Who can settle: BravoLisa (human check) with its legal adviser; Legilux has no consolidated version.
- Status: open

## CT-O-07 · Arm's-length salary of a shareholder-manager
- Question: What salary of a shareholder-manager does the ACD treat as normal, before part of it becomes a hidden profit distribution?
- Why it matters: Risk of reclassification as a dividend.
- Working answer: Compare with what an unrelated manager would be paid for the same work: the Cour administrative's test is whether « un gestionnaire prudent et avisé » would have granted the same to a third party, and the tax office must first show circumstances that make a hidden distribution probable. [C · S34, S35] No ACD salary figure exists. [I · S8 art. 164(3)]
- Who can settle: ACD; an adviser for a given case.
- Status: open

## CT-O-08 · Company paying the manager's own CCSS contributions
- Question: A self-employed manager's contributions are his or her own debt. If the company pays them, are they extra salary (with withholding) or a deductible company cost?
- Why it matters: Small SARLs often pay the manager's CCSS invoice from the company account.
- Working answer: The manager's CCSS contributions are the manager's own debt and in principle a private expense, not a cost of the company. [C · lu-social-security S28 arts 32, 240 for the debt; I for the tax reading] If the company pays them, either book the payment to the manager's current account (the manager repays, no tax effect), or treat it as part of the manager's gross pay: deductible for the company, taxable salary with withholding for the manager, who deducts the contributions as special expenses. [I · S27 points 2.1.3, 6] If it is neither repaid nor agreed and taxed as pay – or the person gets no pay for managing – it is a hidden profit distribution, not deductible. [I · S27 point 6.2] This is what ACD circular L.I.R. n° 46/2 of 1998 said in so many words; the ACD abrogated it on 26 July 2023 without a reason or a new text, so today the rule rests on the law and circular 104/1, not on a specific circular. [C · S47, S48 for the old text and its abrogation]
- Who can settle: ACD.
- Status: open

## CT-O-09 · Investment tax credit on used equipment contributed by the founder
- Question: Can single used items that a founding partner contributes for shares to her newly formed company – without transferring her business – get the investment tax credit as a first establishment (art. 152bis §7a)?
- Why it matters: 12% of the contribution value in the company's first year.
- Working answer: Probably yes: used assets are lifted from the exclusion in the first three years, and §7a(3) removes only a business, part or fraction acquired en bloc and leased assets. Against: no text defines « premier établissement » or « fraction d'entreprise », and the ACD could treat the founder's whole equipment as acquired en bloc – the situation §7a(3) was written for. Do not count on the credit. [I · S8]
- Who can settle: ACD.
- Status: open

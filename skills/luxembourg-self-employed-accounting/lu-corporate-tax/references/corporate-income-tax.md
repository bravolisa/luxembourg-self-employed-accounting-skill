# Corporate income tax (IRC)

_Figures in this file: 2025–2026. The year files in `figures/` win if they differ._

## Who pays it

- Resident capital companies – including the **SARL** and the **SARL-S** – pay corporate income tax (*impôt sur le revenu des collectivités*, IRC; *Körperschaftsteuer*) (art. 159 LIR). [C · S8]
- All income of a capital company is **commercial profit**, whatever the activity: a consultant's or an architect's SARL earns commercial profit, not liberal-profession income (art. 162(3) LIR). [C · S8]
- ASBLs: asbl-taxation.md.

## Rate (2025 and 2026)

| Taxable income | IRC | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Up to €175,000 | 14% | [C · S8 art. 174(1)] |
| €175,000 – €200,001 | €24,500 + 30% of the part above €175,000 (smooths the step) | [C · S8] |
| Above €200,000 | 16% | [C · S8] |

- The 14% / 16% scale applies from tax year 2025 (law of 20 December 2024). [C · S9, S1]
- On top: the **solidarity surcharge** (*contribution au fonds pour l'emploi*) of **7% of the IRC** – not 7 points: 16% × 7% = 1.12 points. [C · S5]
- Plus **municipal business tax** (municipal-business-tax-companies.md). The ACD's combined rate for a company in Luxembourg City is **23.87%** from 2025 (16% + 1.12% + 6.75%; 24.94% for 2019–2024). [C · S3]
- For taxable income up to €175,000 in Luxembourg City the nominal combined rate is 14% × 1.07 + 6.75% = 21.73%, before the €17,500 ICC allowance, which lowers the effective rate on small profits further. [I · S1, S4]
- **There is no minimum IRC** any more: since the 1 January 2016 net wealth assessment, the minimum net wealth tax has replaced it. [C · S1] A company with no profit still pays the minimum net wealth tax (net-wealth-tax.md).
- Pitfall: Guichet's IRC page (changed 7 April 2026) still shows 15% / 17% – the pre-2025 rates. Use the law. [I · S6 outdated; S8]

## Taxable base

- The tax balance sheet follows the company's commercial balance sheet unless a tax rule sets another value (art. 40(1)) – the IRC starts from the annual accounts. [C · S8]
- IRC, net wealth tax and municipal business tax are themselves **not deductible** for the company (art. 168 n° 2). [C · S8]
- **Hidden profit distributions** (*distributions cachées de bénéfices*; *verdeckte Gewinnausschüttung*): an advantage a shareholder receives from the company that he would not have had without being a shareholder is added back to the taxable income (art. 164(3)). Typical risks for an owner-manager: an excessive salary, a private car or flat at a low price, a loan at no interest. [C · S8; examples I]
- **Losses** of financial years closed after 31 December 2016 can be carried forward for 17 years, oldest first; no carry-back. [C · S18] A company dormant for years and revived only to use its old losses on one profitable deal: a first-instance court held this an abuse of law (§ 6 StAnpG) and refused the losses (tribunal administratif, 30 March 2023). [I · S37] Bill 8800 would remove the 17-year limit – figures/2027.md.

## Worked example (invented figures)

A SARL in Luxembourg City with €80,000 taxable profit in 2025: IRC 14% = €11,200; surcharge 7% of that = €784; ICC (€80,000 − €17,500) × 6.75% = about €4,219; total about €16,200 (about 20%), plus net wealth tax. [I · our arithmetic on S8, S5, S4]

# Net wealth tax (IF) – companies

_Figures in this file: 2025–2026. The year files in `figures/` win if they differ._

## Who pays it

- Net wealth tax (*impôt sur la fortune*, IF; *Vermögensteuer*) for individuals was **abolished from 1 January 2006**: sole traders and partners in transparent partnerships do not pay it. **Companies do.** [C · S16]
- It is assessed on the company's assets less debts on **1 January** of each year (the key date). [C · S15]
- A new company: liability starts with the wealth at the beginning of the next calendar year – a SARL formed during 2025 is first assessed on 1 January 2026 (the ACD's example: formed on 2 January N, first taxed at 1 January N+1). [C · S32 point 3] Which balance-sheet total sets its first minimum tax: open point CT-O-05.

## Ordinary tax

0.5% of taxable net wealth up to €500 million; above that €2.5 million plus 0.05% of the excess. [C · S2]

## Minimum net wealth tax (from tax year 2025)

| Balance-sheet total | Minimum NWT | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Up to €350,000 | €535 | [C · S10] |
| €350,000 – €2,000,000 | €1,605 | [C · S10] |
| Above €2,000,000 | €4,815 | [C · S10] |

- These three brackets apply from tax year 2025 (law of 20 December 2024); the old test on financial assets above 90% and the scale up to €32,100 no longer apply. [C · S10, S2]
- The balance-sheet total used is that of the last closing balance sheet before the key date (31 December 2025 for the tax at 1 January 2026). [C · S2]
- The minimum is **reduced by the IRC plus solidarity surcharge** due for the preceding tax year, after any tax credits – the reduced minimum can be zero [C · S32 points 18, 33]; if what is left is not higher than the ordinary 0.5% tax, the ordinary tax applies – the company pays the higher of the two. [C · S2]
- In practice: a profitable small SARL whose previous-year IRC and surcharge exceed €535 pays no minimum top-up, only the ordinary 0.5%; a **dormant or loss-making SARL or SARL-S pays the full €535** (or €1,605 / €4,815). [I · S2]

## Reducing it with a reserve (§ 8a)

- On request with the IRC return, a company can reduce its NWT by booking a reserve of **5 times the reduction** and keeping it for **5 tax years**. [C · S17]
- The reduction is capped at the IRC plus surcharge (before tax credits) of the preceding tax year – after tax credits if the company chose deferred depreciation (art. 32(1a) LIR). [C · S17; S32 point 32]
- The reserve cannot take the tax below the minimum that **would be due** – the minimum after its own reduction by last year's IRC. That reduced minimum can be zero: « il est parfaitement possible qu'après réduction(s), le montant de l'I.F. minimum s'élève à zéro euro ». So a profitable small company can bring its tax to €0 with the reserve, while a company that actually owes the minimum cannot reduce it. [C · S17; S32 point 33]
- If the reserve is used (e.g. distributed) before the 5 years end, the tax is increased by one fifth of the reserve used. [C · S17]

## Payment

Advances on 10 February, 10 May, 10 August, 10 November. [C · S23] Declared on Form 500 (form-500-and-deadlines.md).

## History

Tax years 2017–2024: minimum €4,815 for companies with financial assets above 90% of the balance sheet and €350,000, otherwise a scale from €535 to €32,100. Only for corrections of those years. [C · S2]

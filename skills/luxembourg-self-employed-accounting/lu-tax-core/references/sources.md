# Sources and how to use them

## Order of authority

1. **Written answers from the administration** logged by BravoLisa (ACD, AED, CCSS). They outrank our inference from forms. They are administrative positions, not rulings: an ACD email states « un courriel n'engage pas son expéditeur ».
2. **Law and official forms of the tax year**: Legilux texts, the consolidated income tax law (LIR) and VAT law, the year's official forms (ACD Forms 100/110/112/113/152/800; AED/eCDF VAT returns).
2a. **Court judgments**: the CJEU and the appeal courts (Cour administrative, Cour d'appel, Cour de cassation) show how the law is read and can confirm a rule; a first-instance judgment (tribunal administratif, tribunal d'arrondissement) is one court's reading of one case – an Interpretation until an appeal court confirms it. A judgment binds only its own case: always name the court and the year.
3. **Administrative guidance**: ACD circulars, AED FAQs, Guichet.lu pages, CCSS pages, IGSS social parameters.
4. **BravoLisa wiki** (wiki.bravolisa.lu): plain-language articles for users. Our answers must agree with it; where it is less precise, verify against 2–3.
5. **Chambers, professional bodies and established firms**: Chambre de Commerce, Chambre des Métiers, House of Entrepreneurship, accounting and law firms: they help find the official text; they never confirm a rule on their own.
6. **Anything else** (press, blogs, forums, taxx.lu, AI answers, other skills): unknown until checked against 2–3.

A Confirmed rule always rests on a level 1–2 source; one resting only on a firm, the press or our own documents is an Interpretation at most.

**Practice** (what offices or accountants actually do) is not in this list on purpose: it tells what happens, never what the law requires. Record it with the [P] label and say who reported it and when.

When Luxembourg guidance is silent, how Germany, France or Belgium apply the same EU rule can illustrate a point. Luxembourg law always wins; foreign practice is context, not a source. Watch out for Belgian rules that look similar but do not exist in Luxembourg (for example CO2-based limits on car costs).

## Working rules

- Open the page you take a figure from; a search snippet is not a source.
- Record the date checked next to every source in the skill's `sources.md`.
- French originals are authoritative; English pages and translations are aids.
- Tax rules change every year: a source from another year must be labelled with its year.

## Escalation when the answer matters

- **Asking the ACD** – its contact page, https://impotsdirects.public.lu/fr/support/contact.html, gives the online contact form in French, German or English (https://impotsdirects.public.lu/fr/formulaires/contact-pages.html – « Contact – Personnes physiques / Natural persons » for an individual), the phone line **(+352) 2475 2475**, Monday to Friday 7:45–17:00, and the addresses of the tax offices; it advises calling or writing before going to an office. Fast, short answers to one narrow question (same-day replies have been seen). The ACD says it is not an independent tax adviser, and an e-mail answer does not bind it (see the order of authority above). (ACD contact page, updated 27/04/2026, checked 2026-11-23)
- **Written request to the competent tax office** (bureau d'imposition) for anything load-bearing. A resident's office depends on the commune of residence or the profession: list of the 28 offices for individuals, https://impotsdirects.public.lu/fr/profil/organigramme/impo_pers.html; questions on tax withheld from wages and pensions go to the RTS offices, https://impotsdirects.public.lu/fr/profil/organigramme/rts.html.
- **Advance decision** (décision anticipée) for a binding position on a specific case – procedure in the règlement grand-ducal du 23 décembre 2014 (named on the ACD contact page).
- An open point's record says who can settle it. Write the question about the person's own case: the facts in a line or two, then one short yes/no or either/or question.

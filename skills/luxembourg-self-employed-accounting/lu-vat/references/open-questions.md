# Open points – lu-vat

Answer an open point by saying it is not settled, giving the working answer with its label, and naming who can settle it.

## VAT-O-01 · Capital goods bought under the franchise, when deduction starts
- Question: After leaving the franchise, can part of the VAT on capital goods bought under the franchise be recovered (adjustment of deduction)?
- Why it matters: Can recover VAT on recent equipment for people who switch regime.
- Working answer: Probably yes, by the general rule: the RGD of 3 March 1980 adjusts an initial non-deduction when the right to deduct changes within five years (ten for buildings), in fifths (tenths), except for VAT of €125 or less per asset. [C · S49, S50] The franchise regulation that applied this expressly to franchise moves was repealed from 1 January 2025; whether the AED applies the general rule to leaving the franchise is for it to say. [I] VAT on purchases under the franchise is otherwise not deductible. [I · S1]
- Who can settle: AED (written question).
- Status: open

## VAT-O-02 · Non-EU sales of franchise users
- Question: Does the AED expect services sold to non-EU customers by a franchise user to be reported anywhere?
- Why it matters: Completeness of the simplified return.
- Working answer: There is no box on the simplified return. [C · S2] A franchise user who supplied no services to EU business customers and owes no VAT on purchases (art. 61) files no simplified return at all – only the written turnover notice before 1 March; non-EU sales alone never trigger a return. [C · S24 art. 57bis(4)] Services to **business** customers outside the EU do not count towards €50,000; services to **private** customers outside the EU count unless they are listed in art. 17(2) 8° (consulting, legal, accounting, advertising, data processing…) or are electronic services. [C · S24 arts 17, 57] (Corrected 2026-10-25: the earlier answer said no non-EU sale counts.)
- Who can settle: AED.
- Status: open

## VAT-O-03 · Goods exports of franchise users on the simplified return
- Question: Where, if anywhere, does a franchise user report goods exported outside the EU?
- Why it matters: Exports count towards the €50,000 but the simplified form has no export box.
- Working answer: The export counts towards the threshold. [C · S24 art. 57(2)(b)] Exports alone do not oblige the user to file. [C · S24 art. 57bis(4)] When a simplified return is filed: the eCDF notice describes the franchise turnover line as the total of « toutes les factures établies … sous le régime de la franchise », which points to box 481 for exports too – the notice is outdated and does not name exports. [I · S54]
- Who can settle: AED.
- Status: open

## VAT-O-04 · Crossing purchase under the €10,000 EU-goods threshold
- Question: Is the EU purchase that takes a franchise user over €10,000 itself an intra-Community acquisition? Art. 2(2)(b) reads « n'excède pas, pendant l'année civile en cours au moment de l'acquisition ».
- Why it matters: Changes the VAT on that one invoice.
- Working answer: Treat it as taxable (the cautious choice). [I · S7] The law reads « n'excède pas, pendant l'année civile en cours au moment de l'acquisition » – the crossing purchase itself already exceeds it in our reading; earlier purchases of the year stay untaxed. [I · S24 art. 2(2)(b)] Quoting the VAT number to the supplier also makes the buyer liable for VAT on an acquisition (art. 61(6)). [C · S24]
- Who can settle: AED (written question).
- Status: open

## VAT-O-06 · Which rate applies to which supply
- Question: Rate catalogue (17 / 14 / 8 / 3%), e.g. beauty and cosmetic services versus hairdressing, healthcare.
- Why it matters: Wrong rate on sales invoices.
- Working answer: none – check the law's annexes for the specific supply; an exempt supply is not a 0% supply. [I] Settled case by case in rates.md: courses, conferences and events (3% only for a short « manifestation » with people present – S59).
- Who can settle: VAT law annexes; AED.
- Status: open

## VAT-O-09 · E-invoicing (bill 8815) and franchise or exempt businesses
- Question: Must franchise users and VAT-exempt businesses issue structured e-invoices from 2029?
- Why it matters: Invoicing software choices for small businesses.
- Working answer: On the bill's wording franchise users are in scope for invoices to Luxembourg business customers (issuing from 1 January 2029 for small businesses); exempt supplies without an invoicing duty are outside; everyone must be able to receive from 1 January 2028. The bill does not name the franchise – our reading. [O · S28; I]
- Who can settle: final law; AED / efacturation.public.lu guidance.
- Status: open

## VAT-O-11 · Imports and credit notes around a rate change
- Question: Which rate applies to imports and to credit notes on supplies made before a rate change (1 Jan 2023, 1 Jan 2024)?
- Why it matters: Corrections of 2023 invoices.
- Working answer: Imports: the rate in force when import VAT becomes chargeable (art. 39(1)). [C · S24] Credit notes: the regulation makes the supplier correct « la taxe due » for the supply it made, so the credit note reverses the tax actually charged – the original invoice's rate. [C · S52 for the mechanism; I for the rate]
- Who can settle: AED; circulars 812 / 812-1 are silent.
- Status: open

## VAT-O-12 · Goods a franchise user sells to a business in another EU country – threshold and reporting
- Question: The sale is not an exempt intra-Community supply (settled – franchise.md). Does it count towards the €50,000, and does it go on a recapitulative statement?
- Why it matters: Small webshops and craftspeople selling to businesses abroad – whether they stay under the threshold.
- Working answer: Count it towards the €50,000 – the cautious reading. Art. 57(2) counts intra-EU supplies only « lorsque l'exonération y prévue s'applique » (point c), and other supplies « dans la mesure où elles seraient taxées si elles étaient effectuées par un assujetti ne bénéficiant pas de la franchise » (point a); which applies here is not settled. No recapitulative statement: art. 64bis(1) covers goods supplied « dans les conditions de l'article 43, paragraphe 1er, points d) et f) », and the exemption does not apply. [I · S24 arts 57(2), 64bis(1)]
- Who can settle: AED.
- Status: open

## VAT-O-13 · Who must file AN_TABACAM
- Question: Which businesses must file the eCDF table of depreciable capital acquisitions and depreciation (AN_TABACAM) with the annual VAT return?
- Why it matters: A missing form on the annual return; BravoLisa can file it through eCDF.
- Working answer: None of the CTIE documents says; file it when the business bought depreciable assets in the year and deducts VAT. [I · S43, S44]
- Who can settle: AED.
- Status: open

## VAT-O-15 · Exempt turnover of an art. 44 user on the simplified return
- Question: An art. 44 user who must file the simplified return (after a reverse-charge purchase, say): where does its exempt turnover go, when the form's box 012 may only hold 481 + 482?
- Why it matters: The return is rejected or understates turnover.
- Working answer: Leave 012 at what 481 + 482 give and keep the exempt turnover in the books; do not invent a box. [I · S47]
- Who can settle: AED.
- Status: open

## VAT-O-16 · Distance sales taxed abroad on monthly and quarterly returns
- Question: Monthly and quarterly returns have no box 203. Where does a business report distance sales of goods taxed in another member state each period (art. 64(2))?
- Why it matters: Online sellers above the EU threshold who are not in OSS.
- Working answer: Box 019 (other supplies carried out abroad) on the periodic return; 203 on the annual return. [I]
- Who can settle: AED.
- Status: open

## VAT-O-17 · One activity split between a sole trader and his own company to stay under the franchise
- Question: A franchise user passes €50,000, then forms a SARL or SARL-S he manages and sells the same services through it under its own franchise, aiming to keep each under €50,000. Can the AED treat the two as one taxable activity?
- Why it matters: Each taxable person has its own €50,000 limit; if the AED treats the split as abusive, VAT is due on the combined turnover, with interest and fines.
- Working answer: Separate legal persons each have their own threshold [C · S24 art. 57bis], but splitting one and the same activity (same person, same services, same clients) mainly to stay under the limit is what the EU abuse principle targets (CJEU C-255/02 Halifax); no Luxembourg text or AED guidance naming this case was found. Do not suggest it; genuinely separate activities with their own organisation are a different case. [I]
- Who can settle: AED.
- Status: open

## VAT-O-18 · How EU institutions and international organisations based in Luxembourg claim the VAT exemption
- Question: For sales to an EU institution or an international organisation based in Luxembourg (art. 43(1)(k) ii and iii), does Luxembourg exempt directly on a certificate or refund the institution, and with which minimum amount and which form?
- Why it matters: Direct exemption means an invoice without VAT in box 015; a refund means the seller charges 17% as usual. Getting it wrong leaves VAT unpaid or charged twice.
- Working answer: No Luxembourg regulation or AED circular found; the law points to Protocol No 7 and the headquarters agreements [C · S24 art. 43(1)(k)]. Ask the institution for its exemption certificate or written instructions before invoicing; without them, charge VAT [I].
- Who can settle: AED.
- Status: open

## VAT-O-19 · Art. 44-exempt service sold to an embassy or EU institution: box 016 or box 015?
- Question: A seller whose service is exempt under art. 44 (for example an approved training body) invoices an EU institution or an embassy that holds an exemption certificate. Does the sale stay art. 44 (box 016, no deduction) or become art. 43(1)(k) (box 015, with deduction)?
- Why it matters: Box 015 gives a right to deduct input VAT; box 016 does not and lowers the pro-rata.
- Working answer: Art. 44 – box 016, no deduction: the Commission services say art. 132 exemptions take precedence over art. 151 [I · S62 §3.4.4]. Luxembourg law is silent; art. 43(1)(k), unlike (n), has no art. 44 carve-out [C · S24].
- Who can settle: AED.
- Status: open

## VAT-O-20 · Home-to-office km in the private-use declaration
- Question: For appendix B (private use of a business car), are the self-employed person's own trips between home and a fixed place of business private km or business km?
- Why it matters: It sets the private share on which VAT is paid every year; for income tax these km are kept out of the business share.
- Working answer: Count them as private km – it cannot understate the VAT and it matches the income-tax split. No Luxembourg text decides it; the Court of Justice ruled only for employees (home to a fixed workplace normally serves private needs). [I · S65; O]
- Who can settle: AED.
- Status: open

## VAT-O-21 · Purchase price in the private-use base: book depreciation or a fifth a year
- Question: In appendix B, does the car's purchase price enter the private-use base as the depreciation booked for the year, or as one fifth a year?
- Why it matters: A longer book period lowers the yearly VAT on private use.
- Working answer: The depreciation booked for the year – the form takes it from the car's book values. [C · S3, S76] No text fixes the years; the AED uses five years for cars rented to employees, and five years is the adjustment period, so a much longer period may be questioned. [I – medium · S36, S49]
- Who can settle: AED.
- Status: open

## VAT-O-22 · New car from another EU country bought by a franchise user: return or customs
- Question: Does a franchise user who buys a new car in another EU country pay the Luxembourg VAT through the simplified return or with the declaration 446-L at customs?
- Why it matters: The VAT is due in every case; the route decides where and when it is paid.
- Working answer: Through a VAT return: the regulation sends persons identified for VAT to the return, and the law makes the simplified return compulsory in a year in which a franchise user owes VAT as a buyer. No official page names the case. [I – medium · S48 art. 18; S24 art. 57bis(4)]
- Who can settle: AED.
- Status: open

## VAT-O-23 · Car lease with a purchase option: service or supply of goods
- Question: Is a car lease with a mere purchase option (no automatic transfer of ownership) a service, with VAT on each rent, until the option is used?
- Why it matters: A supply of goods would make the VAT on the whole price due at hand-over.
- Working answer: A service until the option is used: the law treats a hire as a supply of goods only when the contract provides that ownership passes at the latest with the last instalment. The Court of Justice may treat some finance leases as goods (judgment not opened). [I – medium · S24 art. 12(a)]
- Who can settle: AED.
- Status: open

## VAT-O-24 · Charging an electric car: charging billed from abroad, and proof for home charging
- Question: Is charging in Luxembourg billed by a provider not established here reverse-charged to the VAT-identified customer? And what evidence supports the business share of electricity charged at home?
- Why it matters: Foreign charge cards are common; home charging has no invoice of its own.
- Working answer: By the text of the law the customer owes the VAT when electricity is supplied by a supplier not established in Luxembourg – check such invoices. Home charging: the kWh charged × the business-km share, with the electricity invoices. No official text on either. [I – medium · S24 arts 14(1)(f), 61(4)]
- Who can settle: AED.
- Status: open

## VAT-O-25 · Taxable amount of a contribution in kind paid in shares
- Question: When single capital goods are contributed to a company for shares, is the VAT charged on the contribution value stated in the deed?
- Why it matters: It fixes the VAT on every contribution of equipment by a VAT-registered founder.
- Working answer: Yes – the issue value of the shares the parties agreed, which at formation equals the contribution value in the deed (Court of Justice, C-241/23); the open-market value only in the three cases of art. 28(3). The VAT comes on top of that value. No AED circular or page on contributions was found. [C · S89, S24; I for Luxembourg practice]
- Who can settle: AED.
- Status: open

## VAT-O-26 · A freelancer's equipment contributed while the clients follow informally: universality or single items
- Question: A sole trader stops and contributes all her equipment to her new company; clients and contracts are not transferred in writing but in fact follow her. Is that a transfer of a universality (no VAT) or a taxable supply of single items?
- Why it matters: VAT shown on a universality is owed and not deductible; VAT not charged on single items is assessed later.
- Working answer: Not settled. The Court of Justice asks whether the assets passing are sufficient to carry on an independent activity, on all the facts (Zita Modes, Schriever); no text or judgment we opened decides it for a one-person service business, or says whether staff must pass. Charging VAT on single items is the less risky mistake when the company deducts in full. [I · S92, S93]
- Who can settle: AED.
- Status: open

## VAT-O-27 · Deducting VAT on an invoice dated before the company has its VAT number
- Question: Can a new company deduct, in its first return, the VAT on an invoice it received before its VAT number was issued?
- Why it matters: The founder's invoice for contributed equipment is usually dated at formation, before the company is registered for VAT.
- Working answer: Yes: the right to deduct arises with the chargeable tax and an invoice (art. 48); the customer's number is not a compulsory mention on a domestic invoice (art. 63(8)); the Court of Justice treats registration as a formality (Polski Trawertyn); the AED's legal bulletin n° 11 notes that VAT on expenses for a planned taxed activity is deductible. Whether a regulation under art. 54(1)(b) limits this was not checked. [I · S24, S97, S101]
- Who can settle: AED.
- Status: open

## VAT-O-28 · Goods kept when the business stops: which box, and current value or remaining fifths
- Question: Is the deemed supply of goods held at cessation (art. 13(c)) declared in box 008 of the annual return, and is it taxed on the current value of the goods or by repaying the remaining fifths of the VAT deducted?
- Why it matters: Every business that stops with equipment or a car on which VAT was deducted.
- Working answer: Current value of similar goods (arts 13(c), 28(1)(b)), also after the five years (Mateusiak), in box 008 – whose label names art. 13(a) only. Guichet's example repays the remaining fifths instead; the law prevails. [C · S24, S70 for the rule; I for the box; S103 conflicting]
- Who can settle: AED.
- Status: open

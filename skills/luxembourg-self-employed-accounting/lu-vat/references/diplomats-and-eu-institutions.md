# Embassies, EU institutions and international organisations (art. 43(1)(k))

_Figures in this file: 2025–2026. The year files in `figures/` win if they differ._

Sales to embassies and consulates, to EU institutions (for example the European Investment Bank or the EU bodies based in Luxembourg) and to recognised international organisations can be exempt from VAT. It is an exemption **with** the right to deduct, but only when the paperwork is in order. Most freelancers meet it when an embassy or an EU body asks for an invoice "without VAT".

## The exemption

- Art. 43(1)(k) exempts supplies of goods and services (i) « effectuées dans le cadre des relations diplomatiques et consulaires »; (ii) to the EU, Euratom, the ECB, the EIB and EU bodies covered by Protocol No 7 on privileges and immunities, « dans les limites et conditions fixées par ce protocole » and the headquarters agreements; (iii) to other international organisations recognised by the host state, and to their members, within the limits of their founding conventions or headquarters agreements. Further points cover armed forces (iv–vii). [C · S24 art. 43(1)(k)]
- **Deduction kept:** VAT on purchases used for these sales stays deductible (art. 49(2)(a)), and the sales give a right to deduct in the pro-rata (normal-regime.md). [C · S24 art. 49(2)(a)]
- **Return:** box **015** « Other exemptions (art. 43 and 60bis) » – not box 016, which is for art. 44. [C · S3]
- Art. 60bis in the same box label is a different thing (the special suspensive regime for goods). [C · S24 art. 60bis]

## When the seller's activity is already exempt (art. 44)

A doctor, a therapist, a school-level tutor or an approved training body whose service is exempt under art. 44 stays under **art. 44** when the customer is an embassy or an EU institution: **box 016, no deduction**, and the sale counts in the pro-rata denominator. [I · S62 §3.4.4] Why it is only [I]:

- The Luxembourg law does not say which exemption wins. Art. 43(1)(k) has no art. 44 carve-out, while art. 43(1)(n) has one (« à l'exception de celles visées à l'article 44 »). [C · S24 art. 43(1)(k), (n)]
- The European Commission services read the Directive the other way: art. 132 exemptions (art. 44 in Luxembourg) « take precedence over those in Article 151 », and « when the transaction is exempted without right to deduct when supplied to other persons, it cannot give right to deduct when supplied to bodies included in Article 151 ». They rely on the Court of Justice in Eurodental (C-240/05), where a specific exemption took precedence over a general cross-border one. [I · S62 §3.4.4] The paper is not binding, and Eurodental concerned intra-EU supplies, not art. 151.

Open: VAT-O-19. Most freelancers are not art. 44 at all (IT, consulting, coaching, design; exempt-art44.md): for them the question does not arise and art. 43(1)(k) applies when its conditions are met. A trainer is art. 44 only as an approved or authorised training body, or for personal lessons at school or university level. [C · S24 art. 44(1); S59]

## Embassies and consulates in Luxembourg – the conditions

The RGD of 7 February 2013 sets the procedure. [C · S61]

| | Official use of the mission (art. 2) | Personal use of a diplomat (art. 4) |
|---|---|---|
| What is exempt | Water, gas, electricity, heating, fuel: any amount. Everything else **at least €120 excl. VAT each**: food, drink and catering for official receptions at the mission or the head's residence; building work on the mission or the official residence; all other goods and services for official use | Goods and services of **at least €240 excl. VAT each**, up to **€10,000 excl. VAT a calendar year** (€40,000 from arrival to the end of the next year); not food, drink, tobacco, vehicles (cars have their own rule), building work, restaurants or accommodation |
| Certificate (arts 5–7) | Held by the seller before the supply: on the AED form, in three copies, sealed by the mission, with the price excl. VAT, the official-use declaration and the AED's conformity attestation (or a reference to a dispensation, art. 9) | Same, with the personal-use declaration and the head of mission's confirmation of the person's status |

- **Order of events:** the mission sends the certificate and a purchase order to the Foreign Ministry, which visas it and passes it to the AED for its check – all **before** the supply. [C · S61 art. 7(2)]
- **Invoice:** compulsory, with the mention « Exonération de la TVA – régime diplomatique suivant certificat du … ». [C · S61 art. 7(3)]
- **Thresholds:** a continuous service under a subscription or rental contract counts as one operation; grouping several invoices, or separate supplies on one invoice, to reach €120 or €240 is not allowed. [C · S61 art. 11]
- **Reciprocity:** the exemption applies only if the foreign state grants Luxembourg's mission the same. [C · S61 art. 10] The seller cannot check that; in practice the AED's attestation on the certificate covers it. [I]
- **No certificate before the supply:** no exemption – charge VAT. The exemption is « subordonnée à la détention » of the certificate. [I · S61 arts 5, 7(2)]

## Embassies and EU bodies based in another EU country

A Luxembourg seller exempts a supply taxed in Luxembourg on presentation of the EU exemption certificate (Implementing Regulation 282/2011, art. 51 and annex II), duly completed and signed, under the **host state's** conditions and limits. [C · S61 art. 12] The same rule covers other bodies with diplomatic privileges in that state (definition of « pays d'accueil »). [I · S61 arts 1 h), 12] Keep the certificate with the invoice. [I]

## EU institutions and international organisations based in Luxembourg

The law points to Protocol No 7 and the headquarters agreements, but we have found no Luxembourg regulation or AED circular that sets the procedure: direct exemption or refund, minimum amount, certificate form (VAT-O-18). [O] Working answer: ask the institution for its VAT exemption certificate or written instructions before invoicing; without them, charge VAT. [I]

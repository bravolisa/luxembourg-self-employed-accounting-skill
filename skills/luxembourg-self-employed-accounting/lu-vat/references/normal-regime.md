# Normal regime

_Figures in this file: 2025–2026. The year files in `figures/` win if they differ._

## Charging VAT

- Rates (2025 and 2026): 17% standard, 14%, 8%, 3%. [C · S3, S24] The 16/13/7% rows on the forms are for 2023 only (figures/history-2023-2024.md). [C · S19] Which rate applies to which supply is not covered yet: open point VAT-O-06.
- VAT is charged per invoice line; the invoice shows the rate and amount per rate (invoicing.md).

## When VAT becomes due

- When the supply is made (art. 21). Supplies billed in successive statements or payments: at the end of each period they cover (art. 22). [C · S24]
- An advance received before the supply: VAT is due on receipt, on the amount received (art. 23). A pro-forma that is not an invoice does not make VAT due by itself; the payment received on it does. [C · S24 art. 23; pro-forma I]
- Where an invoice is required (supplies to businesses, invoicing.md): when the invoice is issued, if within the deadline of the 15th of the following month, otherwise when that deadline runs out (art. 24(1)). This does not apply to services for which the customer abroad owes the VAT. [C · S24 arts 24, 63(5)]
- Cash basis: when paid (below). [C · S24 art. 25]

## Deducting input VAT

- Deductible to the extent purchases are used for the business's taxed activity, with an invoice that meets art. 63: a full invoice naming the business as customer, or – up to €100 including VAT – a simplified invoice, which need not name the customer (art. 63(10)–(11)). [C · S24 art. 48(1), (3)(a), 63(8) 5°, 63(10)] For VAT the business owes itself as the buyer (EU goods, reverse-charged services) the tax must also appear on the return, and the supplier's invoice follows the EU invoicing rules; for imports a customs document naming the business as importer is needed. [C · S24 art. 48(3)(b)–(e)]
- Deduction also stays open for purchases used for exports and intra-EU supplies exempt under art. 43, and for supplies made abroad that would give a right to deduct in Luxembourg – but not for supplies made abroad under another country's small-business franchise. Only supplies exempt by nature (art. 44) or outside the scope block deduction. [C · S24 art. 49]
- Mixed use of a **business asset** (e.g. a car kept in the business): deduct the input VAT and declare the private use as a supply of services (art. 16(a)), taxed on the costs incurred (art. 28(1)(c)) – annual-return box 010 and appendix B (forms/2025/vat-returns.md). Art. 16(a) does not apply when no VAT was deducted on the asset, e.g. under the franchise. [C · S24 arts 16(a), 28(1)(c)] Deducting only the business share from the start is the other route, and then there is no private use to declare. [I] A car – the routes, buying, leasing, running costs, selling: cars.md.
- Mixed-use **services** (phone line, internet): deduct the business share. [I]
- VAT on private use of business assets is a recognised item: it is due to the AED and is not deductible for income tax (art. 12 n° 3 b LIR). [C · see lu-income-tax-business]
- No deduction for VAT on expenses « qui n'ont pas un caractère strictement professionnel, telles que les dépenses somptuaires, de divertissement ou de représentation » – client entertainment and representation included. [C · S24 art. 54(1)] The grand-ducal regulation the article announces does not seem to have been made, so there is no official list of covered items (BravoLisa's Legilux check, September 2026). [I] Selling later a good whose VAT was excluded under art. 54 is exempt (art. 44(1)(x)). [C · S24]
- Under exempt activity (art. 44) or partial exemption, deduction is restricted: VAT linked to exempt supplies goes through box 094, the art. 50 pro-rata through box 095. Report the full input VAT in its origin box and exclude the non-deductible part there, never both. [C · S3 boxes; I method] The art. 50 pro-rata applies to purchases used for both taxed and exempt supplies; it is set once a year as a percentage rounded up to the next whole percent. [C · S24 art. 50] The AED can authorise or oblige deduction by actual use, or sector pro-ratas (art. 51) [C · S24], and since 2013 it obliges businesses with both taxed and exempt activity to assign input VAT by actual use through cost accounting (by staff, floor space, transaction or sector), keeping the general pro-rata only for overheads that cannot be assigned otherwise; the business must be able to justify its keys. [C · S40] How the pro-rata is calculated (RGD of 21 December 1979): numerator = the year's turnover excluding VAT from supplies that give a right to deduct (arts 48(1), 49(2) – including exports, intra-EU supplies and supplies made abroad); denominator = the numerator plus the supplies without that right; both leave out ancillary sales of capital goods and ancillary exempt financial or property operations. The pro-rata is ignored when it is at least 90% and the VAT it would disallow is at most €250 a year. [C · S51, S50]
- For rent and accounting fees the annual return asks for the supplier's name and address; deduction of VAT on operating costs can be refused if the supplier's name is not given (comment 8). [C · S3]
- Gifts: taking business goods to give away is a taxable deemed supply, except gifts of low value and commercial samples (art. 13(a)); it does not apply when no VAT was deducted on them. [C · S24 art. 13(a)]
- Assessing one purchase invoice (reverse charge, imports, foreign VAT, instalments): purchases.md. [I]

## Filing frequency and deadlines

| Prior-year turnover (or intra-EU goods + services received) | Returns | Deadline |
|---|---|---|
| Above €620,000 | Monthly + annual | Before the 15th of the following month; annual before 1 May N+1 |
| Above €112,000, not above €620,000 | Quarterly + annual | Before the 15th after the quarter; annual before 1 May N+1 |
| Not above €112,000 | Annual only | Before 1 March N+1 |

The limits read « n'était pas supérieur à »: exactly €112,000 means annual only. [C · S48 arts 5, 5bis]

The annual return restates the whole year and settles the balance; it is not the sum of the periodic returns. The definitive pro-rata of the year is applied there. [C · S24 arts 52, 64(7)] A business that stops trading files its annual return within **two months** of stopping, whatever its usual frequency. [C · S24 art. 64(7)] Returns and recapitulative statements go to the AED by electronic file transfer (eCDF). [C · S24 arts 64(8), 64bis(4)]

VAT is paid by the same deadline. The AED reviews the frequency at each year end and decides; returns for the wrong period are not accepted. [C · S6] The deadlines are « avant » (before) the date: monthly and quarterly returns before the 15th, the annual return before 1 May, annual-only filers before 1 March. [C · S24 art. 64(6), (7)] There is no general extension; the AED may grant an individual one for special circumstances, possibly subject to a provisional payment (RGD of 23 Dec 1992, art. 7). [C · S48] A late return can be fined €250 to €10,000 per offence, and the AED may assess the VAT itself on presumed turnover. Late payment can add a fine of up to 10% a year of the unpaid VAT. [C · S24 arts 74(2), 77(1)]

## Other returns

- Recapitulative statements (EU B2B sales): before the 25th after the month or quarter. [C · S6]
- OSS return (B2C EU sales above €10,000): end of the month after the quarter. [C · S6]

## Time limits

- The AED's claim for VAT and fines expires five years from 31 December of the year the amount became due; after an interruption a new period runs to the end of the fourth year after the last interrupting act. [C · S24 art. 81]
- A claim for a refund of VAT expires five years from 31 December of the year the tax relates to. [C · S24 art. 82]

## Credit notes and unpaid invoices

- Any change in the taxable amount – a credit note, a discount, goods returned, a price not collected – is adjusted on both sides: the supplier corrects the VAT due and a taxable customer its deduction. [C · S52 art. 1]
- The adjustment is made **at the latest by the end of the return period in which the change happened** – in the current return, not as a correction of the period of the original invoice. [C · S52 art. 2]
- Unpaid invoices: when it can reasonably be assumed that the customer will not pay, or not in full, the supplier may adjust the VAT due. A taxable customer that has still not paid two years after the VAT became due must reverse its deduction for the unpaid part. A later payment reverses the adjustment again. [C · S52 art. 3]

## Invoice basis or cash basis

VAT is normally due when the supply is made (invoice basis). Businesses below €500,000 turnover may account on payments received (box 205 "on payments received" instead of box 204 "on sales"). [C · S6, S12]

- Condition: previous year's turnover excluding VAT not above €500,000 (for a new business, the expected turnover of the current year). On request; it applies from 1 January after the request and must be kept for at least five calendar years, unless the condition stops being met. It ends on 31 December of the year in which the condition is no longer met. [C · S24 art. 25]
- The €500,000 is the same « chiffre d'affaires annuel au Grand-Duché de Luxembourg » as for the franchise: supplies made inside Luxembourg, including exempt exports, but not services whose place of supply is abroad (B2B services to EU customers, listed services to non-EU customers). [C · S24 arts 25(2), 57 point 2]
- Only the supplies whose VAT is otherwise due under arts 21 and 24(1) – essentially domestic taxed sales – move to the payment date; intra-EU supplies and other special chargeability rules keep their own dates. [C · S24 art. 25(1)] On joining, VAT already due on invoices not yet paid is regularised; on leaving or stopping, unpaid supplies made under the scheme become chargeable. [C · S24 art. 25(3)–(4)]
- A business on the cash basis deducts VAT on its own purchases only once it has paid the supplier, and its invoices must carry the mention « Comptabilité de caisse ». [C · S24 arts 48(2), 63(8) 8°] Its customer deducts only when it has paid (purchases.md). [C · S24 arts 48(2), 25(1)]

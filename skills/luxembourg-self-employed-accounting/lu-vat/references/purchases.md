# Purchase invoices – who owes the VAT, what is deductible, which box

_Figures in this file: 2025–2026. The year files in `figures/` win if they differ._

## 1. Facts to collect

Ask only for the facts that can change the answer. [I]

- **Buyer:** VAT profile on the date of the purchase (normal, franchise, art. 44 exempt, or mixed with a pro-rata) and filing frequency. A profile never carries over to another person or another period.
- **Supplier:** the legal entity and the country of the establishment that supplies, the VAT numbers on the invoice, and any wording ("reverse charge", "exempt", "art. 57bis"). Brand, billing currency or a VAT prefix alone do not prove where the supplier is established.
- **What was bought:** goods or services. For services, where they are performed if that could matter (hotel, restaurant, event, transport, a building). For goods, where they were shipped from and to, and who is the importer on the customs papers.
- **Dates and amounts:** invoice date, supply date, payments, earlier partial invoices or credit notes; net, VAT and gross per rate; currency.
- **Use:** business share; the art. 50 pro-rata if the buyer has both taxed and exempt activity.
- **EU goods only (franchise and art. 44 buyers):** total EU goods purchases this year and last year, and whether they opted to be taxed.

If a missing fact can change who owes the VAT, the deduction or the box, give a provisional answer, say which fact is missing, and ask that one question. Never replace an unknown percentage with 0% or 100%. [I]

## 2. Decide in this order

**1. Where is it taxed?** (details in eu-vat)
- Services to a business: in principle where the customer is established. [C · S15 art. 17(1)(b), S17]
- Exceptions, also for business customers: property (where the property is), passenger transport (countries crossed, by distance), admission to cultural, sporting or educational events (where they take place), restaurant and catering (where physically performed), short-term hire of a means of transport (where it is made available; under 30 days, 90 for boats). [C · S17] Hotel accommodation is a property-related service. [I]
- A foreign hotel or restaurant bill is therefore taxed abroad: no Luxembourg reverse charge just because the customer is a Luxembourg business. [I · S17]

**2. Who owes the VAT?**
- Luxembourg supplier charging Luxembourg VAT: the supplier.
- Foreign business supplying a service taxed in Luxembourg: the Luxembourg customer (reverse charge). Art. 61(5): « La taxe est due par l'assujetti … à qui sont fournis les services visés à l'article 17, paragraphe 1er, point b), si ces services sont fournis par un assujetti qui n'est pas établi à l'intérieur du pays. » [C · S33] The same applies to franchise and art. 44 users: the simplified return they file has a section for exactly these services (D.1 EU suppliers, D.2 non-EU suppliers), and it becomes mandatory when they owe VAT as recipients. [C · S2, S1 p. 9] Services have no equivalent of the EU-goods threshold: the reverse charge applies from the first euro. [I]
- Who owes the VAT: as a rule the supplier, also a supplier not established in Luxembourg selling a Luxembourg-located supply (goods from Luxembourg stock, a service performed here) – then it must charge Luxembourg VAT. The Luxembourg buyer owes it only for B2B services under the general rule (art. 61(5)), network gas and electricity (61(4)), the art. 61(3) list, imports (61(7)) and intra-EU acquisitions (61(6)). [C · S24 art. 61] From 1 July 2028 the EU plans a general reverse charge for non-established suppliers (eu-vat, figures/2027.md). [O]
- Amounts the supplier paid **in the customer's name and on the customer's account**, booked in transit accounts, are not part of the price: no VAT on them (e.g. registration duty advanced by a notary). Customs duties paid by a customs agent in its own name are excluded from the agent's fee too. [C · S24 art. 30] Public bodies acting as public authority charge fees outside VAT. [C · S24 art. 8]
- A supplier from another EU country invoicing with an **EX** number under that country's cross-border small-business scheme supplies exempt in Luxembourg (art. 57ter): no VAT and no reverse charge for the buyer. [C · S24 art. 57ter] Without EX, the usual rules apply (eu-vat, sme-cross-border.md).
- Gas, electricity and heat supplied through networks by a supplier not established in Luxembourg: the Luxembourg buyer owes the VAT. [C · S24 art. 61(4)] The domestic reverse-charge list of art. 61(3) never applies to franchise users. [C · S24 art. 61(3)]
- Foreign-currency invoices: the VAT is stated in euro; the rate is the last selling rate published by the BCL or an approved bank from the ECB rate, or the ECB's, when the VAT becomes due – not always the invoice date. [C · S24 arts 37(2), 63(12)]
- Goods from another EU country: a taxable intra-EU acquisition for normal-regime buyers. For franchise and art. 44 buyers only if their EU goods purchases exceeded €10,000 the previous calendar year, or exceed it in the current year, or if they opted in; the option covers two calendar years. [C · S15 art. 2(2)(b)] Below the threshold without an option, the supplier's own VAT treatment applies. [I] Never an acquisition when the EU supplier is itself under a small-business franchise in its own country. [C · S24 art. 2(1)(b)(i)]
- Goods from outside the EU: import VAT is due by the importer – the person in whose name the goods are declared for import – and deducting it needs that import document. [C · S24 arts 61(7), 48(3)(c)] A non-EU seller shipping from stock inside the EU is not an import. [I]
- Quoting the Luxembourg VAT number to an EU goods supplier matters: VAT on an intra-EU acquisition is due by the buyer « ou par la personne qui a communiqué au fournisseur son numéro d'identification TVA ». [C · S24 art. 61(6)] Whether that alone makes a below-threshold purchase taxable for a franchise user: open point VAT-O-04.
- A net invoice is not automatically reverse charge, and an invoice showing VAT is not automatically right. [I]

**3. Can the buyer deduct it?**
- Franchise: no deduction. [C · S1 "ne peuvent pas récupérer la TVA"]
- Art. 44 exempt activity: no deduction for it; on a normal return the VAT goes through box 094. [C · S3 box wording; I]
- Normal regime: the business share, limited by the art. 50 pro-rata for mixed activity (box 095). [C · S3; I for the method] Get the buyer's actual method and percentage; do not assume one.
- Private use and exempt activity are different restrictions: apply each once. [I]
- **Foreign VAT** charged by a supplier in another country is never Luxembourg input tax. A business not registered there reclaims it through the EU refund procedure, limited to VAT the buyer could deduct at home; a business registered for VAT in that country deducts it on that country's return instead (eu-vat, reverse-charge-and-reporting.md). Franchise users are excluded: SME-exempt persons have no right to deduct or to a refund under arts 167–171 of the VAT Directive. [C · S16, S1] For a franchise user, foreign VAT is simply part of the cost.
- **VAT wrongly charged** (for example an EU supplier charging its own VAT where reverse charge applied): not deductible. Ask the supplier for a corrected invoice and refund; the tax authority must refund directly only if the supplier cannot repay, for example when insolvent. The reverse charge still applies on the correct basis. [I · S18] Whoever shows VAT on an invoice owes it. [C · S24 art. 61(8)]

## 3. Amounts

For a business purchase with base B, VAT V and deductible proportion p:

| Item | Formula |
|---|---|
| Deductible VAT | D = V × p |
| Non-deductible business VAT | N = V − D |
| Cost of the expense or asset | B + N (+ other purchase costs) |
| Paid to the supplier | Domestic: B + V. Reverse charge: B |
| Net VAT cost of a reverse-charge purchase | V − D (paid to the AED) |

[I · arithmetic]

- Non-deductible VAT on an asset is part of the asset's cost; it is depreciated, not expensed at once (unless the asset qualifies for immediate write-off). [I · see lu-income-tax-business]
- Keep private spending, foreign VAT to be reclaimed, and VAT disputed with the supplier separate from both D and N. [I]

## 4. Timing, instalments and credit notes

- Keep separate: invoice date, supply date (tax point), payment date, VAT period and income-tax year. VAT timing never follows from the income-tax cash method. [I]
- Supplier on cash accounting (« Comptabilité de caisse » on the invoice): the customer deducts only when, and as far as, it has paid. The right to deduct « prend naissance au moment où la taxe devient exigible », and with cash accounting the tax becomes chargeable when the supplier is paid. [C · S24 arts 48(2), 25(1)] The Commission says the same for the customer of a cash-accounting supplier. [C · S5]
- An invoice dated around 1 January 2023 or 1 January 2024 may carry 16/13/7% or 17/14/8%: see figures/history-2023-2024.md. [C · S19, S20]
- The VAT deducted on one invoice, across all instalments, never exceeds that invoice's VAT. Example: €1,200 + €204 VAT paid in two halves: €102 + €102 if deduction follows payment, or €204 once at the invoice; never more than €204 in total. [I]
- A credit note reverses the matching part of the original cost, VAT due and deduction. An annual pro-rata adjustment is a different thing from a credit note. [I]

## 7. Checks before answering

The answer is wrong if it:
- treats every invoice without VAT as reverse charge;
- gives a franchise user a deduction;
- reports only the net effect of a reverse charge instead of both sides;
- mixes the €10,000 EU-goods test with the €50,000 franchise threshold;
- treats foreign VAT as Luxembourg input tax, or promises a franchise user a foreign refund;
- calls a purchase an import because the seller is outside the EU;
- assumes 100% deduction because the buyer has a VAT number;
- uses the periodic boxes 458–461 on the annual return;
- puts self-assessed VAT in the appendix "VAT invoiced" column;
- deducts the same VAT twice across instalments;
- gives a final answer when a deciding fact is missing.

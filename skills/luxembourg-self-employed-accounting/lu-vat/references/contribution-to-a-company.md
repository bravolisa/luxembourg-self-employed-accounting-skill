# Contributing or selling business equipment to your own company

_Figures in this file: 2025–2026. The year files in `figures/` win if they differ._

A sole trader sets up a SARL or SARL-S and puts business equipment into it – as share capital (a contribution in kind) or by selling it to the company. For VAT the question is always the same: **single items, or the business?** Company law and costs: lu-entity-profiles contribution-in-kind.md. Income tax: lu-income-tax-business contribution-to-a-company.md. A car: cars.md applies as well. Buildings are not covered.

## 1. Single items for shares: VAT as on a sale

- Nothing has to be sold: the deed stays a contribution, with no sale contract and no payment. « Sale » below only describes how the VAT is worked out. [I]
- A normal-regime sole trader who contributes single items of business equipment and receives shares makes a **supply of goods for consideration** – the shares are the price. VAT is due as on a sale. The Court of Justice ruled this in 2024 for buildings contributed by companies; arts 2(1)(a) and 9(1) of the Luxembourg law make no distinction for equipment or for a sole trader. [C · S89; I · S24]
- The Luxembourg VAT law never uses the words « apport en société ». The only rule that reaches a contribution is art. 9(2): the transfer of a whole or partial universality « sous quelque forme et à quelque titre que ce soit » is not a supply (section 3). Single items fall under the general rule. [C · S24; I]
- Luxembourg guidance agrees: a contribution of single assets by a taxable person can be subject to VAT (Guichet); for every transfer other than a universality the transferor must invoice the VAT (the AED's cessation form). [C · S100, S99]
- Rate: **17%** – computers, machines, tools, furniture and vehicles are not in the reduced-rate annexes. [C · S24 art. 39; I for the annexes]
- The company owes nothing on the shares it issues: issuing shares is not a supply. [C · S90]
- Only equipment in her **business assets** is concerned. Items she always kept private are outside VAT. [C · S66]
- A sale to the company instead of a contribution is taxed the same way, on the price. [C · S24 arts 2, 28]

## 2. On which amount

- VAT is charged on everything she receives in return. For a contribution paid in shares that is the **value of the shares the parties agreed in the deed** (their issue value: nominal value plus any share premium) – not an independent estimate of the market value. [C · S24 arts 28(1)(a), 29; S89]
- At the formation of a SARL or SARL-S that equals the contribution value in the deed: **VAT = 17% of the value in the deed.** [I – the AED's own view is not published: VAT-O-25]
- The agreed value must reflect economic reality and not be abusive. [C · S89]
- She and her own company are related parties (« liens … de propriété »). The open-market value replaces the agreed value only in the three cases of art. 28(3) – in practice: the value is **below** market value and the **company cannot deduct in full** (it is under the franchise or has exempt activity), or the value is **above** market value and **she** cannot deduct in full. When both deduct in full, the deed value stands. [C · S24 art. 28(3); S91]
- **The VAT comes on top**: the deed states the value without VAT; her invoice adds 17%, which the company pays her or books as a debt to her. No text describes these mechanics. [I] If nothing was said about VAT and she cannot recover it from the company, the agreed value counts as already including the VAT. [C · S96]

## 3. Single items, or the business (universality)?

- No supply – and no VAT – when a business or an autonomous part of one passes: tangible and, where needed, intangible elements that together allow an independent activity to be carried on, to someone who intends to run it. A simple transfer of assets is not covered. [C · S24 art. 9(2); S92]
- The assets passing must be **sufficient to carry on the activity**, judged on all the facts and by the nature of the activity. [C · S93]

| Element | Must it pass? | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Equipment and tools the activity needs | Yes | [C · S93] |
| Stock | Yes, where the activity needs it (a shop) | [C · S93] |
| Premises | Ownership need not pass: a lease to the company is enough | [C · S93] |
| Clients, contracts, the name | Intangible elements count where the activity needs them; no judgment we opened says they must always pass | [I] |
| Staff | Not addressed in the judgments opened | [O · VAT-O-26] |
| Shares | A transfer of shares is never a transfer of a business | [C · S94] |

Reading for a sole trader:

1. She moves the whole activity – equipment, any stock, clients and contracts – and stops trading herself: a universality. No VAT, **and none shown on any document**; the company continues her position. [C · S24, S99; I for the application]
2. She contributes some equipment and keeps trading, or the equipment alone does not allow the activity to be run: single items, taxable (section 1). [I]
3. In between – a freelancer whose business is herself, a laptop and her clients; the deed lists only equipment, the clients simply follow her: **not settled**. The Court asks for an assessment of all the facts, not of the deed alone. [O · VAT-O-26]
4. One **line of activity** that runs on its own passes, and she keeps her other activity – for example equipment rented out through an online marketplace, where the platform finds the customers and pays out: a partial universality if everything that line needs to keep running passes and she stops it herself. What that is depends on the activity: the equipment, the hosting or premises contract it runs under, and the relationship with the platform (the provider account or contract, the listings). That the company is afterwards paid by the same platform shows that it carries the activity on; it is not the test – the test is what was transferred. [I · S92, S93] A Luxembourg court of first instance accepted one let building, run on its own, as such a part. [I · lu-entity-profiles S32] No official text deals with platform businesses. [O · VAT-O-26]

- It is not a choice: where the conditions are met the law says the transfer « n'est pas considérée comme livraison ». [C · S24 art. 9(2); S92]

| Mistake | Consequence | Status |
|---|---|---|
| VAT shown although a business passed | She owes the VAT shown (art. 61(8)); the company cannot deduct it, because it is due only by reason of the invoice. A real cost unless corrected in time | [C · S24, S57, S95] |
| No VAT charged although single items passed | She owes the VAT and the AED can assess it, with a fine of up to 10% a year. She can still invoice it to the company, which then deducts it | [C · S24 arts 61(1), 77; I for the later invoice] |

- So the two mistakes are not equal: VAT charged on single items is neutral when the company deducts in full; VAT wrongly shown on a business is a cost. In the unsettled case 3, ask an accountant or the AED before the deed is signed. [I]

## 4. Invoice and returns

- **Invoice: compulsory**, at the latest on the 15th of the month after the contribution, with the usual mentions (invoicing.md): her VAT number, both names and addresses, the items, the date, the value without VAT, 17%, the VAT. The **company's VAT number is not required** on an ordinary domestic invoice. [C · S24 art. 63]
- The deed can serve as the invoice if it carries all the mentions; a separate invoice that refers to the deed is simpler. [C · S24 art. 63(8); I]
- **Her return:** annual box **005** (sales of capital assets as ancillary transactions, inside box 012), the amount and VAT on the 17% line, boxes 701 / 702. [C · S3; I for 701]
- **The company:** box **081** (VAT invoiced by other taxable persons on capital expenditure). [C · S3]
- **Invoice dated before the company has its VAT number:** the company deducts in its first return. The right to deduct arises when the tax becomes chargeable and needs an invoice; the customer's number is not a compulsory mention; the Court of Justice treats registration as a formality that cannot cancel the deduction; the AED's legal bulletin says VAT on expenses for a planned taxed activity is deductible. No Luxembourg text names this exact case. [I · S24 arts 48, 63; S97; S101; VAT-O-27]
- **Adjustment periods:** a new five-year period starts for the company on 1 January of the year of the contribution. She has nothing to repay where she deducted in full and the contribution is taxed. [C · S49]

### Laptops, tablets, phones: reverse charge above €10,000

For supplies of mobile phones, game consoles, tablets, laptops and earphones to a VAT-identified business that is not under the franchise, the **customer** owes the VAT when the amount invoiced for these goods exceeds **€10,000 before tax**; up to €10,000 the supplier charges VAT as usual. [C · S24 art. 61(3)] Above the limit: invoice without VAT, the mention « Autoliquidation » and the company's VAT number; her box 419. [C · S24 art. 63(8); S3] A typical freelancer stays far below; an IT business contributing its stock of devices may not.

## 5. She stops the sole trade

Every asset falls into one of three groups. [I · S24 arts 9, 13(c)]

| The asset is | VAT | Her annual return |
|---|---|---|
| Part of a transferred business (universality) | None | – |
| Contributed or sold as a single item | 17%, invoice | Box 005 |
| Kept by her when the VAT activity ends, and VAT was deducted on it | Treated as sold to herself: 17% on the price of similar goods on that day (its second-hand value) – also when the five years have run out | Box 008 in our reading – the form's label names art. 13(a) only [VAT-O-28] |

- [C · S24 arts 13(c), 28(1)(b); S70] Nothing is due on equipment for which she never had a deduction. [C · S24 art. 13]
- Guichet illustrates the cessation with a repayment of the remaining fifths of the VAT deducted. The law taxes the current value of the goods instead; the law prevails. [C · S103 against S24; VAT-O-28]
- **Deadlines:** the cessation declaration within 15 days – the form asks to whom the business went, the price by element (installation, machines, stock, licence, clientele) and rate, and the VAT invoiced; the return for the shortened year, with payment, within two months. [C · S99; S24 art. 64(7); S102]
- Cash accounting (art. 25): VAT on invoices still unpaid becomes due at cessation. [C · S24 art. 25]

## 6. Franchise

| Case | VAT | Status |
|---|---|---|
| She is under the franchise and contributes equipment | None: her supplies are exempt and she may not show VAT. Invoice with the franchise mention (invoicing.md) | [C · S24 art. 57bis] |
| Does it count towards the franchise threshold? | No – disposals of capital goods are left out | [C · S24 art. 57] |
| The company receiving it | Nothing to deduct: no VAT was due on the supply. The VAT she once paid to her own supplier stays a cost | [C · S24 art. 48(1); I] |
| She is in the normal regime, the company under the franchise | The company cannot deduct the 17%; it is part of the cost of the equipment. The value must not be below market value (section 2) | [C · S24 arts 57bis(5), 28(3)] |
| She stops under the franchise and keeps equipment | No charge – nothing was deducted | [C · S24 art. 13] |

- Recovering the VAT she paid when buying is not possible through a contribution of single items made **under the franchise**. Two other ways exist on paper – leaving the franchise first, or a transfer of the business or of an autonomous part, where the company continues her position. Both depend on VAT-O-01 (below). [I · S49; VAT-O-01]

### Equipment she bought in another EU country under the franchise

- EU goods purchases above €10,000 in the year are taxed in Luxembourg: she owes 17% on the purchase price and cannot deduct it (franchise.md §2). It is owed whether or not she later contributes the equipment, and it is hers to declare – not the company's. [C · S15; the purchase that crosses the limit: VAT-O-04]
- **Contributed as single items while she is under the franchise:** an exempt supply – no VAT, nothing for the company to deduct – and the regulation on capital goods gives her nothing back, because her supply opens no right to deduct. The 17% is a final cost. [C · S24 art. 57bis; S49 art. 2(3)(b)]
- **Contributed after she has left the franchise** (franchise.md §6): the contribution is taxed at 17% on the deed value and the company deducts it. For a taxed supply of a movable capital good within its five years, the regulation corrects the initial non-deduction in one go: she deducts one fifth of the VAT on the purchase for each year still to run. A supply after 30 June counts from 1 January of the next year. Not for VAT of €125 or less per item. [C · S49 arts 1, 2(3)(a); S50] Whether the AED applies this to equipment bought under the franchise is not confirmed. [O · VAT-O-01] Side effects: her other sales carry VAT from then on, for at least one calendar year.
- **A line of activity passes** (section 3, reading 4): no supply and no VAT; the company continues her position, so the five years run on in the company – one fifth a year, if the AED accepts it. [I · S24 art. 9(2); S49 art. 2(2); O · VAT-O-01]
- It is not a choice between the last two: if a line of activity passes, there is no supply to tax. [C · S24 art. 9(2)]
- Simplest when it can still be chosen: the company buys the equipment itself. A normal-regime company declares the Luxembourg VAT on the EU purchase and deducts it in the same return. [C · S24 arts 2, 48; I]

Example (invented figures): servers bought in another EU country in March 2026 for €20,000 without VAT, under the franchise. Luxembourg VAT owed on the purchase: €3,400, not deductible.

| What she does | VAT on the contribution | Back from the purchase VAT | Net cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| Contributes in October 2026, still under the franchise | None | Nothing | €3,400 |
| Leaves the franchise on 1 October 2026, contributes on 15 October at €18,000 | €3,060, which the company deducts | Four fifths (2027–2030): €2,720, if the AED accepts it | €680 |

[I – our arithmetic on S49; VAT-O-01]

## 7. Used items she bought without VAT

- Equipment bought from a private person (or under a margin scheme) and held as a business asset is taxed on the **whole** contribution value – that no VAT could be deducted on the purchase does not matter. The rule cars.md states for a car applies to any capital good. [C · S66; I for a contribution]
- No exemption fits (art. 44(1)(x) covers goods used only for exempt activity or excluded from deduction), and the margin scheme is for dealers. [C · S24]
- Way out, as for the car: take the item out of the business first – not taxed, because nothing was deducted – and contribute it as a private person. [C · S24 art. 13; S66]

## 8. Worked example (invented figures)

A normal-regime sole trader contributes machines worth €6,000 (bought two years ago for €10,000 plus €1,700 VAT, all deducted) to her new SARL-S, which is in the normal regime with taxed sales only. No clients, contracts or other assets pass: single items.

| Step | Amount | Where |
|---|---|---|
| Value in the deed = issue value of her shares | €6,000 | Deed |
| Her invoice: €6,000 + 17% | VAT €1,020, total €7,020 | By the 15th of the next month |
| The company pays her the €1,020, or owes it to her | €1,020 | – |
| Her return | €6,000 in boxes 005 and 701; €1,020 in 702 | She pays €1,020 to the AED |
| The company's return | €1,020 in box 081 | Deducted; a new five-year period starts |

Net VAT cost: nil – the company pre-finances €1,020 until its refund. [I – the steps as labelled above]

Variants: the company is under the franchise – the €1,020 is a final cost. She is under the franchise – no VAT at all. The machines were bought second-hand from a private person – still €1,020. The whole business passes – no VAT, and none shown anywhere.

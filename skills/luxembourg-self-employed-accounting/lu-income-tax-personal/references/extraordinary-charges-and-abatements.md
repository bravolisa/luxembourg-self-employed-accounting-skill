# Extraordinary charges and abatements

_Figures in this file: 2025–2026. The year files in `figures/` win if they differ._

Abatements are deducted from taxable income after special expenses. Most are computed by the ACD from what the return shows (Form 100 page 20, « Réservé à l'Administration »). [C · S1]

## Extraordinary charges (art. 127)

- On request, an abatement for charges that are **unavoidable** and **considerably reduce the ability to pay** – illness, disability, supporting needy parents, maintenance, lawsuit and divorce costs – net of any reimbursement. [C · S2; S9]
- Only the part above a **"normal charge"** counts: a percentage of taxable income set by income band, tax class and number of child reductions (art. 127(4)). [C · S2]

| Taxable income | Class 1, no child reduction | Class 1a or 2: no child | 1 child | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Below €10,000 | 2% | 0% | 0% | 0% | 0% | 0% | 0% |
| €10,000–20,000 | 4% | 2% | 0% | 0% | 0% | 0% | 0% |
| €20,000–30,000 | 6% | 4% | 2% | 0% | 0% | 0% | 0% |
| €30,000–40,000 | 7% | 6% | 4% | 2% | 0% | 0% | 0% |
| €40,000–50,000 | 8% | 7% | 5% | 3% | 1% | 0% | 0% |
| €50,000–60,000 | 9% | 8% | 6% | 4% | 2% | 0% | 0% |
| Above €60,000 | 10% | 9% | 7% | 5% | 3% | 1% | 0% |

- The child columns also apply to class 1 with at least one child reduction; a 50% child reduction counts as a full one. [C · S2]
- The law's bands share their limits (« de 30.000 à 40.000 », « de 40.000 à 50.000 »); the ACD's own example below puts exactly €40,000 in the higher band. [C · S9]
- Nothing already deducted as a business expense, a cost of earning income or a special expense can be claimed again. [C · S9]
- Form 100 page 17 (sheet CE), with details and proof. [C · S1]

### Worked example (ACD's own)

Class 2, no child, taxable income €40,000, unreimbursed medical costs €4,000: normal charge 7% = €2,800, abatement €1,200. [C · S9]

## Extra-professional abatement (art. 129b)

- **€4,500 a year** (€375 a month) for spouses or partners taxed jointly who **both** have professional income. [C · S2]
- **Self-employed profit counts** – commercial or liberal-profession – provided each spouse is personally compulsorily insured; also when one spouse runs the business and the other is insured as a **helping spouse** (*conjoint-aidant*). [C · S13; S2]
- It cannot exceed either spouse's net professional income (after compulsory contributions and the €480 minimum): a spouse with a very small or negative profit limits it. [C · S2]
- Also on request when one spouse or partner has professional income and the other has drawn a pension or annuity (art. 96(1) n° 1–2) for **less than 3 years (36 months)** at the start of the year (art. 129b(2)(c)). Ask on Form 100 page 8, box 860, with the pension start date in box 861. [C · S2; S1 p. 8]

## Staying in work after pension age (from 2026)

New from tax year 2026: the *abattement de maintien dans la vie professionnelle* (art. 129g) – up to €9,000 a year (€750 a month) for people entitled to a personal pension who keep working, **including** commercial and liberal-profession activity, with a certificate from the pension body. [C · S31] The CNAP issues the certificate once, on request through MyGuichet (people aged 57–65 in the general scheme), by post; it stays valid until the pension starts, and the person sends it to the ACD. [C · S63] It is granted on request, and the request must come with that certificate – name, first name, matricule and the date the pension right opened – sent to the ACD on paper or electronically (art. 129g(7)). [C · S2] A self-employed person is in the general pension scheme, so the certificate comes from the CNAP, and the abatement is claimed in the tax return, not through payroll. [C · S63 for the CNAP; I · S190 for the return – the law's commentary] Which box of the 2026 Form 100: not known until the form is published (watch list W-08). Bill 8773 would extend the abatement to people on a progressive pension (watch list W-15).

Bill 8773, voted at first reading on 28 October 2026 (not law yet – watch list W-15): from tax year 2026 a progressive pension (private sector) or a partial pension (civil servants) no longer bars the abatement, and it runs until the legal retirement age (civil servants: their age limit). [O · S2 for the current text; bill 8773 committee report of 23 Oct 2026]

## Articles no longer in force

Arts 127ter, 127quater, 128, 128bis, 129, 129a, 129c and 129d LIR are repealed; 127, 127bis, 129b, 129e, 129f and 129g apply. [C · S2] Older websites citing them describe past rules.

## Coming (bill, not law)

Bill 8676 (from 2028) proposes a €6,000 childcare and household abatement, a new normal-charge table, and keeping the extra-professional abatement only for couples still taxed jointly during the transition – watch list W-05. [O · S33]

## Childcare monthly calculation for 2025

The 2017 circular replaces the relevant 2012 guidance from tax year 2017. For each month, take eligible costs actually paid, subtract third-party reimbursements, and apply that month's limit from figures/2025.md. Add those monthly results. An annual total divided by twelve can give a wrong deduction. The circular says « Les dépenses effectivement exposées sont à prendre en considération » and specifies a monthly assessment in paragraph 36 (read 2026-11-04). [C · S73]

The flat abatement is unique across children and the three covered categories: domestic help, dependence care and childcare. Do not multiply it by child or provider. [C · S73] Apply one combined limit to the married joint household's two payer columns. [I · S73; S72 art. 3]

A qualifying child is under 14 on 1 January, or disabled without that age limit, and gives the household the child reduction. The provider must have the relevant approval, including when in another EU state. The full qualifying care fee counts without removing a standard upkeep/training portion. Approval, payment and reimbursements need evidence. [C · S73]

Choosing the common flat claim excludes actual domestic-help, dependence-care and childcare claims across all three categories, including different invoices; distinct medical or disability claims can coexist. [C · S74 art. 3(3)–(4), S10]
The flat regime does not subtract the ordinary article-127 normal-charge percentage. Advance or arrears payments covering different service months remain PIT-O-15; do not invent an allocation.
[C · S73 for the alternatives; O for payment timing]

Childcare reduces **adjusted taxable income after box 2038**, not special expenses or the box-2038 amount itself (arts. 7 and 126). PDF boxes 1718–1723 and XML H1520 contain actual expenses, not the capped abatement. Varying monthly costs need a transparent detail schedule; the tool writes a monthly annex and distinct XML periods. [C · S72, S1, S75 for the fields/rules; B for the annex]

Detailed combined-claim choices, irregular timing, pooled non-household-child ceilings and evidence: [childcare-and-extraordinary-cases.md](childcare-and-extraordinary-cases.md).

## Extra-professional allowance: income and period limits

The joint maximum is limited by each spouse's relevant net professional/recent-pension amount, after the specified article 110 expenses, special-expense minimum and applicable cessation allowance.
Part-year reduction uses whole months of tax liability, not just months worked. Payroll AC bundles several allowances and does not create another annual claim. The estimator requires a reviewed amount; see payroll-certificate-deductions.md. [C · S82 art.129b; S115; B for tool boundary]

## Actual medical automation and table boundaries

The 2025 household adapter supports reviewed self-paid adults, cross-paid adults in a supported joint assessment, and extraordinary treatment for a minor child in the tax household, with complete final same-year reimbursements and income. It preserves gross/refund/net/normal-burden/allowed amounts separately. See lu-annual-return forms/2025/medical-and-partnership.md for the full contract. This does not automate all article 127 charges. [B]

The ACD table and example establish the 40000 class 2/no-child case and the strict above 60000 final band. By the same pattern an income exactly at 20000, 30000 or 50000 takes the band that starts there – our reading of the table, not an ACD statement; the tool still stops there when the choice changes the relief. [I · S82, S9; B for the stop] Fractional-cent relief remains guarded; do not claim a verified rounding rule or silently extend the last child column. PIT-O-33/34 record these and cross-payer/cross-year evidence questions. [C · S9 for table; O for unresolved semantics]


### Family medical costs and reimbursement timing — 2025 research

Child moderation or article 127bis covers ordinary upkeep/education, but article 127(4a) preserves other extraordinary charges. Circular127/1 §19 expressly illustrates additional long and costly child treatment. Qualification remains evidence-based and subject to the single household normal burden. There is no universal minor-only statutory rule in that passage. [C · S82 art.127(4a); S90 §19]

For the supported joint assessment, accepting an evidenced medical bill paid by one declared adult for the other is our bounded interpretation of articles 3/3bis/127 and circular 127/1 §§7,11,13,32. It does not establish care-home, outside-dependent or individually assessed spouse automation. Do not import the needy-relative maintenance test into an ordinary joint-household medical claim. [I · S82; S90]

Extraordinary costs follow the payment year. CNS supplementary participation follows service-year rules and may be claimed until the end of N+2, including co-insured family costs. The tax treatment of a subsequent reimbursement remains open: the social-security service-year rule does not establish the tax-correction year. Record payer, patient, payment date, each refund date/recipient/insurer and final entitlement review, including supplementary coverage. The tool stops cross-year, pending and unallocated refunds. [C · S90 §4; S82 art.108; S158 art.154bis; B/O for boundary]

The official 2024 tutorial includes taxpayer/spouse/child H0020 choices, but does not explicitly settle whether a different payer changes the selected person. Paper payer-column allocation with a separate patient annex is I; cross-person XML remains blocked for 2025. Existing old 157ter examples retain cent precision but do not resolve fractional cents or disputed exact table endpoints.

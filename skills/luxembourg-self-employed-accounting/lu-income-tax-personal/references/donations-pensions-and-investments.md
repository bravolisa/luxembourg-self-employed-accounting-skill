# Donations, pension income and private investment income

_Figures in this file: 2025–2026; pension CO2 amounts differ by year. Use the year files and the explicit 2025 form map._

## Donations: recipient, year and carryforward

Check the recipient's statutory approval and its effective first year. Ordinary gifts are cash gifts without a counter-benefit; not every association, crowdfunding payment or gift of goods qualifies. In-kind routes are restricted statutory cultural/audiovisual cases and require the proper valuation. The updated 2025 law already includes Kultur Ix; an older consolidation can miss it. Approved social-impact companies need the required all-impact-share status. [C · S98, S82 art. 112]

Equivalent foreign bodies can be in the **EU or EFTA** (including Switzerland), subject to the general-interest/equivalence and local-deductibility conditions. This is not an EEA-only rule. Do not copy the historical 2010 member list as today's geography. Keep recipient identity, approval/equivalence evidence, payment date and amount, transfer proof and attestations; equivalent-body documentation may be in English, French or German. [C · S99, S103]

Apply the annual minimum and the lower of the percentage-of-total-net-income and absolute caps in the year file. The payment year controls. Excess **over the upper limits** can carry to the next two years; gifts below the minimum do not become a carryforward merely because they were not deducted. Keep 2023, 2024 and 2025 vintages separately for the 2025 return, with prior amounts used and expiry. The personal-tax priority order among old/current donations was not established by the opened sources: PIT-O-19. Do not borrow a corporate integrated-group FIFO rule. [C · S82 arts. 109, 112; S7; O for ordering]

## Pension certificates and deductions

Classify the product before copying numbers: statutory/former-employer pensions use gross section-A income; qualifying private retirement annuities have their statutory half-exemption; ordinary article-111bis capital repayments are miscellaneous income under article 99(4), not monthly annuities. Non-exceptional early repayment has its own article-99(5) route. A foreign pension requires treaty classification by country and pension type, including exemption with progression where applicable. [C · S82 arts. 96, 99, 115; S1]

The pension earning-cost flat is once per person for the category, not per certificate. It can coexist with the separate salary-category flat; it doubles on joint assessment only if both spouses have the relevant income. Higher actual costs need an annex. The flat cannot exceed category receipts. Part-year **tax liability** has a statutory monthly rule; do not confuse that with a full-year resident receiving a pension for only part of the year. [C · S82 art. 107; S1]

Compulsory pension/social contributions are special expenses, not a reduction of the declared gross pension; dependency contributions are not deductible. Income tax withheld and credits already paid are different amounts and must be reconciled separately. [C · S82 arts. 110, 154; S1]

### Pensions from the United Kingdom (convention of 7 June 2022)

- The new convention applies in Luxembourg from **1 January 2024**. [C · S183] UK private and workplace pensions paid to a Luxembourg resident « may be taxed » in the UK (art. 17(1) – « pensions and other similar remuneration arising in a Contracting State »). Luxembourg then **exempts** them, but may use them to set the rate on the household's other income (art. 22(4)(a); exemption with progression, art. 134 LIR); the credit method of art. 22(4)(b) does not cover pensions. [C · S182; S2 art. 134] The UK State Pension falls under the same article: the treaty has no separate rule for social-security pensions, and HMRC's guidance treats it so. [I · S182]
- This reverses the old convention, under which such pensions were taxed in the country of residence. Only someone who already received the pension before the new convention entered into force (23 November 2023) could elect to keep the old rule, within three years (art. 29(5)) – not for a pension that starts later. [C · S182; S184]
- **Government-service pensions** (paid by the UK state for services to it): taxable only in the UK, unless the person is resident in and a national of Luxembourg (art. 18(2)). [C · S182]
- **On the return**: a resident reports worldwide income, the exempt pension included; on Form 100 it goes on page 8, line A (pensions paid by former employers or autonomous pension funds), as exempt income. [C · S2 art. 134; I for the column] It is **not** a 50% case: the half-exemption of art. 115 n° 14a is for annuities from Luxembourg private pension contracts (arts 111bis, 111ter). [C · S2 art. 115]
- **Credits**: no pensioner credit (CIP) on it – the CIP is only for pensions « dont le droit d'imposition revient au Luxembourg », and it cannot be combined with the self-employed credit (CII). [C · S2 art. 154quinquies]
- **Dependency contribution**: none is assessed on the exempt pension – « Les revenus exonérés en vertu d'une convention contre les doubles impositions ne sont pas à comprendre dans l'assiette de la contribution dépendance » (ACD circular CADEP 1, point 5, still in the ACD's collection). [C · S104]
- A UK **lump sum** most likely follows art. 17 like the pension (the UK may tax, Luxembourg exempts with progression), not art. 20 – our reading of art. 17(2); not confirmed, check before taking one: PIT-O-39. [I · S182; O]

### Dependency contribution the ACD assesses

The pension fund already withholds the 1.4% dependency contribution from statutory pensions (Luxembourg social-security and public-sector pensions); the ACD never charges it again. The ACD assesses it with the tax return on income nobody withholds it from, for residents covered by the dependency insurance: other pensions and annuities under art. 96 – for example a former employer's direct pension or an annuity from an art. 111bis private retirement contract, but not payments from a supplementary pension scheme under the law of 8 June 1999 – and net capital, rental and miscellaneous income. [C · S104 §§3.1, 6]

- Base for these pensions and annuities: gross, minus actual costs or the €300 flat, minus exempt amounts (the annuity's exempt half). With several pensions the ACD takes the whole €300 flat off the pensions in this base, without splitting it.
- No allowance of a quarter of the minimum wage: that one is only for salaries and statutory pensions.
- Each income category counts only if positive; a loss in one category does not reduce another.
- Rate 1.4%. A contribution of €24.79 or less a year counts as nil, tested separately for pensions and for capital and rental income, and not doubled for a couple. [C · S104 §§3.1, 6–7]

Example (invented): statutory pensions €18,000 and €12,000, plus a €6,000 art. 111bis annuity. Income tax: 36,000 − 3,000 exempt half − 300 flat (once) = €32,700 net pension income. Dependency base at the ACD: 6,000 − 300 − 3,000 = **€2,700**, contribution 1.4% = €37.80. The statutory pensions are not in this base. The circular's own example: a €5,000 annuity gives 5,000 − 300 − 2,500 = €2,200. [C · S104 §3.1]

For the extra-professional abatement on professional income plus a recent pension, test jointly assessed spouses, qualifying pension type, the **less than 36 months at the beginning of the year** condition, request and start date, and each spouse's limiting net amount. Exactly 36 months fails this branch. [C · S82 art. 129b; S1]

## CIP and pension CO2 credit

CIP is **not identical to CIS at low income**. Qualifying taxable pension income and personal compulsory social-security affiliation are required; use all relevant pensions, including treaty-exempt pension in the statutory gross-income measure. The annual 2025 schedule is in figures/2025.md. Below the pension minimum or from the upper threshold neither credit is granted; the low pension band has a base CIP and pension CO2 credit. [C · S82 art. 154quinquies; S101, S102]

The annual figures require an eligible full year. For incomplete periods use the statutory monthly/daily amounts and round upward to cents. Multiple pensions do not create multiple credits. Overlap with salary/business is reconciled **per person and per month**, ranking categories by highest average monthly income, not by highest possible credit. Spouses remain separate credit recipients even in joint assessment. Reconcile certificates' credits already paid with entitlement; the assessment circular treats a positive repayment below €12 as zero. Do not confuse this with article-124 tariff tax below €12, which is a different rule. [C · S57, S82, S101]

## Dividends and assessed capital income

Start from gross distributions. The 50% dividend exemption requires the statutory issuer test: fully taxable resident capital company, qualifying EU directive company, or corresponding fully taxable treaty-country company. A fund distribution or any foreign company does not qualify automatically. Luxembourg dividend withholding is 15% of gross; it remains separate from the income exemption and goes to the withholding-credit section. [C · S82 arts. 115(15a), 148, 154]

For actual expenses directly linked to half-exempt dividends, only the taxable share is deductible. Choose actual qualifying expenses or the flat, not both. The capital flat is doubled only when both jointly taxed spouses or partners **each** receive capital income: « Les montants visés à l'alinéa 1er sont doublés lorsque des époux imposables collectivement au sens de l'article 3 perçoivent chacun des revenus de l'espèce » (art. 107(4), unchanged since 1990); the ACD's Mémento repeats the condition. With one recipient the flat stays single. [C · S82, S188] The 2025 form (page 9) and the ACD capital page shorten this to "doubled for jointly taxed spouses"; that is shorthand – the law decides, so do not award the doubled flat to a sole-recipient couple. [C · S1, S100 for the wording; I for reading it as shorthand] Spouses holding a joint account both receive the income. [I] The separate capital-income exemption follows a different rule: it is doubled on joint taxation alone (art. 115 n° 15). [C · S82]

After the relevant exemptions and costs, apply the annual assessed-capital exemption, limited to remaining capital income; it cannot create a loss. Ordinary excess capital costs cannot offset other categories, but art. 97(5) has an exception for the specified dividend income from a substantial participation where more than half the taxpayer's professional income comes from an occupation in that company. Test the whole exception before ring-fencing a loss. [C · S82 arts. 97, 115]

Foreign withholding is not automatically a Luxembourg credit: treaty limits and Luxembourg tax limits apply, and half-exempt dividends have a particular credit/deduction allocation. Amounts above treaty entitlement may need recovery in the source country; do not credit or deduct the full foreign deduction by default. [C · S105; S82 arts. 115, 134bis]

## Savings interest: three distinct routes

1. Private interest under final Luxembourg **RELIBI**: no assessment declaration and no assessment credit for its final levy. The assessed-capital allowance does not apply. The specific qualifying savings exception up to €250 per person and payer (annual crediting) is exempt and also not declared. Above it, the whole qualifying amount bears RELIBI, not only the excess. [C · S30]
2. Interest outside RELIBI, including low-rate current/sight accounts within the circular's exclusion, remains assessable under ordinary income rules. **€250 is not a universal exemption**. Foreign interest without a valid final-levy election also follows assessment. [C · S30; S82]
3. Eligible foreign EU/EEA paying-agent interest may use the optional final levy through Form 931 by the end of the following year; the option covers the full eligible pool, not selected accounts. Switzerland's EFTA membership alone does not make its payer EEA-eligible for this option. Business interest remains business profit, with its withholding creditable under the business rule. [C · S30]

Private movable-asset sales within six months can be speculative; the annual gain threshold in the year file is a strict exemption boundary, not a deductible allowance. After six months, substantial shareholdings can remain taxable under article 100: more than 10% during the prior five years, including relevant spouse/partner/minor-child and indirect holdings. Do not infer that every long-held investment gain is exempt. [C · S82 arts. 99bis–100]

## Supported donation preparation, 2025

Current-year reviewed cash gifts now have a household mapper and cap calculation. The percentage base is total net income **before** special expenses, including before compulsory contributions classified there. Donations remain outside the flat-minimum comparison. [C · S82 arts7,109,112,113]
Actual donor rows and paid totals remain gross; the PDF's deductible total is adjusted separately and explained in a calculation/carry annex. This is a generated-output convention, not an ACD instruction. Incoming carryforwards and competing vintages stay manual under PIT-O-19; fractional cent cap treatment stays open under PIT-O-32. Negative or insufficient income is outside this first automatic route. See lu-annual-return `references/forms/2025/form100-deductions.md`. [B/O · S150]

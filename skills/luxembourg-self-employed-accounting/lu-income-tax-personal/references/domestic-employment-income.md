# Domestic employee income and CCSS remuneration certificates

Figures in this file: 2025 – the year files win if they differ.

Use this for the **worker's** income from a private household. The household employer's deduction is a different claim: childcare-and-extraordinary-cases.md. A CCSS issuer does not make a document a self-employed contribution certificate. Establish the person, covered year, employer, activity, regime, period and whether the document reports gross remuneration or monthly net declarations. [B]

## Establish the regime before adding income

The RGD of 19 November 1999, consolidated for 2025, implements article 137(5): qualifying private household cleaning, childcare and dependency care are subject to the simplified flat regime.
Childcare qualification has the beginning-of-year age condition and disability exception. Do not infer a free prospective choice of ordinary payroll from the ACD overview's word “can”; establish the actual applicable regime and certificate. The confirmed choice below is post-year regularisation. [C · S108 arts. 1–2; S109]

The employer bears flat tax of **10% of remuneration net of tax, employee health/pension contributions and dependency contribution**, not 10% of gross. CCSS calculates the gross and contributions from the declared net and hours. Do not reverse gross using only a tax percentage:
use the annual CCSS extract and its contribution breakdown. The worker does not present an ordinary tax card for this regime. [C · S108 arts. 3–6; S110]

An annual extract identifies the regime, employment period, gross salary, employee contributions, dependency, flat withholding, net remuneration and employer. It may combine several occupations.
The semestrial registration check supports the annual extract; partial monthly net declarations are neither a second salary nor proof of a full year's gross. Reconcile period and employer before deduplicating. Direct CNS payments require their own evidence, distinct from sickness paid by the employer and already included in the annual gross. [C · S108 art. 4; S110; S112 note 6; B for intake]

## Post-year choice and household scope

| Decision | Treatment |
|---|---|
| No request for normal-regime regularisation | Leave the flat-taxed remuneration, its related deductible special expenses and its flat withholding out of ordinary assessment/annual adjustment. Do not omit income but retain its contributions or withholding. [C · S108 art. 7] |
| Request normal-regime regularisation | Include all affected remuneration and the associated evidence for normal treatment. The request covers **all** flat withholdings of the employee **and the jointly assessed spouse**, not a selected employer or just the most beneficial contract. Attach all CCSS extracts. The employer-borne tax is treated as withholding on the specified net remuneration. [C · S108 art. 8] |
| Jointly assessed PACS partners | **Form 163 annual adjustment** expressly includes all flat remuneration of the taxpayer and jointly assessed spouse or partner from 2022 (RGD 17 December 2021). For **Form 100 assessment**, the interaction with article 8 of the 1999 regulation remains PIT-O-22; do not extrapolate blindly between routes. [C · S128; O · S82 art. 3bis; S108; S127] |

The route may be Form 100 assessment or Form 163 annual adjustment, depending on filing eligibility.
A CCSS certificate does not alone establish a duty to file Form 100. Apply the current filing rules;
historical thresholds in circular 153/1 are not substitutes for the 2025 statute. [C · S109; S82 arts. 145,153; S111 for historical explanatory context]

Record the household's explicit request and completeness of all affected contracts before output.
Compare the two treatments with reviewed facts rather than promising that an election is always better. Never create an election merely to make a generator accept an input. [B]

## Employee credits remain a separate calculation

CCSS CIS/CO2 payment under article 154quater(3) requires the employee's **entire salary income** to fall under article 137(5); ordinary plus domestic employment does not satisfy that condition.
Article 139quater(5) CISSM is different: CCSS grants it **per employment contract**. Do not transfer the CISSM rule to CIS/CO2 or combine either with a second full annual credit. Read employee-tax-credits.md for paid-versus-retained reconciliation and the statutory eligibility conditions. [C · S82 arts. 139quater,154quater; S113]

The mixed ordinary/domestic case **without** domestic-income regularisation still needs a precise credit-gross rule: the assessment circular lists flat-regime remuneration, while the 1999 regulation excludes it from ordinary assessment without an election. Keep the income election separate from the credit base and request review; do not infer that one exclusion settles both.
PIT-O-21 records this boundary. [O · S57; S108]

## Form and tool boundary

Manual 2025 PDF/XML mapping is in forms/2025/salary-certificates.md. Paper row E is distinct from first/second employment and “other contracts”. The schema has distinct domestic occupation/pay codes; their existence is not evidence that the live assistant applied an election correctly.
RET-O-01 remains open. [C · S1; S75; B/O for implementation]

The household tools stop for declared non-ordinary regimes and flat-regime codes. They do not make the election, prepare a complete domestic-flat return or calculate its final balance. Keep the source regime visible and use reviewed manual preparation; do not relabel it SALA/S to bypass the stop. [B]

## Worked examples — invented components

- Declared monthly net EUR 1,000: flat tax is EUR 100, employer-borne. Gross cannot be concluded to be EUR 1,100 without the employee contribution calculation. [I · S108 art. 3]
- Married joint household with three domestic contracts split between spouses: an article 8 request covers all three; selecting only one contract is invalid. Without a request, do not retain their contribution/withholding amounts in ordinary assessment. [I · S108 arts. 7–8]
- A worker also has ordinary employment: preserve both categories and review the credit route;
  the fact that the CCSS certificate shows no CIS is not proof of no possible annual entitlement.
  Missing, explicit zero, and an incorrect paid amount are three different intake states. [I · S82;
  S57; B for intake]

These are component examples, not verified complete household assessments.

## Reconcile the worker's certificate before choosing the route

Collect the worker's CCSS annual extract for every employer and period. The employer's certificate may include employer contributions for the employer's deduction; it is not a second worker salary.
Use the worker gross, employee health/pension contributions, dependency, flat withholding and net as separate fields. A combined extract can already contain several jobs. [C · S108 art4; B for intake]

CCSS states that the preceding year's salary certificate is issued automatically in March. CIS and CISSM payments may arrive with a three-month lag. Preserve the certificate's covered year and the credit's entitlement period separately from issue date and bank-payment date. Do not turn a credit received in March 2026 into2026 entitlement without its period evidence. [C · S110; S113; I for intake]

Invented extract: gross EUR14,800, deductible employee health/pension EUR1,500, dependency EUR100, flat tax EUR1,200, contractual net EUR12,000. The reconciliation is 14,800−1,500−100−1,200=12,000;
1,200 is 10%of 12,000. With an established regularisation request and all contracts complete, the paper components are gross14,800 in rowE, contributions 1,500 and withholding 1,200. Dependency100 is not another deductible contribution. Without the request, omit the associated salary, contribution and withholding together. This example does not calculate credits or a final refund.
[I · S108 arts3–4,7–8; S1; S82 art110; B for example scope]

## Compare complete annual outcomes, not just the flat withholding

Prepare both outcomes with the same household facts and independently reviewed credit treatment:
(1) ordinary assessment excluding domestic flat pay and its associated special expenses/withholding;
(2) normal-regime treatment including all required flat salaries, the relevant contributions and the employer-borne flat tax as withholding. Recompute any affected allowances and credit entitlement;
do not add the flat tax to a refund while leaving the domestic salary out. [C · S108 arts7–8; I/B for comparison]

Historical ACD circular 137/1 §4.3 and example2 require a favourable-only comparison. It is useful evidence of the comparison method, but its2000 figures and obsolete6%rate are not 2025 rules. The current ACD guidance describes requesting normal treatment when it appears more favourable. This research did not establish a new2025 statutory guaranteed-refund rule; do not promise an outcome before both current-year calculations and credit issues are resolved. [C/history · S178; C · S109; O for unverified guarantees]

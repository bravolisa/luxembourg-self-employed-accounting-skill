# Income categories – what else a self-employed household declares

_Figures in this file: 2025–2026. The year files in `figures/` win if they differ._

The business profit comes from Form 110 or Form 152 (lu-income-tax-business). This file covers everything else on Form 100 and how it adds up.

## How total income is built

- Only **eight categories** of net income count: commercial profit, agricultural and forestry profit, liberal-profession profit, salary, pensions and annuities, capital income, rental income, miscellaneous income (art. 10 LIR). [C · S2]
- Total net income is the sum of the categories; a loss in one category offsets income in the others unless the law says otherwise (art. 7(2) LIR). [C · S2]
- Taxable income = total net income − special expenses (art. 7(1) LIR); the abatements (extraordinary charges, extra-professional abatement…) come after that. [C · S2]
- Rent or interest that belongs to the business (a business bank account, a building in the business assets) is business income, not private rental or capital income (arts 97(4), 98(5) LIR). [C · S2]
- **When income counts.** Salary, pensions, capital, rental and miscellaneous income count in the tax year in which they are « mises à la disposition » of the taxpayer – paid, or made available so the person can use them – not when they are earned or booked. A regular payment made available shortly before or after the year it belongs to counts in that year. Expenses count when paid (art. 108(1) LIR). [C · S2 art. 108] Business profit follows its own method (lu-income-tax-business). Pay from one's own company credited to a current account the manager can draw on counts as made available. [I] Pay a controlling shareholder-manager has decided but not received: PIT-O-38. [O]

## The categories a self-employed person typically has

| Category (FR / DE) | Typical case | Key rules | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Salary – *occupation salariée* / *nichtselbständige Arbeit* | A side job; a salary from one's own SARL | Flat costs of earning income (*frais d'obtention forfaitaires*) €540 a year without proof; commuting deducted separately (art. 107(1)) | [C · S2] |
| Pensions and annuities – *pensions ou rentes* | Early pension; annuity from a private pension plan | Flat costs €300 a year (doubled for spouses who each have one). An annuity from an art. 111bis contract is 50% exempt; a qualifying capital payout follows miscellaneous income and the arts. 131/132 conditions, including the extraordinary-income threshold | [C · S2; S6] |
| Capital income – *capitaux mobiliers* / *Kapitalvermögen* | Savings interest; dividends from one's own company | See below | [C · S2, S18] |
| Rental income – *location de biens* / *Vermietung und Verpachtung* | A rented flat; the own home (rental value zero) | home-and-rental.md | – |
| Miscellaneous – *revenus nets divers* / *sonstige Einkünfte* | An occasional brokering fee; shares, crypto-assets or property sold soon after buying | See below | [C · S2] |

## Capital income

- **Qualifying private interest within RELIBI** bears final 20% withholding and is not declared by assessment, subject to the specific small-savings exemption below. A Luxembourg bank address alone does not establish RELIBI: certain current/sight accounts are outside it and may remain assessable. [C · S18, S30]
- It is **not** final for interest that belongs to a business (e.g. on the business account): that interest is part of the profit, and the 20% is credited against income tax. [C · S18]
- Qualifying savings interest credited annually up to €250 per person and payer is exempt under RELIBI and not declared; above it all bears the final levy. This is not a universal exemption for interest outside RELIBI. [C · S30]
- **Dividends** from a fully taxable resident company (or a comparable treaty or EU company) are 50% exempt (art. 115 n° 15a); the first €1,500 a year of capital income taxed by assessment is exempt, €3,000 for spouses taxed jointly (art. 115 n° 15). [C · S2]
- Luxembourg companies withhold 15% on dividends (art. 148); a resident shareholder credits it against income tax (Form 100 page 19). [C · S2, S1]
- Flat costs €25 a year (doubled for jointly taxed spouses who each have such income). [C · S2]
- Capital losses normally stay within the category, but art. 97(5) has a substantial-shareholding/professional-income exception; test it before ring-fencing the loss (donations-pensions-and-investments.md). [C · S2]

## Miscellaneous income

- **Occasional services** not covered by another category (an occasional brokering or finder's fee): not taxable below €500 a year; a loss cannot be offset (art. 99 n° 3). [C · S2]
- **Speculation gains**: a private sale is speculative when the asset was held **5 years or less (real estate)** or **6 months or less (other assets, e.g. shares or crypto-assets)** (art. 99bis(1)); not taxable if the year's total gain is below €500 – a separate €500 threshold. [C · S2] Bill 8800 would double that holding period for movable assets from tax year 2027 – not law (figures/2027.md, watch list W-03). [O · S60]
- **Shares held privately for more than 6 months**: the gain is not taxable, unless it is a substantial holding – more than 10% at any time in the 5 years before the sale, counting the spouse or partner and minor children and indirect holdings (art. 100). Such a gain is taxed at half the global rate (arts 131(1) c), 132(2) 2°) after an allowance of €50,000, €100,000 for jointly taxed spouses, over 10 years (art. 130(4)). [C · S2 arts 99bis, 100, 130–132] Bill 8800 would change the 6 months to 12 from 2027 – not law (watch list W-03). [O]
- Temporary 2025 measure: real estate sold between 1 January and 30 June 2025 (to 30 September 2025 if the preliminary contract was registered by 30 June 2025) was speculative only if held 2 years or less. [C · S2]
- The ACD's « Revenus nets divers » page still mentions 2 years for real estate; the law says 5. [C · S2; S17 outdated]

## Volunteer indemnities

Indemnities for an accessory activity next to a main one – culture, sport, social work, an officer of a non-profit: up to €5,000 gross a year, flat expenses absorb them and nothing is declared; above €5,000, a flat €5,000 of expenses is deducted (higher actual costs allowed). If the main employer also pays for the volunteer work, it is salary. [C · S28 – circular of 2012, still linked by the ACD]

# Special expenses (dépenses spéciales)

_Figures in this file: 2025–2026. The year files in `figures/` win if they differ._

Special expenses (*dépenses spéciales* / *Sonderausgaben*) are private payments the law lets you deduct from total net income – only if they are neither business expenses nor costs of earning income (art. 109(1) LIR). [C · S2] Form 100 pages 13–16. [C · S1]

## Covered by the €480 flat minimum

A flat **€480** a year is deducted without proof for the items in this group; if the real total is higher, the real total counts (art. 113). [C · S2] For two jointly taxed salary recipients, the second amount is reduced where the lower salary after earning costs and related article 110 expenses is below €480. The tax-liability period also matters. A household with only one salary recipient, or only self-employed income, has one minimum. See payroll-certificate-deductions.md for the low-income rule and its negative-remainder boundary. [C · S82 art. 113; O · PIT-O-20]

| Item | Ceiling | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Interest on consumer loans (car, private goods, travel, building land not then built on) **and** premiums for life, death, accident, disability, health and liability insurance – one shared ceiling | **€672** a year, plus €672 for a jointly taxed spouse or partner and for each child giving right to a child reduction | [C · S2 art. 109(1) n° 1a; S4] |
| Material damage, theft, fire, glass and legal protection are not eligible private-insurance risks; split a bundled motor policy because its liability/bodily-injury portion may qualify | Ordinary shared ceiling applies only to eligible risks | [C · S143] |
| Home-savings contract (*épargne-logement* / *Bausparvertrag*), separate ceiling | **€672** per household member, **€1,344** if the youngest adult subscriber is 18–40 at the start of the year; the spouse or partner counts only if taxed jointly, a child only if giving right to a child reduction (art. 111(5)) | [C · S2] |
| Home-savings contract ended during the saving period, or money not used for housing | Earlier deductions are taken back: the years concerned are reassessed (art. 111(7)). The MyGuichet assistant asks what was done with the money of a contract that matured or ended in the year. | [C · S2; S61] |
| Private pension savings (*prévoyance-vieillesse*, art. 111bis and 111ter incl. PEPP), one ceiling for all contracts | Per person: see figures (€3,200 for 2025, €4,500 from 2026) | [C · S1; S6; S2] |
| Voluntary, continued or optional health and pension insurance, and buying back periods (*achat de périodes*), with a qualifying scheme | Article 110 n° 4 has no separate ceiling, but article 113(1) puts these payments **inside** the minimum comparison. Do not add them on top of an unreduced flat minimum. Eligibility: lu-social-security | [C · S82 arts. 110, 113; S118] |
| Maintenance to a divorced spouse | Up to €24,000 a year per ex-spouse (art. 109bis(2)) | [C · S2] |

- **Insurance conditions** (art. 111(1)–(4), (7)) [C · S2; S32]:
  - the insurer is authorised in Luxembourg, or authorised with its seat in another EU state; contributions to recognised mutual societies (*sociétés de secours mutuels*) count too;
  - a contract with a savings part (« garantie d'avantages en cas de vie ») counts only if its effective duration is **at least 10 years** and the premiums are actuarially calculated on the insured person; a unit-linked life contract must also cover death for at least 60% of the planned regular premiums (at least 5 yearly premiums) or 130% of the premiums paid;
  - premiums linked to a loan are excluded – unless the contract is more than 5 years old with unchanged regular premiums, or it secures a loan taken to buy an asset;
  - surrendering or assigning the contract brings the earlier deductions back (corrective assessment).
- **Single premium** for qualifying decreasing-capital mortgage death cover (*prime unique, solde restant dû*): the increased ceiling, age/child allocation, five-year history and financed-premium treatment are in [mortgage-death-insurance.md](mortgage-death-insurance.md), verified for 2025. It is separate from property-loan interest. [C · S72, S74, S77, S78]
- **Private pension contract** (art. 111bis) [C · S2; S6]:
  - a product made for the purpose by an insurer or a credit institution; each spouse's own contract has its own ceiling;
  - repayment deferred at least 10 years, paid at the earliest at 60 and at the latest at 75, as capital, a monthly life annuity, yearly withdrawals or a mix; a contract can be taken out until the day before the 65th birthday;
  - an earlier payout is taxable in full, except for serious illness or invalidity;
  - at payout: an annuity is 50% exempt, a qualifying capital payout follows miscellaneous income and the arts. 131/132 conditions, including the extraordinary-income threshold ([donations-pensions-and-investments.md](donations-pensions-and-investments.md)).
- Interest on a loan for **one's own home or a rented property is not a special expense**: it goes on the rental page (sheet L) – home-and-rental.md. [C · S4]
- Home-savings **contributions** generally include contractual/extra payments during the savings phase, credited interest and fees/commissions for concluding the contract. The exception is a conclusion commission tied to an already very concrete construction/purchase project, or to a contract concluded exclusively to obtain a loan directly producing rental-category income: it is then an earning cost in that category, not a special expense. Do not generalise the exception to all fees or all contracts. [C · S5; S145 §2.4.1–2.4.2, rechecked2026-11-04] Separate cash payments, credited interest, conclusion charges, routine administration charges and post-allocation loan movements in the private document record. A total bank movement is not automatically the qualifying contribution. Ordinary administration charges need their own supported classification; no blanket deduction/exclusion is inferred here. Credited interest also has a capital-income character; its contribution treatment does not itself establish a general income-tax exemption. [C · S145 §2.4.1 footnote2, §2.4.3–2.4.4; B for intake] The savings bank's yearly statement may show the eligible total as « dépenses avec avantage fiscal ». [P]
- Home-savings money must go to the saver's own home (incl. land, maintenance, integrated solar panels); using it for an office building or a rental flat is a non-qualifying use, and cancelling the contract within 10 years (except death or permanent incapacity) brings back the deductions. [C · S5]

## Outside the flat minimum

| Item | Rule | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Compulsory social-security contributions | Deductible without limit (art. 110 n° 2); dependency insurance excluded – lu-social-security, certificate-and-tax.md | [C · S32] |
| Supplementary pension of a **self-employed** person (RCPi, law of 8 June 1999) | Contributions for retirement deductible up to **20%** of the net professional income for which the person is compulsorily insured (art. 110 n° 3a); the scheme manager's certificate is required. The manager withholds a **final 20% tax** on the full contributions, which « ne peut ni être déduite de la base d'imposition, ni être imputée sur l'impôt sur le revenu » (art. 152, title 3); the benefits paid out are exempt (art. 115 n° 17a) | [C · S2; S19; S1 p. 16] |
| Supplementary pension – an employee's personal contributions | Up to €1,200 | [C · S1 p. 16] |
| Donations (*libéralités* / *Spenden*) to approved bodies (public utility, approved development NGOs, listed public institutions, approved social-impact companies, similar EU/EFTA bodies) | At least €120 in the year in total; at most 20% of total net income and €1,000,000; the excess can be carried over to the next two years | [C · S7; S2] |
| Business losses carried forward (art. 114) | 17 years (losses of 1991–2016 without time limit), oldest first, only by the person who made them; requires proper bookkeeping in the loss year | [C · S2] |

So an RCPi gains only the marginal rate minus the 20% withholding on each euro paid in, plus a tax-free payout – far less than a private pension contract within its ceiling. [I · S2] Reviewing what a household leaves unused: [deduction-review.md](deduction-review.md).

**Losses on the receipts-and-expenses method are not carried forward.** The ACD requires regular bookkeeping in the year of the loss – « L'annotation des recettes et des dépenses ne suffit pas » (circular L.I.R. n° 114/1) – and whoever keeps regular accounts is excluded from that method anyway (circular L.I.R. n° 18/5). Such a loss still offsets other income of the same year. Expecting losses in the first years? Keep full double-entry accounts from the start. [C · S62; lu-income-tax-business S21]

## Worked example (invented figures)

Couple taxed jointly, one self-employed, one employed, one child: insurance premiums €900 and car-loan interest €700 → ceiling 3 × €672 = €2,016, so €1,600 counts; home-savings €1,000 (saver aged 35) counts in full; private pension €2,000 (2025) counts in full. Total €4,600 is above the €480 minimum, so €4,600 is deducted, plus the compulsory contributions outside the minimum. [I · our arithmetic on S2]

Bill 8676 (single tax class from 2028) proposes €900 instead of €672 and €1,500 / €900 for home savings – status on the watch list W-05. [O · S33]

## Child multiplier (2025 evidence check)

For the ordinary insurance/consumer-interest and home-savings ceilings, count children giving this taxpayer an article 122 child reduction, not everyone living at the address. The youngest **adult subscriber** determines the home-savings age rate; a young child does not make an older adult subscriber eligible for that rate. [C · S72, arts. 111(5), 111(8)]

Donation recipient checks, EU/EFTA equivalence, in-kind restrictions and carryforward vintages/order: [donations-pensions-and-investments.md](donations-pensions-and-investments.md).

## Exact retirement dates and employment-contribution automation

Ordinary111bis/111ter contracts must satisfy ten years and the exact60th/75th-birthday limits cumulatively; subscription precedes the 65th birthday. Each contract is considered independently; a qualifying PEPP requires a Luxembourg sub-account. Pre2002 contracts have separate transitional rules. [C · S144 §3.1; S82]

The 2025 household tools now check these dates, require payment timing in subscription/maturity years, and stop legacy/leap-day ambiguity. Occupational employee contributions and voluntary statutory contributions use separate arrays and separate minimum/cap treatment. See lu-annual-return forms/2025/employment-deductions.md. Product/provider qualification still requires documentary review; dates do not prove it. [B]

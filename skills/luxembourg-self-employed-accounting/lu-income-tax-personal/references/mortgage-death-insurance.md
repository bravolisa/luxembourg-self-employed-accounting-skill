# Mortgage death insurance paid as one premium (2025)

_Figures in this file: 2025. The year file `figures/2025.md` wins if it differs._

Use this for **prime unique d'assurance temporaire au décès à capital décroissant** / **assurance solde restant dû** / **Restschuldversicherung**. It is a special-expense insurance deduction, separate from the loan interest on the property. The increased-ceiling rule is established by the regulation and ACD circular below. [C · S74, S77, S78]

## Eligibility and year

- The policy must secure a qualifying loan through decreasing death cover. Personal housing includes purchase, construction, enlargement, conversion and restoration; a qualifying business acquisition is also listed, but the age increase below is for personal housing. A pure death policy does not inherit the minimum duration for a life policy with a savings component. General insurance eligibility still applies: read `special-expenses.md`. A business expense or income-earning cost cannot also be deducted here. [C · S72 arts. 109, 111; S74 art. 3; S77 §§1–9]
- Deduct a premium actually paid in the tax year, not an unpaid amount due. It is not a once-in-a- lifetime entitlement. An amount that exceeds the allowed deduction does not carry forward.
  Annual or monthly premiums do not become a single premium just because a certificate gives an annual total. [C · S77 §§1, 9]
- For a mixed policy only the death-risk part qualifies for this increase. For a loan with several uses, establish the qualifying housing share; do not assume every amount included in a mortgage is a housing cost. Planned later occupation can qualify with evidence of genuine personal housing intent. This premium rule is not the own-home-interest availability rule. [C · S77 §9]

2025 basis: LIR art. 111(8), the RGD of 7 March 1969 art. 3 as replaced on 19 December 2008 (effective from 2009), ACD circular 111/1 of 2 November 2017, and the 2025 Form 100. The 2008 text's old reference to the ordinary ceiling in art. 111(5) must be read with the 2017 regrouping under art. 109(1) n° 1a; the circular explains that change. [C · S72, S74, S77 §9, S1]

## Calculation for a qualifying own-life policy

For each insured taxpayer with an eligible own-life contract, record the **completed age at subscription**, the eligible premium attributable to that person, assigned children, and the increase actually used for the same property in the preceding five tax years. Unknown historical use is not zero. [C · S74 art. 3; S77 §§9–11; B for the intake stop]

1. Base additional ceiling: the basic amount plus the increment for each assigned eligible child, using the [2025 figures](figures/2025.md).
2. For personal housing, increase that base by **8% for each completed year above age 30**, capped at **160% extra**. Thus the factor reaches 2.60 at 50, not 1.60.
3. Subtract the increases **actually deducted** for this taxpayer and this property during the preceding five tax years; floor the remaining increase at zero.
4. Limit the current increase to the person's qualifying premium actually paid.
5. Add the resulting individual increases to the household's ordinary shared insurance/consumer- interest ceiling. The deduction cannot exceed either that combined ceiling or all eligible actual insurance/consumer-interest payments, counted once. [C · S74 art. 3; S77 §§9–11]

For a 2025 payment, the lookback is **2020–2024**. It concerns the extra deduction above the ordinary ceiling that was actually used, not the old premium, a theoretical maximum, or an amount merely claimed. Obtain the assessment and supporting calculation where an earlier return alone does not establish this. [C · S77 §11; I for the document reconciliation]

Unused ordinary ceiling can absorb premium exceeding the individual increased ceiling. But spare theoretical increase cannot absorb excess ordinary premiums, and unused increased ceiling of one spouse cannot be transferred to the other's premium. Do not subtract the ordinary ceiling from the premium twice. [C · S77 §§9–11, worked examples]

## Spouses, partners and children

For jointly assessed spouses/partners, compute the increase individually when each has a policy or both lives are insured. Each eligible child is allocated **once**, to either adult; this allocation is separate from the ordinary household multiplier. Do not automatically allocate every child to the older adult: actual premiums and previous use can change the useful allocation. [C · S74 art. 3(3); S77 §§9–11; I for comparing allocations]

For a two-life contract, use its individual-risk split or the insurer's detailed evidence. The circular permits an office to estimate half each when that detail is absent **only if this is not manifestly unrealistic**, particularly given the age difference. A joint contract is not a blanket permission to assume 50/50. [C · S77 §9]

The published age table assumes the taxpayer subscribed a policy on their own life. Where the subscriber and insured adult differ, verify who can deduct and the applicable age before calculating the increase (PIT-O-16); do not silently choose either person's age. Multiple properties, unclear mixed-use allocation, incomplete previous assessments and non-resident entitlement require case review.
[C · S77 §§3, 9–11 for the distinction; B for the review boundary]

## If the premium is financed

A loan used to pay the premium is economically linked to insurance. Its interest belongs to the special-expense interest category, subject to that category's shared limit; it is not all property interest just because the bank financed it with the house. The premium itself must have been paid to the insurer. Principal repayments are not a second premium deduction. [C · S77 §§1, 9]

The circular demonstrates allocation of both the premium and interest where a loan finances the home, furniture and the premium. It also describes the office's discretion over negligible allocations. Record the actual uses and the chosen evidenced treatment; never turn that discretion into an automatic exemption from allocation. [C · S77 §9; B for documentation]

## Worked examples (invented figures, 2025)

Assume eligible own-life personal-housing cover, evidenced payment, and no prior increased deduction unless stated. These are deduction calculations, not final tax/refund estimates. [I · arithmetic on S74, S77]

| Case | Working | Insurance/interest deduction |
|---|---|---|
| Single, age 33 at subscription, no children; €9,000 premium and €700 other eligible premiums | Extra limit 6,000 × 1.24 = 7,440; combined ceiling 672 + 7,440 = 8,112; actual 9,700 | **€8,112** |
| Same age/profile, €7,000 premium and no other payment | Combined ceiling 672 + min(7,000, 7,440) = 7,672; actual 7,000. Increased deduction actually used: 7,000 − 672 = 6,328 | **€7,000** |
| Single, age 38, €5,000 current premium; increased deduction actually used in 2020 for the same home €6,328 | 6,000 × 1.64 − 6,328 = 3,512; plus ordinary 672 | **€4,184** |
| Joint couple, one child; age 29 adult alone has €12,000 premium; €2,500 ordinary premiums; child assigned to that adult | Extra 7,200; ordinary 3 × 672 = 2,016; no second insured adult's increase | **€9,216** |
| Joint couple, two children, both ages 50; each €25,000 premium, one child allocated each; €3,000 ordinary premiums | Each extra (6,000 + 1,200) × 2.60 = 18,720; ordinary 2,688; actual 53,000 | **€40,128** |

If an eligible premium is below its theoretical increase, add only that actual premium to the ordinary ceiling. For example, single age 40, €2,000 premium and €1,500 ordinary payments:
combined ceiling 672 + 2,000 = **€2,672**, not the full age-based maximum. [I · S77 §9]

## Evidence, declaration and software boundary

Collect the policy, subscription date, insurer's premium/payment certificate, identities and birth dates of subscribers and insured lives, risk split, loan purpose/allocation, child entitlement and chosen allocation, and the relevant earlier assessments/calculations. Record a confirmed absence of prior use explicitly. This is an intake checklist, not a claim that every item is a mandatory upload in every return. [B, based on S77; mandatory attachments: S76]

Form 100 page 14: premium in the insurance rows; **1474/1475 are housing-purpose checkboxes**, not amounts; **1476/1477 are child counts**. The correctly combined ceiling goes in **1470**, and the allowed total in **1471**. See `forms/2025/form-100.md` for PDF/XML mapping. [C · S1]

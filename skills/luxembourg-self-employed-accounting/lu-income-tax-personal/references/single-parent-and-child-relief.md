# Single parents and child relief

_Figures in this file: 2025–2026. The year files in `figures/` win if they differ.
Form references below are for 2025. The 2026 consolidation S2 corroborates the unchanged credit amounts and both years of the transitional bonus._

## Establish the entitlement before calculating

| Relief | What it does | Main evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Child moderation (*modération d'impôt pour enfant*) | Usually already embedded in family allowance, study aid or volunteer aid; otherwise a requested non-refundable tax rebate | Child's age/studies, tax-household assignment, benefits awarded during the year |
| Class 1a | Changes the tax scale; it is not itself a credit | Civil status, class-2 transition, child moderation in the tax household, or the other class-1a grounds |
| CIM (*crédit d'impôt monoparental*) | Refundable single-parent credit, subject to income and child-support tests | Qualifying child, both parents' living arrangements, support per child, CIM already paid |
| Child bonus (*bonification d'impôt pour enfant*) | Separate non-refundable credit after moderation ends, or the special shared-residence transition | Prior moderation, earlier bonuses, household assignment, and shared family-allowance evidence where relevant |

[C · S82 arts 119, 122–123bis, 154ter; S80, S83]

Neither a benefit certificate nor a child listed on last year's return establishes all these rights.
Determine each child’s fiscal household for the target year. A child bonus alone does not establish child moderation, class 1a, CIM or the child multiplier for deductions. [I · S82]

## CIM: eligibility and annual calculation

A claimant must be unmarried, in class 1a because of at least one qualifying child in their tax household, and resident or assimilated to a resident. Age-based or widowhood-based class 1a alone does not suffice. Married people, including individually taxed spouses and people marrying during the year, cannot claim CIM. Nor can class-2 taxpayers during the divorce/widowhood transition or registered partners choosing joint taxation. [C · S80, S81, S82]

Both parents sharing a home with their common child rules out CIM **for that child**. An unrelated new partner is not automatically disqualifying. Where a couple also has a child from an earlier relationship whose other parent lives elsewhere, that child can establish CIM eligibility in principle; verify the other conditions and support amounts. [C · S80]

For a full year, use adjusted taxable income `R` (after abatements, not gross pay): [C · S82]

| R | Annual CIM before the child-support reduction |
|---|---|
| Below €60,000 | €3,504 |
| €60,000 through €105,000 | €3,504 − (R − €60,000) × 0.0612 |
| Above €105,000 | €750 |

The threshold formulas meet: exactly €60,000 gives €3,504; exactly €105,000 gives €750.
It is one credit per taxpayer, not per child. [C · S81, S82]

Reduce it by half the relevant child's annual support above €2,712 (monthly threshold €226).
Support includes maintenance and direct payment of upkeep, education or training costs, whether paid to the child or parent. Family benefits and orphan pensions are excluded. For several children, use the lowest relevant support amount per child, not their total. [C · S80–S82] A reduction that exhausts the credit is read as no entitlement, not a new tax charge; retain unusually large or irregular support cases for review. [I · S82]

Part-year tax liability invokes a whole-month proportion of the maximum; this is not the number of months the person worked, received a tax card or had the child. Annual assessment and payroll credits are different calculations. For a move or mixed eligibility/support period, retain dates and obtain a case-specific calculation; do not extrapolate the full-year examples. [C · S82 for whole months; B for the review boundary]

CIM already paid by an employer/pension fund must be reconciled against final entitlement. A request after the year may cover unpaid or partly paid CIM, but does not give a second full credit.
Non-residents assimilated to residents claim through assessment, not through the annual salary adjustment. [C · S81, S82 arts 154bis, 154ter]

Keep the annual credit formula separate from tariff rounding. The circular confirms upward-cent rounding for its monthly payroll example; this research has not established the annual fractional-cent convention or a general instruction to use the tariff's rounded income in credit thresholds. PIT-O-17 records that remaining calculation question. [C · S80 for payroll; O]

## Child moderation and assignment

If a qualifying benefit already supplied the moderation, there is no second rebate and no residual rebate for months without it. Even one family-allowance payment in the year excludes the separate rebate for that child. Where no qualifying allowance/study/volunteer aid was awarded, request the alternative rebate, limited to tax due. [C · S82, S84, S88]

A child belongs to one tax household for the year. For alternating residence with joint parental authority, the prior assignment continues unless expressly renounced. If the child belonged to both parents or neither in the preceding year, the parents jointly designate the beneficiary.
Check the particular assignment rules rather than allocating half the child to each return.
Form 104 records the relevant designation and irrevocable renunciation for 2025. [C · S82, S85]

When both parents in qualifying alternating residence receive the first annual family allowance, common siblings are grouped with the same parent using the oldest qualifying child's prior-year moderation, unless the permitted renunciation/designation changes it. They cannot select one common child each to create two fiscal households. The annual choice is irrevocable. For unmarried cohabiting parents, the separate 2016 regulation also groups common children; it uses the oldest child's first allowance payment and prescribed continuity/designation rules for adult-paid benefits, study/volunteer aid and rebate cases. Preserve both parents' benefit records and distinguish own children from common children. [C · S86, S87]

Splitting the family allowance does not split fiscal household membership. The 2025–2026 special bonus below is a distinct response for the other household. Cohabitation, siblings, previous assignment and individual taxation must be checked before selecting a household. [C · S82; I for the intake sequence]

## Child bonus: ordinary and transitional routes

**Ordinary route:** moderation expired at the end of either of the two preceding tax years. For 2025, check expiry at the end of 2023 or 2024. The same child can produce only two successive bonuses; renewed studies do not restart an exhausted entitlement. The claimant cannot combine bonus and moderation for the same child and year. There is no separate minimum age test replacing these conditions. The parent whose household included the child when moderation ended claims;
if both did, they designate one beneficiary for each year. [C · S82, S83, S85]

**Transitional route, 2025 and 2026 only:** a child lives alternately under two roofs, both adults exercise joint parental authority, and both are recipients of the child's family allowance. The parent whose fiscal household **does not** include the child may request the art. 123bis(1a) bonus.
It is not a second moderation, and does not give both parents CIM or the child ceiling increases.
The other parent's moderation need not have expired. [C · S82, S83; I for consequences]

The July 2025 LIR snapshot predates this provision. Use the 2025 consolidation updated on 2 February 2026, which incorporates the law of 19 December 2025, rather than interpreting the older snapshot's silence as an exclusion. [C · S82]

For no more than five children giving right to moderation **or** bonus, per eligible bonus child:
[C · S82 art. 123bis(3)]

| Adjusted taxable income R | Bonus before the tax-due limit |
|---|---|
| R ≤ €67,400 | €922.50 |
| €67,400 < R ≤ €76,600 | ( €76,600 − R ) / 10 |
| R > €76,600 | Nil |

The formula applies separately to each eligible bonus child. At exactly €67,400 the full amount applies; just above it the statutory formula does **not** start at €922.50. Do not invent a smooth taper. If at least six children give right to moderation or bonus, the income limitation does not apply. Count both groups, not just children claiming bonus. The total credit remains limited to tax due and is not refundable. [C · S82, S83]

## Documents, output and limits

Collect the current-year family-allowance/study/volunteer evidence; custody and residence facts;
prior tax-household assignment and any signed designation; maintenance and directly paid costs per child; full salary/pension certificates including CIM paid; tax-liability dates; and prior bonus years. An older return is a lead, not proof of current-year eligibility. [B]

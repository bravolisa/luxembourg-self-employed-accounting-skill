# Household and tax class

_Figures in this file: 2025–2026. The year files in `figures/` win if they differ._

## The three tax classes

| Class (*classe d'impôt* / *Steuerklasse*) | Who | Status |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | Everyone not in class 1a or 2 | [C · S2] |
| **1a** | Widowed people; people who receive the child tax reduction for a child in their household; people who were 64 or older on 1 January – unless they fall in class 2 | [C · S2] |
| **2** | Couples taxed jointly (married, or registered partners who ask for it); and, for three tax years, people widowed, divorced or judicially separated in the three years before | [C · S2] |

## Married couples and partners

- **Married residents meeting the joint-assessment conditions, including no qualifying legal/judicial separation,** are taxed jointly (*imposition collective*) by default; a couple who marries during the year is taxed jointly for that whole year. [C · S2]
- Since 2018 a married couple can jointly ask for **individual taxation** – "pure" (each on their own income) or with reallocation of the common income; each spouse is then in class 1. [C · S52] The request must reach the tax office by **31 December of the year after** the tax year and cannot be withdrawn once made after the year. [C · S2] On Form 100: boxes 316–323. [C · S1]
- **Registered partners** (law of 9 July 2004, or a comparable foreign partnership) are taxed jointly only **on joint request**, if the partnership lasted the whole year (1 January – 31 December) and they lived together all year. [C · S2]
- **Unmarried couples without a registered partnership** are two separate tax households. [C · S38]
- A resident married to a **non-resident**: joint taxation only on joint request and only if the resident spouse earns at least 90% of the household's professional income in Luxembourg. [C · S2]
- Spouses or partners taxed jointly are **jointly liable** for the whole tax; the balance cannot be split between them. [C · S38]
- The extra-professional abatement for qualifying couples who both work (also when one is self-employed), limited by each spouse's relevant net income: extraordinary-charges-and-abatements.md. [C · S2]

## Separation, divorce, death

- For resident spouses, living apart **without** a qualifying legal or court authorisation does not end joint taxation: the spouses stay in class 2 and jointly liable, even at different addresses. [C · S38] This holds in every later year too; without court proceedings the only way to be taxed apart is the joint request for individual taxation. [C · S197]
- **The year of the court's permission is still a joint year.** The conditions of joint taxation are tested on 1 January (« à la date clé du 1er janvier »): spouses who on that day are married, both resident and not yet living apart under a court dispensation are taxed jointly for the whole year, even if the court lets them live apart later that year. [C · S82 art. 3; S197; S38]
- After divorce or court-authorised separation, class 2 continues for the **three tax years after the year separate residence was authorised** (not the year of the divorce judgment), unless already used in the previous five years. [C · S55] In those three years the person is assessed alone on his or her own income with the class-2 tariff (« sans qu'il y ait imposition collective des conjoints »): one person's ceilings, no extra-professional abatement, no single-parent credit – table in family-changes.md. [C · S55]
- Where resident spouses meet the joint-assessment conditions and have not validly chosen individual assessment, the year of a spouse's death is their last joint year; the survivor keeps class 2 for the next three years and then normally moves to class 1a. [C · S38] Class 1a then applies with or without a child (art. 119, n° 2 a)). [C · S82]

For the 2025 event-year timeline, foreign partnership proof, mixed-residence test, non-resident divorce exceptions and maintenance, read [family-changes.md](family-changes.md).
Do not apply the resident separation summary indiscriminately to non-residents. [B]

## Children

- The **child tax reduction** (*modération d'impôt pour enfant*) is paid inside the family allowance (or state study aid, volunteer aid) and is deemed granted to the taxpayer in whose household the child lives. [C · S2] The separate "boni pour enfant" ended in July 2016; the CAE (Zukunftskeess) pays it with the allowance. [C · S51]
- A child counts if part of the household during the year and **under 21 on 1 January**; from 21 only in continuous full-time vocational studies of more than a year (or disabled with continued allowance). [C · S2]
- If no allowance or aid was paid for a child, the parent can ask for the reduction as a **tax rebate of €922.50** per child, set only against tax due (not refunded). [C · S2] Form 100 page 2. [C · S1]
- A child belongs to **one tax household** a year. In 2025 alternating residence with joint parental authority, the previous assignment continues unless that parent expressly renounces it; if the prior household was both parents or neither, the parents jointly designate the beneficiary. Other moves follow the rules in art. 123(3), not a universal 50/50 split. [C · S72]
- **Child tax bonus** (*bonification d'impôt pour enfant*): ordinary post-moderation relief and a distinct 2025–2026 shared-residence route for the parent **outside whose fiscal household** the child is assigned. It is non-refundable. Exact income boundaries, six-child exception, prior claims and the shared-allowance conditions: [single-parent-and-child-relief.md](single-parent-and-child-relief.md). [C · S82, S83]
- Single parents in class 1a: [single-parent-and-child-relief.md](single-parent-and-child-relief.md); CIM needs its own eligibility and support tests. [C · S80–S82]

## Evidence needed for a 2025 child

Check birth date, actual household assignment, allowance/study/volunteer aid, and the age-21+ study or continued-allowance condition separately. Children born during 2025 count in the ordinary age category. A married/partnered child not separated in fact, or a child who has children, has a separate household even at the same address (art. 123(3)). [C · S72]

The page-2 checkbox requests the alternative rebate; it is not a generic dependent flag. Allowance or aid already containing the reduction means no second rebate; even one family-allowance payment excludes a residual rebate for months without it. [C · S88] The reduction affects eligible household multipliers, while the rebate itself offsets tax rather than taxable income. A qualifying single parent's class 1a does not establish CIM entitlement. [C · S72; S1]

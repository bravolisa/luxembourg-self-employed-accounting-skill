# Foreign household costs — 2025

Figures in this file: 2025 — the year files win if they differ. Read on 2026-11-04.
France, Germany and Belgium: check the Luxembourg assessment route, then the nature of each cost. A foreign tax certificate is evidence, not automatic Luxembourg eligibility.

## Eligibility before amounts

Without assimilation, earning costs need a direct economic link to Luxembourg-source income;
private deductions are restricted. Article 127 extraordinary charges and CIM are unavailable, but **article 127bis support for a child outside the household remains a separate available route** if its conditions hold. Specified compulsory contributions and the special-expense minimum are not universally lost. [C · S82, S139]

With assimilation, both spouses' foreign income enters the rate calculation. Positive and negative foreign income must be reconstructed under **Luxembourg tax rules**; do not simply copy the taxable-income total from the foreign assessment. Establish the thresholds using [cross-border-income.md](cross-border-income.md). [C · S82, S138]

## Kindergarten, crèche and childcare abroad

An EU provider can qualify when approved by the competent authority in its own country.
Verify the actual childcare service and approval; the foreign word for the institution is not decisive. The child must give the taxpayer Luxembourg child moderation and be **under fourteen on 1 January 2025**; the age limit does not apply to disabled children.
[C · S74]

For **qualifying childcare fees**, the Luxembourg circular says not to extract an assumed upkeep, education or training component. Do not import a French food exclusion or German domestic tax formula into Luxembourg's allowance. Ordinary schooling or tuition is not made eligible merely by calling it childcare. Separate school tuition, lessons, care and canteen-only charges where separately invoiced; the treatment of every mixed foreign school invoice or standalone meal bill has not been established. [C · S73, S82] [O for the unresolved mixed invoices]

Subtract actual third-party reimbursements. Apply the monthly actual-cost test and the one common allowance for childcare, domestic help and dependency care, using the ceilings in `figures/2025.md`. Several children do not multiply the allowance. The payment-year rule does not settle how an advance or arrears payment spanning months uses the monthly cap: PIT-O-15 remains open. [C · S73, S82]

Keep three separate records:

1. Targeted grants, employer support or refunds paying part of the actual care bill.
2. Ordinary family benefits and any CAE differential-benefit decision.
3. Foreign tax credits, reductions or deductions generated by the same expense. [B]

France's own childcare credit removes targeted CMG support and can be refunded above tax due;
its food rule is a French rule. Germany's relief is a special-expense deduction, with its own employer-subsidy adjustment. These facts do **not** establish whether or how Luxembourg must net a French credit, Belgian reduction or German deduction. Do not subtract a theoretical tax saving, a full family-benefit statement or a later refund by an invented formula. Record the amount, legal type, expense year and payment year, then retain the unresolved interaction for review. [C · S146, S147, S148] [O for Luxembourg treatment]

## Insurance contracts in another EU country

An insurer authorised and seated in another EU state can qualify; Luxembourg establishment is not required. The ACD generally accepts approval identified on the policy schedule. Read the contract and annual certificate, including each covered risk. [C · S82, S143]

Distinguish **policyholder**, **insured**, **beneficiary** and **bank-transfer payer**. The policyholder who contracted and undertook to pay the premium claims the deduction. A third party paying on their behalf does not transfer that deduction. The beneficiary's identity does not itself bar eligibility. Covered persons are the taxpayer, spouse/partner and qualifying children; joint assessment is not itself required merely for the spouse/partner to be an eligible insured person. Assessment-route restrictions and ceiling allocation still apply.
[C · S143, S82]

Complementary health cover can qualify. For a bundled motor policy, obtain the premium split:
liability and bodily-injury components may qualify, while material damage, theft, fire, glass and legal protection do not qualify as these private special expenses. Do not reject the whole bundle or deduct it all. Keep any business portion separate. [C · S143]

Foreign life savings need the minimum effective contract term and actuarial conditions;
unit-linked products also need the statutory death cover. A product name such as assurance-vie does not prove compliance. Use the complete ordinary life/loan-linked/single-premium conditions in `special-expenses.md` and `mortgage-death-insurance.md`, including the alternative death-cover branches. There is no separate more generous foreign-policy ceiling. [C · S82]

## Foreign pensions and home savings

A foreign retirement provider must be eligible and authorised to operate in Luxembourg, and the product must be specifically designed for article 111bis. Obtain its annual certificate of compliance. A PEPP needs article 111ter compliance and payments into the Luxembourg sub-account. A French PER, German Riester/Rürup or Belgian pension-savings label is not proof of equivalence. Apply the **2025** year-file ceiling; the later increase is not retroactive.
[C · S144]

Home savings require a qualifying *caisse d'épargne-logement / Bausparkasse* authorised in Luxembourg or another EU state, not any bank selling a similarly named account. Qualifying use can include an owned ordinary principal home **abroad**. Apply contract/subscriber and use conditions; payment by somebody else does not itself confer the deduction. A Bausparvertrag is a candidate to verify; a PEL/CEL or ordinary savings account is not automatically equivalent.
[C · S82, S145]

## Interest on the foreign principal home

For an assimilated non-resident, qualifying foreign principal-home interest generates a **foreign rental loss in the worldwide rate base**. It can reduce the rate applied to Luxembourg income; it is not a direct reduction of the Luxembourg-source base. Recompute the qualifying loss under Luxembourg rules instead of copying a foreign home deduction. [C · S139, S138]

Apply the existing home rules: occupation anchors historical years through 2022, while effective availability anchors 2023 onwards; distinguish pre-availability interest/loan-opening costs, secondary homes, letting and personal use. Reconcile state assistance and employer support separately using employer-interest-support.md; do not subtract a taxable employer benefit as if it were exempt assistance. The annual home-interest ceiling is not prorated merely because occupation lasts part of the year. A co-owner's use must correspond to their legal rights. [C · S1, S22, S3, S21]

Collect legal ownership/shares, borrower, actual interest burden, loan purpose and assessment election separately. A bank certificate alone does not prove them all. On a joint return a sole owner and borrower uses the whole family ceiling (home-and-rental.md) [I · S27, S64]; unequal shares or a borrower who is not the owner remain PIT-O-14. [B; O · PIT-O-14]

Trace refinancing to the original qualifying debt and any extra borrowing. Do not presume a new unrestricted-interest period simply because the bank contract is new: the ordinary rule is tied to the property's availability. Refinancing set-up costs are not deductible once the rental value is fixed, and extra borrowing is split by the actual use of the money (home-and-rental.md); a redemption penalty is not established (PIT-O-31). [I · S27, S3, S187; O for the penalty]

The foreign-property entries use exempt-income columns; the loan details ask for purpose, year-end debt and the reconciled interest base after the correct state/employer-support treatment. See the manual form map. The old foreign-home circular is evidence for the rate mechanism, not permission to reuse its obsolete notional-rent figures or ceilings. [I · S1; C · S1]

## Children and foreign family benefits

One child belongs to one Luxembourg fiscal household for the year. Form 100's already-granted moderation refers to CAE family allowance and Luxembourg study/volunteer aid. Foreign tax-child assignment and a foreign benefit certificate do not by themselves decide Luxembourg moderation or the eligible child multipliers. [C · S82; I · S1]

Obtain CAE entitlement/payment/refusal and differential-benefit decisions, foreign benefit certificates by recipient and period, custody/residence facts and prior Luxembourg assignment.
Do not grant a second rebate merely because most benefits were paid abroad. Where CAE pays a zero differential because foreign benefits are higher, the precise moderation/assignment interaction remains open. [B; O]

|---|---|---|---|---|
| Approved childcare | Crèche/childminder approval | Kindergarten/daycare approval | Care-provider approval | EU approval, actual service, child moderation, monthly net payment |
| Relief on same care bill | Credit and CMG recorded separately | Tax deduction and employer grant separately | Tax reduction and actual refunds separately | Foreign tax-relief interaction remains open; do not equate it with a reimbursement |
| Private insurance | Contract and risk split | Contract and risk split | Contract and risk split | EU authorisation, policyholder, insured, risk and contract conditions |
| Principal-home loan | Deed, loan, interest/subsidies | Same | Same | Qualifying foreign loss affects the worldwide rate base |
| Retirement/home savings | PER/PEL/CEL name insufficient | Riester/Rürup/Bauspar name insufficient | Pension/savings name insufficient | Specific statutory product or institution requirements |

The matrix applies the sourced rules above; it does not approve any named provider or product. [B]

## Worked examples — invented amounts

- Approved French care costs EUR 400 monthly with EUR 100 targeted monthly reimbursement:
  EUR 300 monthly, EUR 3,600 for twelve months, **before** resolving any separate later French tax credit. No invented meal haircut or subtraction of all family allowances. [I]
- A EUR 1,200 German motor policy identifies EUR 450 liability and EUR 150 bodily injury:
  EUR 600 is potentially eligible before the ordinary shared ceiling and other conditions;
  the remaining material/legal cover is not eligible under that private-insurance route. [I]
- A Belgian household has EUR 60,000 Luxembourg professional net income and EUR 40,000 Belgian professional net income, all already computed under Luxembourg rules. The 60% household share can satisfy Belgium's alternative threshold even when neither adult passes an individual ordinary test. Qualifying Belgian home interest affects the rate base, not LU-source profit. [I]
- A non-assimilated German resident's non-household-child support must be tested under article 127bis; it is not rejected solely because ordinary childcare under article 127 is unavailable. [I]

These are component expectations. They are not complete returns, professional sign-off or evidence that the automatic household calculator supports these branches. [B]

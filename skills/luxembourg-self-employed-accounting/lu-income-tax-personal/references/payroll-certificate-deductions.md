# Payroll certificates, tax-card deductions and several workplaces

Figures in this file: 2025 – the year files win if they differ.

Read this before translating Model 160 or an employer's equivalent French/German certificate into annual deductions. The certificate reports payroll operations; annual eligibility and limits must be established separately. Document language does not establish the income's source country. [B]

## Preserve the certificate's arithmetic

Model 160 gross includes cash/in-kind remuneration and employer-advanced sickness payments.
Separate salary/sickness components must reconcile to the printed subtotal; do not add that subtotal once again. Section J identifies CNS interruptions not already liquidated by the employer and included in section A; obtain a direct-payer certificate where needed. [C · S112 notes 2,6]

Model 160 contribution line 10 excludes dependency contributions already. Its deductible line 16 subtracts the separately identified non-deductible contribution lines, including those linked to partially exempt remuneration. Read the actual labels; do not subtract dependency a second time from a number already labelled deductible. The withholding base is after the certificate's applicable deductions/exemptions, not invariably gross minus contributions minus FD. Positive gross can coexist with a zero withholding base. [C · S112; I for the arithmetic implication]

Tax withheld and credits already paid are separate certificate amounts. See employee-tax-credits.md.
A payroll “imposable” amount is neither annual gross nor final annual taxable income. [C · S112;
S124; B for preparation]

## AC and annual spouse allowances

AC bundles the salary-cost flat, special-expense minimum and extra-professional allowance on the relevant additional tax card. Standard 2025 amount is EUR 5,520 annually, EUR 460 monthly, EUR 18.40 daily; FD is separate. These are payroll figures, not an automatic Form 100 deduction.
A changed card or correction can alter the certificate's cumulative deductions; an unusual amount needs the payroll/card explanation, not replacement by a guessed standard. [C · S115; S117; I for handling unexplained differences]

For the annual return:

- Apply the employee cost minimum once per person, across all salary certificates. Article 107(5) caps it at receipts remaining after FD. A short job does not itself prorate the annual minimum:
  article 107(2) uses the **period of tax liability**, with whole-month reduction when liability did not last the year. [C · S82 art. 107]
- The special-expense minimum is doubled for two qualifying salary recipients, but article 113(2) reduces it where the lower spouse's salary after earning costs and related article 110 expenses is below the individual minimum. Do not apply an unconditional doubled amount. A negative remainder is a manual-review boundary, PIT-O-20; no unsupported floor is invented. [C · S82 art. 113; O for negative-remainder treatment]
- Article 129b requires qualifying income and compulsory affiliation. The joint extra-professional allowance is limited by **each spouse's relevant net amount**, after the specified article 110 expenses and special-expense minimum; relevant cessation allowances also reduce that amount.
  The liability-period rule is separate from employment duration. “Both work” alone does not establish the full annual allowance. Read extraordinary-charges-and-abatements.md. [C · S82 art. 129b; S117]

## Commuting across employers and workplaces

Use official commune/distance units, the current-year free units/rate/ceiling and the employee's actual regular work pattern. LIR 105bis caps FD at salary receipts. Claiming the full annual amount on every certificate duplicates the deduction. The 1990 regulation, read with its 1992/2001 amendments, supplies the multi-place method. [C · S82 art. 105bis; S119–S121]

| Regular work pattern | Global distance units |
|---|---|
| Different workplaces in the same day | Half the sum of home→first workplace, the between-workplace distances, and last workplace→home. |
| Whole days at different workplaces | Weight each distance by occupation duration relative to the person's normal weekly/monthly work period. |
| A mixture of these patterns | Combine the same-day and whole-day methods. |

These methods require regular, continuous activity and known frequency. The 1992 amendment removes the original non-reimbursement exclusion; do not reuse the unamended 1990 text on that point.
Employer reimbursement nevertheless has its own salary/exemption treatment; see S122 and the relevant conditions instead of assuming every reimbursement is exempt. [C · S119 art. 2; S120; S122]

Temporary/mobile sites lasting at most nine months and specified travelling activities use the employer's business address; attachment to qualifying longer-term/fixed workplaces matters under article 1. Isolated days and changes within the year need the prescribed payroll-period treatment;
do not turn a few paid days into a full annual deduction. The daily euro amount replaces the old franc figure under the 2001 amendment. [C · S119–S121; S82 art. 105bis]

A distance increase after a residence/workplace change takes effect at the beginning of the month of change. A decrease during 2025 does not reduce the 2025 allowance. Partial-year tax liability uses whole liability months; qualifying sickness/maternity/accident periods have the statutory continuation rule. These are not a universal one-twelfth-per-job formula. [C · S82 art. 105bis; S117]

Retain dated workplace schedules, frequencies, moves and the annual computation, then allocate that reviewed amount to the MyGuichet salary records. The helper's single-distance calculation does not implement these schedules or establish legal entitlement. Fractional-unit/period details remain under PIT-O-23 where the source does not settle rounding. Preserve exact intermediate units and request review instead of inventing a rounding convention. [B/O]

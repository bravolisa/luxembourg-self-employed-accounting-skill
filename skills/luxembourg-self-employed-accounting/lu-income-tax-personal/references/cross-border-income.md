# Cross-border income — France, Germany and Belgium, 2025

Figures in this file: 2025 — the year files win if they differ. Read on 2026-11-04.
Ordinary private employment and individual business/professional income. Public service, directors' fees, transport workers, pensions, replacement pay and investments need their own treaty category. This guide does not implement a non-resident tax calculator.

## Four separate decisions

1. **Domestic residence:** a Luxembourg tax domicile or habitual abode creates domestic resident status; domestic non-residents receive Luxembourg-source income without either. Residents are within worldwide taxation, subject to treaty relief. [C · S82]
2. **Treaty residence:** if both states claim residence, apply the treaty sequence: permanent home, then closer personal/economic ties where both states provide a home, then habitual abode, nationality and competent-authority agreement as required. Do not replace the whole sequence with an address or a single centre-of-interests assertion. [C · S132, S133, S134]
3. **Income allocation:** classify each stream and apply its treaty rule. Employer address, business registration, bank account, payroll withholding and CCSS affiliation are not substitutes for the applicable income rule. [C · S82; I · S132]
4. **Assimilation/assessment:** an eligible non-resident may request resident-style treatment, with worldwide income used to determine the Luxembourg rate. This does not change factual or treaty residence. [C · S82]

For a Luxembourg business owner living abroad, the historical StAnpG section 14(3) domestic residence question remains **PIT-O-11**. Re-reading the original text did not establish its present application; do not declare it repealed or automatically apply it. [O · S67]

## Ordinary salary: work location and short stays

Ordinary salary starts from where work is physically performed: France and Germany treaty article 14, Belgium article 15. A foreign payslip can still relate to Luxembourg-taxable work, and a Luxembourg payslip can contain foreign-taxable work. [C · S132, S133, S134; I · S132]

The ordinary short-stay exception has **three cumulative conditions**: presence in the work state does not exceed **183 days** in a twelve-month period beginning or ending in the relevant year; the employer is not resident in that state; and remuneration is not borne by its permanent establishment there (Belgium also names a fixed base). The treaty texts must be read together with any special labour-hire or employer rules. [C · S132, S133, S134]

This is a presence test, not the working-day tolerance below. Work for an ordinary Luxembourg employer does not become residence-country taxable merely because the worker spent fewer than 183 days in Luxembourg: the employer condition fails. Economic-employer, secondment and cost-recharge disputes require separate review. [I]

## Self-employment and a Luxembourg registration

| Treaty residence | Ordinary independent activity |
|---|---|
| France | Business definition includes liberal/independent services; articles 5/7 require a permanent establishment and attributable profit. [C · S132; I · S132] |
| Germany | Business definition includes liberal/independent activity; articles 5/7 require the relevant establishment and attributable profit. [C · S134] |
| Belgium | Commercial profit follows articles 5/7; liberal professions have article 14's habitually available fixed-base rule, limited to attributable income. [C · S133] |

A registration, permit or Luxembourg customer is not proof of an actual establishment/base.
Check premises at the person's disposal, actual habitual use, work performed, contracts and profit attribution. Dependent-agent and other treaty establishment rules also exist; no rented office is not by itself proof of no establishment. Employee day tolerances do not provide self-employed people with 34 tax-free home-working days. [I · S132] [I for evidence/routing]

Domestic article 156's exercised-or-exploited liberal-profession source rule can be broader than the treaty restriction. Apply both layers. Treaty “business profits” terminology does not decide Luxembourg domestic commercial versus liberal classification, Form 110/152 or municipal business tax. [C · S82, S132, S134, S133] [I for classification guard]

Do not allocate sole-trader profit using an invented employee workday fraction. Establish the functions, assets, risks and activity attributable to each relevant establishment/base. [B]

## Assimilation: three tests and one limited fiction

| Route | Calculation | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Article 157ter ordinary | Luxembourg-taxable income / whole-calendar-year worldwide income, all categories, for the qualifying person | **At least 90%** |
| Article 157ter small foreign income | Qualifying non-resident taxpayer's net income not subject to LU income tax | **Strictly below EUR 13,000** |
| Belgian treaty alternative | Luxembourg-taxable household professional income / worldwide household professional income | **Strictly more than 50%** |

These alternatives are not cumulative. Professional income includes commercial, agricultural, liberal-profession, salary and pension/annuity categories. Use Luxembourg-law category amounts, not bank net pay after withholding or a foreign taxable-income total. Unusual losses, partially exempt categories or a nonpositive denominator need review. [C · S82, S138, S139]

One married spouse meeting an ordinary test can suffice; it is not normally a household 90% ratio. But a spouse with **no Luxembourg-source income** cannot confer eligibility merely by having foreign income below EUR 13,000. A foreign-working spouse does not automatically defeat the other spouse's eligibility. [C · S82, S138]

For the **90% test only**, up to **50 working days** of qualifying treaty-exempt salary can be treated as Luxembourg-taxable in the eligibility numerator. It does not increase the actual Luxembourg taxable base, extend the treaty tolerance, or reduce the separate EUR 13,000 amount.
Preserve actual salary allocation and the eligibility adjustment as separate values.
[C · S82, S138]

Earlier employment abroad still enters the whole-year income schedule. Application of the 50-day fiction to a genuinely foreign employer's earlier employment period remains a narrow open question: statutory language is broad, while the circular's explanation concerns Luxembourg-employer salary. Do not silently grant or deny that adjustment by employer address.
Unequal daily pay also makes a days-only percentage unsafe. [C · S82; I · S82] [O]

## Rate, filing and foreign tax paid

Joint assimilation includes both spouses' foreign income in the fictitious worldwide rate base, then applies the relevant rate to Luxembourg-taxable income. Foreign income may raise or lower the rate. Obtain annual probative evidence and compute it under Luxembourg rules. An election can cost more despite unlocking deductions; compare reviewed 2025 calculations before treating it as automatically beneficial. [C · S82, S138] [I for comparison workflow]

The assimilation request entails assessment. An assimilation-based tax-card rate can require year-end assessment even if eligibility later fails or the request is withdrawn. A non-resident with only Luxembourg-source income without withholding must be assessed; ordinary business income is not settled by a salary tax card. [C · S82, S138, S45]

Non-resident salary assessment has its own legal conditions, including the continuous nine-month employment condition in the ACD's ordinary threshold list. Do not apply resident filing thresholds indiscriminately or conclude that every frontier worker must file. An ACD request, elections and other assessment triggers must also be checked. Where assessment does not apply, Form 163 can adjust payroll; it does not replace a required Form 100.
[C · S45]

## Worked examples — invented amounts and dates

- France, full-year full-time salary: 20 full French days plus 15 half French days gives **35 threshold days**, so the 34-day tolerance fails. With 220 equal workdays, actual French salary time is 27.5/220, not 35/220. The eligibility fiction is a separate assimilation computation. [I]
- Germany: 20 home-work days plus 14 paid home on-call days reaches 34; a further 29-minute activity does not cross the counting boundary, while exactly 30 minutes does. Remuneration allocation still follows actual time and the applicable rules. [I]
- Belgium: 25 foreign workdays in a half-year, part-time LU job do not exceed the full 34-day limit, because no proration applies. A reviewed 55% household professional-income share passes the Belgian alternative; exactly 50% fails. [I]
- France: EUR 40,000 from 110 days at an earlier French employer plus EUR 40,000 from 110 LU days gives 50% before any fiction. Even allowing 50 prior foreign days would yield about 72.73%, still below 90%. A spouse with EUR 5,000 only abroad and no LU-source income does not rescue eligibility. The prior-employer legal question cannot alter this example's failure. [I]

# Open points – lu-income-tax-personal

Answer an open point by saying it is not settled, giving the working answer with its label, and naming who can settle it.

## PIT-O-04 · Bridging loans when changing homes
- Question: Is interest on a bridging loan (crédit-relais) fully deductible from 2024, as some secondary sources say?
- Why it matters: Common when selling one home and buying the next.
- Working answer: No law, regulation, circular, form or ACD page names bridging loans; the bank's label does not matter, because interest follows the actual use of the borrowed money. [C · S187 for the use-of-funds rule; I for "no special rule" – a search result] A bridging loan used to pay for the new home is ordinary debt of the new home: its interest is deductible in full before the home is available and – from tax year 2024 – for the year the rental value is fixed and the following year, then within the yearly ceiling; for an existing home the rental value is fixed at the purchase deed. [C · S27, S22 for each rule; I for applying them to a bridging loan] This general two-year rule is what secondary sources call "fully deductible from 2024"; it is not a bridging-loan entitlement. [I · S27] Not settled: while the old home is still owned it keeps a rental value even if empty, and interest linked to a secondary residence is not deductible – which of the two homes counts as secondary during the overlap is stated nowhere. [C · S22, S64 for the two rules; O for the overlap] Collect the old and new property timelines, the use of the proceeds and the debt settlement; do not replace an unresolved amount by zero.
- Who can settle: ACD.
- Status: open

## PIT-O-05 · Tax credit for the self-employed when both spouses run a business
- Question: Spouses taxed jointly who each run their own business: does each get a CII and CO2 credit, or is a second credit limited to a helping spouse (conjoint-aidant)?
- Why it matters: Up to €600 + €216 a year per spouse.
- Working answer: Probably yes – each spouse who runs an own business and is personally affiliated for that profit gets an own CII and CO2 credit, calculated on that spouse's own net profit. The credit goes « à tout contribuable réalisant un bénéfice » who is « affilié personnellement pour ce bénéfice », and the circular looks at the credits « séparément pour chacun des conjoints/partenaires ». [C · S2 art. 152ter(1); S57 for the texts] The helping-spouse sentence reads as the condition for a second credit on **one** business – the spouse without an own profit who is affiliated as helping spouse; art. 129b(2) separates the same two situations (both spouses with own professional income and own affiliation / one with the income and the other a helping spouse). [I · S2 – medium confidence] No text or worked example names two spouses with two separate businesses, and read alone the helping-spouse sentence could be taken to cover every jointly taxed couple – say so, and do not promise the second credit until the ACD confirms. [O · S43, S57] The tax estimate still counts one credit. [B]
- Who can settle: ACD.
- Status: open

## PIT-O-11 · Owners of a Luxembourg business who live abroad
- Question: § 14(3) StAnpG treats the owners and managing staff of a Luxembourg business as having their habitual abode in Luxembourg even when they do not stay there (the Legilux text has a gap « (.....) »). Is it still applied, e.g. to a non-resident sole trader?
- Why it matters: It would make a cross-border owner fully taxable in Luxembourg.
- Working answer: none – do not apply it. [O · S67] The original text was re-opened; the ellipsis does not prove repeal or present application. Even if § 14(3) made such an owner a Luxembourg resident under domestic law, the person would be resident in both countries, and the treaties with France, Belgium and Germany decide such a case first by the permanent home: an owner whose only home is abroad is resident there for the treaty, and Luxembourg taxes only what the treaty gives the source country (for a sole trader, the profit of the Luxembourg permanent establishment). [C · S132, S133, S134 for the order of the treaty tests; I for the consequence] Keep domestic residence, treaty residence and assimilation separate and review the actual case; cross-border-income.md records the confirmed surrounding rules.
- Who can settle: ACD; an adviser.
- Status: open

## PIT-O-14 · Own-home interest allocation outside an equal joint mortgage
- Question: In a jointly assessed household, how are the family interest ceiling and deduction assigned when one spouse alone owns or borrows for the home, or ownership and borrowing shares differ?
- Why it matters: A household ceiling does not by itself determine each person's PDF/XML amount; arbitrary allocation can overclaim or discard an otherwise available deduction.
- Working answer: The regulation raises the home's interest ceiling for the spouse on one condition only – joint taxation – and asks nothing about who owns or borrows: « Le plafond est majoré de son propre montant pour le conjoint et pour chaque enfant. La majoration pour le conjoint n'est accordée que si les conjoints sont imposés collectivement » (RGD of 12 July 1968, art. 4(2)). [C · S27, S64] Form 100 2025 and the ACD page state the increase for the spouse or partner without an ownership condition, and circular 3ter/1 withdraws it under individual taxation only « comme il n'y a pas imposition collective ». [C · S1, S3, S92] So a jointly taxed spouse who alone owns the home and alone borrowed for it uses the whole family ceiling. In a joint return the two columns are added up, so the split between them does not change the tax: enter the interest in the column of the spouse who owns and owes it, half each for an equal joint mortgage. [I · S27, S64, S61] Still open: a loan signed only by the spouse who does not own the home (the ACD's borrower rule is written for individual taxation only), and different availability dates per spouse. [O · S92] Whether the MyGuichet assistant lets one column carry more than half of the household ceiling is a read-back question (lu-annual-return RET-O-01). The generator still supports only the equal joint case and a single taxpayer's wholly owned home. [B] The same owner/borrower guard applies to a foreign principal home; a confirmed foreign loss affects the worldwide rate base and exempt columns, not a direct LU-source deduction. [C · S139, S138]
- Who can settle: ACD; a tax adviser for the particular ownership and borrowing facts.
- Status: open

## PIT-O-15 · Childcare fees paid outside the service month
- Question: Which months receive childcare costs for the monthly flat-abatement limit when a single advance or arrears payment covers several service months?
- Why it matters: Allocating a lump payment entirely to its payment month or spreading it over service months can produce different deductions.
- Working answer: Article 108 applies to articles 127/127bis and fixes the tax **year** by the payment date, with a periodic near-year-boundary exception. [C · S82 arts. 108, 127(7)] For the **monthly** limit the texts speak of « les frais mensuellement exposés », and the 2025 form asks for a monthly amount and a number of months; this points to the months for which the care was provided rather than the month in which a lump sum happened to be paid. [C · S73 §36, S1 for the wording; I for the reading – low to medium confidence] No official text or example deals with one payment covering several months, so where the two readings give different amounts the case goes to review: no automatic service-month spreading, payment-month concentration or annual average. [O · S73] The tool accepts an evidenced monthly payment schedule and stops ambiguous timing cases for review. [B]
- Who can settle: ACD.
- Status: open

## PIT-O-17 · Annual CIM and child-bonus rounding before automation
- Question: For tax year 2025 assessment, which income rounding is used at CIM/child-bonus thresholds, and how is annual CIM rounded when the formula gives fractional cents?
- Why it matters: Applying payroll or tariff rounding to these credits without evidence changes near-threshold expected results.
- Working answer: The annual CIM is worked out on the adjusted taxable income **before** the floor to a multiple of 50 – art. 126(2) makes that floor a step « avant l'application du tarif » only – and the result of the formula is rounded **up** to the cent. [C · S82 art. 126 for the floor; I for the CIM – high confidence] The ACD's assessment example of 2017 does exactly this (income 42.190, « arrondissement au cent supérieur »), the 2025 circular applies the formula to an unrounded 63 024 in its payroll example and says the assessment regularises the CIM on the adjusted taxable income, and the law rounds the employee credit up to the cent. [C · S192 (historical), S80, S82 art. 154quater for the texts] The child-bonus thresholds use the same unrounded adjusted taxable income (« ajusté au sens de l'article 126 »). [I · S82 – medium confidence] How a fractional child bonus is rounded is not established. [O] The tool keeps its guards until the ACD confirms. [B]
- Who can settle: ACD assessment guidance or a written ACD answer, corroborated by an authorised official calculation for boundary inputs.
- Status: open

- Automation review 2026-11-04: 2025 class 1a tariff now independently verified against the official annual formulas and implemented. Annual CIM runs only where raw/floor50 income readings coincide and the result is exact cents; otherwise stops. This restricted support does not settle the annual rounding question or child-bonus calculation. [B/O]

## PIT-O-20 · Negative lower-spouse salary remainder and DS minimum
- Question: Under article 113(2), what household special-expense minimum applies when the lower spouse's salary after costs and related contributions is negative?
- Why it matters: Blind doubling and an invented minimum floor can both change the result.
- Working answer: When the lower earner's salary after earning costs and the related art. 110 contributions is zero or negative, the household minimum is the single flat amount: the second flat falls away completely, and never more than that. Art. 113(1) grants the flat minimum in every case; art. 113(2) starts from « la somme des forfaits qui seraient applicables si les époux n'étaient pas imposables collectivement » and reduces it « dans la mesure où » the lower salary is below the flat – a reduction that can only take back the second flat. [C · S82 art. 113 for the texts; I for the floor – medium confidence, no official example] Invented example: remainder −200 euros → the household minimum is one flat amount, not one flat minus 200. The tool still stops at this boundary. [B]
- Who can settle: ACD guidance or a tax adviser checking the assessment.
- Status: open

- Handoff review2026-11-04: nonnegative remainder now uses actual earning costs, certified exemptions, retained employee occupational contributions and salary-related voluntary payments. Article 129b separately uses only article 110(1)/(2). Negative remainder is still guarded; this implementation does not settle the question. [C · S82 arts.113,129b; I for retained occupational allocation; B for scope]

## PIT-O-21 · CIS base with unelected domestic flat income
- Question: How does ordinary CIS/CO2 reconciliation include article 137(5) gross when mixed ordinary/domestic income exists and no domestic regularisation is requested?
- Why it matters: The credit circular includes flat remuneration; income exclusion alone does not settle credit aggregation.

[C · S57 for the list; I for "whether or not" – high confidence] The request of art. 8 of the 1999 regulation only decides whether that pay enters the taxable income; art. 7 leaves it out of income, special expenses and withholding credit and does not mention the credits. [C · S108; I] One CIS and one CO2 credit cover all salaries of a person; the CCSS pays them only when all the person's salaries are flat-taxed. [C · S82 art. 154quater] In a mixed case the credits are recalculated on the total gross in the assessment or annual adjustment; do not infer a second credit. [I]
- Who can settle: ACD official worked example or written clarification.
- Status: open

## PIT-O-25 · Earlier foreign employer and the assimilation salary fiction
- Question: Does the article 157ter(2) salary fiction cover an earlier employment period physically performed abroad for a genuinely non-Luxembourg employer?
- Why it matters: It can change the ordinary assimilation ratio after a mid-year move between employers.
- Working answer: All earlier annual salary enters the denominator and the rate schedule. [C · S82, S138] The 50-day fiction was written for the non-resident employee of a Luxembourg employer who works some days outside Luxembourg: the law's commentary and the ACD circular both speak of pay from « leur employeur luxembourgeois », and the circular's own example counts a separate German salary in full as foreign income. [C · S138 for the circular; I · S195 for the commentary] So do not apply the fiction to salary from a separate job abroad for a foreign employer; if the 90% test then fails, check the other route (non-Luxembourg net income below the year's limit), to which the fiction never applies. [I – medium confidence] The statute's wording does not name the employer, so a claim on the literal text cannot be ruled out – a point for the ACD or an adviser, not a default. [O]
- Who can settle: ACD published guidance or written answer; a Luxembourg tax adviser applying the precise employment facts.
- Status: open

## PIT-O-27 · Child moderation when the CAE differential is zero
- Question: How is article-122 moderation and annual fiscal-household assignment established when foreign benefits exceed LU entitlement and CAE pays no differential?
- Why it matters: Entitlement, actual payment and foreign tax-child assignment may differ, affecting rebates and deduction multipliers.
- Working answer: If the CAE paid no family allowance and no differential supplement for the child for the whole tax year, the moderation was not already granted: the law gives it on request as a tax rebate – « Si aucune allocation familiale … n'a été attribuée pour un enfant au titre d'une année déterminée », the taxpayer obtains it « sur demande, après la fin de l'année d'imposition … sous forme d'un dégrèvement d'impôt » (art. 122(3); amount in the year file; set off only up to the tax due; request box on page 2 of Form 100). [C · S82 art. 122(3), S1 for the rule; I for applying it to a nil differential – high confidence: the law's commentary ties the deemed grant for non-residents to the payment of a differential, S193] A differential paid for the child for any part of that year – even small, even paid the next year – counts as the moderation already granted for the whole year: no rebate. [C · S88 for "at least one allowance"; I for the differential] In the rebate case the child is assigned by art. 123(3) – the household in which it lives at the start of the year (or at birth, or when Luxembourg liability starts); unmarried parents living together follow the 2016 regulation (the parent who had the moderation the year before, else the parent they designate). [C · S82 art. 123(3); S86] The applicant certifies that no CAE allowance or differential was received for that child for that year; a foreign certificate alone proves nothing – keep the CAE decision showing a nil differential. [C · S194 for the certification; I for the CAE document] Against: a strict reading of « ouvre droit à l'allocation familiale » could treat an entitlement suspended to nil as enough for the deemed grant; no ACD text or judgment names the case. [O] The tools still stop foreign-only and zero-differential cases. [B]
- Who can settle: ACD with CAE entitlement evidence.
- Status: open

- Automation review 2026-11-04: The new deduction multipliers require explicit positive Luxembourg moderation or independently reviewed rebate evidence. CIM requires positive existing LU moderation; foreign-only and zero-differential cases remain stopped. No legal settlement inferred from these guards. [B/O]

## PIT-O-29 · Foreign contribution components in the personal deduction

- Why it matters: French CSG/CRDS, German private/voluntary or professional-pension items and Belgian supplements cannot all be treated as ordinary CCSS contributions.
- Working answer: Art. 110 covers foreign contributions only when they are compulsory contributions to a foreign scheme covered by a bilateral or multilateral social-security instrument (n° 1 employees, n° 2 non-employees), or personal contributions for continued or voluntary insurance with such a foreign statutory scheme (n° 4); contributions tied to exempt income are out. [C · S82 art. 110; lu-social-security S46] French CSG and CRDS: the France–Luxembourg tax convention lists them among the French taxes on income it covers, so for Luxembourg they are most likely a foreign personal tax – not deductible as special expenses (art. 12 n° 3) – rather than a social contribution under art. 110. [C · S132 art. 2, S82 art. 12 for the texts; I for the conclusion – medium confidence, no ACD text or Luxembourg judgment] Private health or incapacity insurance with a licensed insurer (for example a German private health policy) is an insurance premium under art. 111, with its ceiling, not art. 110. [I · S82 art. 111] Other components (German professional pension funds, Belgian supplements) remain unclassified: obtain component certificates and the legal basis; no blanket deduction. [O]

## PIT-O-31 · Main-home refinancing fees and extra borrowing
- Question: Which refinancing fees or early-redemption penalties qualify, and how must additional private borrowing be separated from the original acquisition debt?

[C · S187 for a loan partly used for other purposes; I for a refinancing top-up] Early-repayment penalty: no Luxembourg text found – do not deduct it without the tax office's agreement. [O] One passage of circular 105/8–98/1 paraphrases the regulation as allowing « le cas échéant les frais de financement »; these words are not in the regulation, and the circular's own rule limits the deduction to interest and annuity arrears – read it as the costs before the rental value is fixed. ## PIT-O-34 · Medical cross-payer identity and later reimbursement

- Why it matters: The wrong person or the wrong year can deduct the same cost twice or leave out a refund.
- Working answer: For 2025 the tools handle, in the calculation and the PDF, a minor child's extraordinary medical costs and costs one adult paid for the other in a joint assessment, when documents show them: patient and payer are kept apart, a refund dated in the same year is taken off, and anything pending, expected or contradictory stops the tool. ## PIT-O-35 · Article 95a replacement payments in extra-professional relief
- Question: How do sickness/maternity/accident replacement payments under article 95a affect professional-activity eligibility and the lower-income limiter of article 129b?
- Why it matters: Treating every employee-category payment as professional activity can grant or mis-size the extra-professional allowance.
- Working answer: Sickness, maternity, accident and parental-leave payments that replace salary are salary-category income (art. 95a) and sit inside the net salary income of Form 100, so they count in the net income used for the lower-income limit of art. 129b(3); a spouse who is employed and compulsorily insured in their own right keeps the professional-activity condition in a year in which part of the pay was replaced. [C · S82, S166, S1 for the texts; I for both conclusions – medium confidence; against: newer credit articles name 95 and 95a separately, art. 129b names only 95] A spouse who received only replacement payments during the whole year: not settled. [O] Tool boundary: general 95a eligibility/limiter stays guarded. Permit only the full-cap invariant where both ordinary jobs and personal affiliation are independently evidenced and each conservative ordinary-income bound, excluding every replacement/advance but subtracting all costs, relevant contributions and article 113 minimum, already reaches the cap. Otherwise stop. KnownIMAL and S-embedded CNS advances trigger the review; S alone is not proof of ordinary gross. Unemployment remains distinct under 95(5). [C · S82; S166; S167; S169; I/B for invariant]

## PIT-O-36 · Employer interest support beyond the ordinary annual household
- Question: How should benefit exemptions and deductible interest be allocated across mixed loans and employers when household status or individual assessment changes during the year?
- Why it matters: The cash versus employer-loan reconciliation is established, but an incorrect allocation can exempt or deduct the same benefit twice.
- Working answer: The exemption is one amount per year for the employee – the own-home tranche and, separately, the other-loans tranche (employer-interest-support.md) – not per employer, loan or payslip. [C · S172, S121] The doubled amounts are for the two spouses together and depend only on joint taxation under art. 3 LIR, which is fixed for the whole tax year: a couple who marry during the year are jointly taxed for that year, and spouses not separated on 1 January stay jointly taxed for that year. So the limit is not prorated when the household changes during the year; registered partners get it only if the partnership lasted the whole year. [C · S172, S82 arts 3, 3bis for each text; I for the conclusion] The assessment applies the yearly household limit to the benefits of both spouses and all employers together; then recompute the deductible interest. [C · S172 art. 4; S22] Still open: under individual taxation of spouses (art. 3ter) the doubling condition is not met as written – whether each spouse then has an own basic tranche is not stated; and which benefit is exempt first when one tranche meets several loans or employers. [O] Do not extend the arithmetic to contribution or credit treatment. [B]
- Who can settle: ACD.
- Status: open

## PIT-O-38 · Pay a controlling shareholder-manager has decided but not received

- Why it matters: It decides the year in which the salary is taxed – and, for a manager insured as self-employed, the year it counts for CCSS contributions and the low-income exemption (lu-social-security company-managers.md).

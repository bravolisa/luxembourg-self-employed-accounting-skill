# Complex home ownership and private rental cases

_Figures in this file: 2025–2026; dated 2025 transitions are explicit. Annual figures and the year's official forms control._

## Build one record per property and loan

Record legal owners/shares, matrimonial regime, assessment election, borrowers, actual payer, loan purpose and any mixed use; acquisition, completion, availability, occupation, letting and sale dates; interest reconciled by assistance type (employer-interest-support.md); and principal separately. Acquisition alone cannot establish the ceiling's start date. Before 2023 occupation remains the anchor; the transition for a pre-2023 acquisition/completion first occupied later uses the start of 2023. From 2023 use free availability, not an elective later move-in. [C · S20–S22; B for the record]

The own-home annual ceiling is not prorated for part-year occupation. Before availability, qualifying financing interest and loan-opening costs can be deductible in full; maintenance/repairs of the own home are not. For a dilapidated property the office decides whether it is uninhabitable from plans, permits, invoices and other facts. The period to which interest relates determines its nature; payment determines tax year subject to article 108. [C · S3; S20–S22; S82]

Allocate mixed own-home/rental property costs on an appropriate evidenced key, including interest. Notional own-use treatment cannot exceed the taxpayer's ownership share. A secondary residence does not obtain the own-home interest deduction. Two addresses or two bank certificates do not by themselves prove two main homes, two new unlimited periods, or a special bridging-loan exemption. [C · S21, S22, S3; I for the two-home caution]

## Individual versus joint assessment

Circular 3ter/1 supplies rules for **individual assessment**. Under pure individual assessment, income and corresponding expenses follow the entitled spouse; separate-property co-ownership follows ownership shares, while debt owed by only one spouse has its own attribution rule. There is no spouse increase to the own-home ceiling, and the child-linked increase is shared 50% each under that regime. Apply the complete regime rather than borrowing one favourable sentence. [C · S92]

Under **joint assessment** the ceiling is raised for the spouse on the sole condition of joint taxation, whoever owns the home, so a sole owner and borrower uses the whole family ceiling (home-and-rental.md). [C · S27, S64 for the regulation; I for the conclusion] The tutorial's half-each example covers an ordinary equal joint mortgage. Unequal rights or crossed owner/borrower/payer roles still need review (PIT-O-14); do not import the individual-assessment borrower rule into a joint return. [C · S61; O for the crossed cases] A bridging loan has no rule of its own: classify each debt by the actual use of the money and apply the ordinary rules; only the months in which two homes are owned at once remain open (PIT-O-04). [C · S187; I · S27; O for the overlap]

For self-employed home offices, test the business part by **value**, not automatically floor area: the regulation's one-fifth/absolute-value tests and grandfathering are in lu-income-tax-business/home-office.md. The employee home-office circular cannot settle self-employed treatment. The private-asset office interest/depreciation branch remains IT-O-03; never deduct the same interest both in business profit and the private property section. [C · lu-income-tax-business S56; lu-income-tax-business S57; lu-income-tax-business S2; I for allocation; O for the private-asset branch]

## Private letting: receipts and cost choice

Use Form 190/210; co-owned income also needs the Form 200 route as applicable. Rent ordinarily follows receipt/availability, with the periodic near-year exception. A security deposit is not rent merely when received; its later application can become income. Unreconciled tenant charge advances enter receipts. Only owner-borne expenses are deductible. [C · S14, S93, S107; S82 arts. 98, 108]

Choose actual costs or the flat regime **per building**. The flat requires the whole building to be private assets and at least fifteen completed years at 1 January. It replaces Form 190/210 categories **A–D** (including repairs, depreciation and other operating costs); financing category E and management/property/communal taxes category F remain additional. Leaving the flat regime bars a new election for the following fifteen years. Do not add repairs or depreciation on top of the flat. [C · S14, S93]

Works-fund contributions can be deductible for qualifying private rented property, not the own home merely because its building has a fund. Repairs versus investment depend on nature/substance/improvement; high cost alone is insufficient. The ACD small-works simplification uses the VAT-inclusive threshold in the year file. Large repairs above half the annual rent may be spread equally over two to five years. Vacancy costs need objective future-letting evidence; acceptance for a vacancy under two years is discretionary, not an automatic entitlement. [C · S14, S22, S94]

Depreciation excludes land. The 20% land proxy is acceptable only if consistent with economic reality; inheritance duties are neither acquisition base nor deductible rental expense. [C · S14, S95]

## Depreciation and separate property abatements

Use dates and previous elections to select the regime in home-and-rental.md and the year file. The post-2022 accelerated 4% regime has **two lifetime property slots per person**. A co-owned property consumes one slot for each owner who uses the regime; selling it does not restore a slot. A jointly owned couple cannot automatically treat this as four shared properties. [C · S95]

Article 129e is a later abatement linked to qualifying 4% depreciation. Eligible VEFA under article 129f instead combines ordinary **2% rental depreciation** with a **separate 4% construction abatement** on the eligible base, subject to the annual cap. Do not record 6% as rental depreciation, stack 129e for that property, or consume a 4%-depreciation lifetime slot for it. The qualifying deed window ends 30 June 2025, extended to 30 September only for a qualifying compromise registered by 30 June. Completion-age and first-year proration still matter. [C · S95, S96, S106; S82 arts. 129e–129f]

For qualifying energy renovation use the **2025 6%** rate; the **2026 10%** rate must not be back-applied. Approved social rental exempts **90% of net rental income**, after eligible expenses, rather than 90% of gross rent with all expenses deducted again. [C · S95; S26; S14]

## Selling and moving in 2025

For non-exempt private property, the speculation holding boundary is **two years** for deeds from 1 January–30 June 2025, and also 1 July–30 September when the compromise was registered by 30 June. Outside this transition it is **five years**. Use the updated 2025 law, not the stale ACD summary alone. Non-speculative gains: a quarter of the global rate for sales from 1 January 2024 to 30 June 2025 [C · S185] and, by the 2025 extension, for deeds from 1 July to 30 September 2025 with the compromis registered by 30 June [I · S186]; otherwise half the global rate (home-and-rental.md). [C · S82 arts. 99bis, 99ter, 131]

The main-residence exemption is a separate test. The moving rule depends on sale by the end of the following year plus a specified occupation/family/professional condition; a later sale can face cumulative conditions including no other dwelling freely available. Preserve the timeline and apply S97 to the actual facts. A bank's main-home label is not sufficient. Suspensive deed conditions can also change the legal date. [C · S97, S29]

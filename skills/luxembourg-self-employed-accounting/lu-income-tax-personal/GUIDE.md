## Before answering

1. **Tax year** → [2025](references/figures/2025.md), [2026](references/figures/2026.md); [2027](references/figures/2027.md) and [2028–2052, single tax class](references/figures/2028.md) are bills or laws not yet in force, never for current answers.
2. **Household**: single, married, registered partners (whole year?), widowed, divorced; children in or outside the household; age 64+.
3. **Residence**: resident, non-resident, moved during the year.
4. **Income besides the business**: salary, pension, dividends or interest, rent, a sale of property or shares.
5. The **business profit** itself comes from lu-income-tax-business (Form 110 or 152); do not recompute it here.

## Rules people get wrong most often

- A self-employed person gets a **tax credit for the self-employed** (CII, up to €600, €300 even with a loss, nil from €80,000 of profit) plus a CO2 credit – **only by filing Form 100**. [C · S2, S43, S57]
- The 2026 scale is the **same** as 2025: 0% up to €13,230, top rate 42% from €234,870, plus the 7% (9%) Employment Fund surcharge on the tax. [C · S2, S36, S40]
- **Resident married couples meeting the joint-assessment conditions** are taxed jointly by default, also in the year of marriage; **partners** only on request and only if the partnership lasted the whole year. [C · S2]
- **Moving apart does not end joint taxation; only a court decision does, and only from the following year.** The conditions are tested on 1 January, so the year of the court's permission is the last joint year; then come three years in class 2 assessed alone (one person's ceilings, no extra-professional abatement). The year a spouse dies is also a joint year. [C · S82, S197, S55, S38]
- The extra-professional abatement for couples who both work is not automatically the maximum: each spouse needs qualifying professional income and compulsory social-insurance affiliation, the amount is capped by that spouse's own net income, and the months of tax liability count, not the months worked – establish both before using the full amount. The AC deduction on the tax card is not a second, yearly deduction. [C · S82 art.129b; S115]
- The €480 special-expense minimum is not simply doubled for a second salaried spouse: it is reduced when that spouse's salary after work costs and compulsory contributions is below €480 (art. 113(2)). [C · S82 art.113]
- Tax class 1a because of age or widowhood alone does not give the single-parent tax credit (CIM): it needs a qualifying child in the parent's tax household. [C · S80–S83]
- Insurance premiums and consumer-loan interest share **one** €672 ceiling per household member; mortgage interest is **not** a special expense – it goes on the rental page. [C · S2, S4]
- Private pension savings: €3,200 (2025), **€4,500 from 2026**. [C · S1, S6]
- Mortgage interest on the own home: in full for the year the home becomes available and the next, then €4,000 / €3,000 / €2,000 per household member. Guichet's 2018 page with €2,000 / €1,500 / €1,000 is outdated. [C · S27; S35 outdated]
- The Form 100 PDF does **not** apply the ceilings: type the capped amounts in 1471, 1527/1528 and 1551/1552 and the flat minimum in 1554 yourself. [C · S1]
- Advances are due 10 March, June, September and December; the balance one month after the bulletin; contest a bulletin within **3 months**, and pay meanwhile. [C · S2, S47, S38]
- Non-resident self-employed people pay at least 15% on Luxembourg business profit unless assimilated (90% rule or less than €13,000 foreign income). [I · S2; C · S2 for assimilation]

The finer traps – employer interest support, cross-border workday rules, care costs, co-owners, pension credits, single-premium insurance – are in the topic files above.

## Calculator

`python scripts/tax_estimate.py --year 2025 --taxable 80000 --class 2 --both-professional --extra-professional-amount 4500 --profit 30000 --withheld 5000 --advances 2000` → the year's tax, credits, withholding, advances and the balance, with the working. `--what-if special=9000 --what-if business=2000` adds the saving of each extra deduction (business costs: tax and CCSS). It estimates; the ACD's bulletin decides.
